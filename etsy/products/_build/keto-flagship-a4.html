A Month of Meals, Four Grocery Lists, and a Food List You Can Actually Use
Thirty days of breakfasts, lunches and dinners that use real food, reuse your leftovers, and do not ask you to count a single thing. Print it, stick it on the fridge, and let it do the deciding for a while.

Start Here (Yes, Actually Here)

Five minutes of reading now saves you a month of second-guessing

Welcome. You are holding a month of decisions that are already made. That is the whole point of this guide. Most people do not quit keto because the food is bad. They quit because it is 5:30 on a Wednesday, the fridge is full of ingredients that do not add up to a meal, and the drive-through is right there.

So here is how to use this. Print the four grocery lists and the food list first, because those are the pages you will touch every week. Put the current week's menu somewhere you will see it, which for most of us means the fridge door. Shop once on the weekend. Then, for the next seven days, stop making decisions and just read the row for today.

You do not have to start on a Monday. You do not have to start on day one. If week two looks more like your life, start there.

How much do I eat?

Here is the entire system: a palm-sized piece of protein at each meal, a fist or two of vegetables, and enough fat to make it taste like something you want to eat. Eat until you are comfortably full, then stop. If you are ravenous an hour later, you almost certainly went light on the protein, so put more on the plate next time.

You will not find a calorie number anywhere in this guide, and that is on purpose. One number cannot be right for a 52-year-old on her feet all day and a 68-year-old managing a heart condition at the same time. Your appetite already knows more about you than a printed number does. We would rather teach you to read it.

Do I have to count carbs?

Not to follow this plan. Most keto guides hand you a daily carb number to hit. This one hands you a menu and a food list instead, and if you stick to them, the carbs sort themselves out. If you already count and you like counting, carry on. If counting is the thing that made you quit last time, you are free.

One thing to do before day one

If you take medication for diabetes, blood pressure, or your heart, or if you have a kidney condition, talk to your doctor or pharmacist before you start. Cutting carbs can change how some medicines act in your body, sometimes within days. Your prescriber may want to watch something or adjust something. It is a short, boring conversation and it is the single most useful thing on this page.

  • Print it single-sided if you want to tear out the grocery lists and take them to the store.
  • Set your printer to Fit to Page. The pages are built for US Letter and A4 both.
  • Cross things out. Write in the margins. This is a working document, not a keepsake.
  • Repeat any meal you liked. Nothing in here breaks if you eat the same dinner twice.

EAT: The Yes List

If it is on this page, put it in the cart and stop thinking about it

These are the foods the whole plan is built on. Meat, fish, eggs, real fats, cheese, and anything green that grows above the ground. You will notice it reads like a normal shopping trip from 1965, because it more or less is.

A note on the phrase you will see everywhere: net carbs means total carbohydrate minus fiber, because fiber does not get digested the way starch and sugar do. It is the number most keto plans go by. You do not have to track it to follow this one.

Week One: Keep It Boring On Purpose

Days 1 to 7

Week one is deliberately dull. You are learning a new default, and new defaults need repetition, not variety. There are five ingredients doing most of the work here: eggs, ground beef, a whole chicken, bacon and a bag of greens.

Roast the chicken on day one. It becomes lunch on day two and soup on day four, which is the kind of math that makes a week cheap. Cook double the ground beef on day two and lunch on day three is already done.

Portions, as a reminder: a palm of protein, a fist or two of vegetables, and fat to taste. Eat to comfortable fullness and stop there.

Week Two: The Week Most People Quit

Days 8 to 14

Nobody quits keto on day two. They quit somewhere around day ten, when the novelty has worn off, the scale has stopped doing anything dramatic, and cooking feels like a chore again. That is normal, it is common, and it passes.

So week two leans hard on prep. Bake a tray of egg muffins on Sunday and four breakfasts are handled before the week starts. Everything else is built to make twice what you need.

Nothing here is complicated. If a meal looks fussy, swap it for one from week one that you liked.

Week Three: You Get Your Personality Back

Days 15 to 21

Most people report that the fog lifts somewhere in here. Energy comes back, cooking stops feeling like homework, and you start having opinions about dinner again. Good. Week three has more variety in it, and your first restaurant meal.

The chili on day fifteen is the workhorse. Make a full pot and it covers two lunches without you thinking about it once.

Day eighteen you go out. That is not a lapse in the plan, it is part of the plan. Learning to order confidently is a skill you will use for years, and doing it once while you still have a menu page to lean on makes the tenth time easy.

What happens on day 31

Nothing dramatic. You go back to week one and run it again, swapping in the meals you liked best from weeks two, three and four. That is the whole long-term plan, and it is why the tracker asks you to note how you felt rather than what you weighed.

Four weeks is long enough to learn a rhythm. It is not long enough to be finished. Be kind about that.

The Sunday Hour

Sixty minutes that make Wednesday possible

You do not have to do this. Plenty of people cook every night and do fine. But if you have ever come home tired and eaten cheese standing up at the counter because nothing was ready, this hour is for you.

Everything below overlaps. The oven works while the skillet works while the kettle boils. Set a timer, put something on the radio, and be finished before the hour is.

A real timeline
ClockDo this
0:00Oven to 400F. Chicken thighs or a whole chicken on a sheet pan, salted, straight in.
0:05Big skillet on medium-high. Brown 2 lb of ground beef with salt and pepper only. Season half of it later, when you know what it is becoming.
0:15Eggs on the back burner for hard-boiling, or the egg muffin tin onto the second oven rack.
0:25Wash and thoroughly dry the greens. Chop celery and cucumbers. Into containers they go.
0:35Pull the ground beef and portion it. Shred the chicken once it is cool enough to handle.
0:45Broccoli or Brussels sprouts onto the hot sheet pan, straight into the fat the meat left behind. Back in the oven.
0:55Wipe down. Label everything with the date. Put this week's menu page on the fridge.
  • Label with the date, not the contents. You will recognize chili. You will not remember whether it is from Tuesday.
  • Dry the greens properly. Wet greens turn to slime in three days and dry greens last a week.
  • Cook once, eat twice is the entire philosophy of this plan. Every dinner that makes leftovers is buying you a lunch.
  • If an hour is too much, do twenty minutes: brown the beef, boil the eggs, wash the greens. That alone changes the week.

The First Two Weeks, Honestly

What people report, why it happens, and what we will not tell you to do about it

Some people feel good from day one. Plenty of others feel distinctly off somewhere in the first week. Tired, headachy, foggy, short-tempered, sleeping badly, making more trips to the bathroom than usual. It is common enough that it has a nickname, the keto flu, and it is the single biggest reason people abandon a plan in the first two weeks.

Here is the plain version of why. When you cut carbohydrate, your body works through its stored glycogen, and glycogen holds onto a lot of water. As that water leaves, minerals leave with it. At the same time your body is switching over to running on fat, and that changeover is not instant. None of this is dangerous for most healthy people and most of it settles within a week or two.

What makes the first two weeks easier, for most people, is unremarkable. Eat the meals. Do not skip meals to speed things up, which is the most common mistake people make in week one. Salt your food to taste, the way you always have. Keep broth, meat, eggs, leafy greens and avocado in the rotation, because those are real food and this plan is already full of them. Go easy on hard workouts for the first week. Sleep, if your body will let you.

None of that is a treatment for anything, and it is not meant to be. If you feel genuinely unwell rather than just flat, or anything worries you, that is a conversation for your doctor, not something to work out from a diet guide.

What you will not find in this guide: a mineral protocol

Most keto guides will give you exact amounts of salt, magnesium and potassium to take every day. Same numbers, every reader, no questions asked. We are not going to do that, and we want to be straight with you about why.

The right amount of any of those depends on your kidney function, your blood pressure and your medications. A printed page does not know a single one of those things about you. If you take a blood pressure pill, a water pill, or anything for your heart or kidneys, a mineral instruction from a stranger can genuinely cause harm, and the people most likely to follow it exactly are the people it is most likely to hurt.

So: salt your food to taste, eat the meals in this plan, and if you are feeling unwell, that is a conversation for your doctor or your pharmacist rather than a guess from a download. Your pharmacist is free, is usually standing right there, and will talk to you today.

When to Stop Reading and Get Help

Plus the weight question, answered honestly

Feeling a bit flat in week one is ordinary. The things on this list are not that, and no diet guide should be your first stop for any of them.

Emergency Signs: Stop and Get Help Now

These are not week one wobbles and they are not something to sleep on. If any of them happen, stop and get emergency help straight away. Dial 911 in the US and Canada, or 999 or 112 in the UK and Ireland. Do not wait until morning, do not wait for an office to open, and do not try to work it out with food.

If you are not sure whether it counts, act as though it does. Emergency staff would far rather see you and send you home again.

  • Chest pain or pressure.
  • A heart that races, pounds or flutters and does not settle.
  • Fainting, or feeling like you are about to.
  • Shortness of breath.
  • Confusion, or someone telling you that you are not making sense.
  • Vomiting that will not stop, or any inability to keep fluids down.
  • Any symptom that frightens you, including ones not on this list.

The one conversation worth having first

If you take medication for diabetes, blood pressure or a heart condition, or you have a kidney condition, speak to your prescriber before day one. Cutting carbohydrate can change how some of those medicines act in your body, occasionally within days. Your doctor may want to check something, adjust something, or simply see you sooner. That is a normal appointment, not a difficult one, and it is far better done in advance than worked out on day nine.

About the scale

The first week usually shows a drop that feels wonderful and is largely water. Then the line goes flat, often for a week or more, and that flat stretch is where most people decide the whole thing has failed. It has not. It is the most ordinary shape there is.

Weigh once a week at the same time of day if you weigh at all, or skip it entirely and go by how your clothes fit and how you feel at four in the afternoon. The tracker on this guide's tracker page asks you to record how you felt for exactly that reason. Thirty days is a short window, and mood is a faster signal than mass.

Where This Usually Goes Sideways

Twelve things worth knowing before they happen to you

None of these are moral failings. They are just the potholes on this particular road, and they are in roughly the same place for everybody.

  • Going light on protein all day, then being ravenous at nine at night. A palm-sized portion at every meal fixes most evening raids on the fridge.
  • Treating cheese and nuts as a meal. They are a supporting cast with an excellent agent. Neither one is dinner.
  • Trusting the front of the package. Keto friendly is a marketing phrase, not a rule. Turn it over and read the ingredients.
  • Forgetting the sauce. Barbecue, teriyaki, glazes and most bottled dressings carry added sugar, and they are on food that otherwise looks perfect.
  • Dropping vegetables entirely, then being bored senseless by day nine. Boredom ends more attempts than hunger does.
  • Weighing every morning and letting one number set the tone of the day. Once a week, same time, or not at all.
  • Quitting on day ten or eleven. That is precisely when most people quit and precisely when it usually turns. Knowing that in advance is half the fix.
  • Walking into a restaurant with no plan, so the bread basket makes it for you. Read the menu in the parking lot.
  • Drinking the carbohydrate. Juice, sweet coffee, beer and mixers slip past because they do not feel like eating.
  • Changing five things at once. New food, new gym, new supplement, new sleep schedule. When something works, you will have no idea which thing it was.
  • Doing it in complete secrecy. Tell one person. It roughly doubles the odds you finish the month.
  • Skipping the conversation with your doctor when you take medication. It is the cheapest and fastest step in the entire plan, and the one people put off longest.

Do I have to count carbs?Not to follow this. Stick to the food lists and the menu and it takes care of itself. If you already count and enjoy it, carry on.
