How to Use This Kit

Start here

You changed how you eat, and you have a doctor's appointment coming up. Maybe you're a little nervous about that conversation. That's normal, and it's exactly why this kit exists.

Here's the truth I've seen over and over: doctors respond well to organized patients. When you walk in with your medications listed, your changes summarized on one page, and your questions written down, the whole visit changes. You're not defending a diet. You're two people looking at the same information together.

This kit isn't a tracker and there's nothing to fill in daily. You'll use it once per appointment, in three stages:

Before

Work through pages 3 to 9. Give yourself 20 to 30 minutes a few days ahead, not the night before. Fill in your medication list straight from your pill bottles, complete your one-page diet summary, and pick the questions you actually want answered.

During

Hand your doctor page 6 (your one-page summary) at the start. It takes them 30 seconds to read and it sets the tone. Use page 8 for your questions and page 10 to write down what they say. You won't remember it all later. Nobody does.

After

Keep page 11 somewhere handy. Questions always come up two days after the appointment. Write them down there so they're ready for next time.

One important thing before you start. This kit helps you organize information and ask better questions. It never replaces your doctor's judgment, and nothing in it is medical advice. You'll see that reminder again at the end, because it matters.

Let's get you ready.

Baseline Labs Request Sheet

Before your visit

When you change how you eat, many doctors like to get a starting snapshot, then compare it against later results. That starting snapshot is what people mean by "baseline labs." Trends over time usually tell your doctor more than any single number can. Here's what the commonly discussed panels actually are, in plain language. This is education about what the tests measure, not advice about what you need. Your doctor decides which tests make sense for you, and your doctor interprets the results. Reference ranges also vary from lab to lab, so the printout from one lab won't always match another. That's normal, and it's another reason interpretation belongs to your doctor.

CMP

Comprehensive Metabolic Panel. A standard blood panel that looks at things like blood sugar at the time of the draw, electrolytes such as sodium and potassium, kidney function markers, and liver function markers. A broad snapshot of how several body systems are running.

Lipid panel

Measures fats in your blood: total cholesterol, LDL, HDL, and triglycerides. If you've read about cholesterol and low-carb eating, this is the panel those conversations are about. What your results mean for you is a conversation for your doctor, because context matters a lot here.

A1c

Hemoglobin A1c. An estimate of your average blood sugar over roughly the past three months. Unlike a single glucose reading, it reflects a longer stretch of time.

Thyroid panel

Usually starts with TSH, sometimes with added tests like T4 or T3. The thyroid helps regulate metabolism, and doctors sometimes like a baseline when eating habits change significantly.

12 Questions Worth Asking

Before your visit

Appointments go fast. If you only get time for three questions, you want them to be your three. Read through this list, check the ones that matter to you, then circle or star your top three. There's space at the bottom for your own. These are the questions I've seen open up the best conversations between patients and doctors during a diet change.

1Given my health history and my medications, is there anything you want to watch more closely while I change how I eat?
2Would baseline labs make sense for me? Which ones would you order?
3How soon would you want to recheck labs after I start?
4I've read that some medications may need adjusting when eating habits change. Are any of mine in that category, and how will we handle that together?
5Are there symptoms that should make me call your office right away?
6Should I monitor anything at home, like blood pressure, glucose, or weight? How often?
7How would you like me to share home readings with you: portal, printout, or phone call?
8What results would tell you this way of eating is working for me? What would concern you?
9If my labs change, how will we sort out what's from the diet versus something else?
10Is there anything in my personal or family history that makes this way of eating riskier for me?
11If I have a question between visits, who do I contact and what's the best way to reach them?
12When should I book my next appointment, and what should we plan to review then?

Symptoms & Changes to Mention

Before your visit

Here's something funny about appointments: the moment the doctor asks "any changes since last time?", your mind goes blank. Everyone's does. So write it down now, while you remember. List anything new or different since your last visit, good or bad. Better sleep counts. A weird headache counts. You're not deciding what's important here, you're just reporting. Let your doctor decide what matters, because sometimes the thing you almost didn't mention turns out to be the useful one.

The Fine Print That Isn't Fine Print

Please read

Please read this part. I mean it.

I'm not a doctor. I've researched this deeply and worked with many people, but I'm not your doctor. If you have health conditions, take medications, or need specific guidance, talk to someone who knows your full medical picture. Everything in this kit is educational, built from research and what I've seen work. Your situation might be different.

And one thing I want to say as plainly as I can: if you're taking medications or have been diagnosed with any medical condition, you need individualized medical oversight. Don't make changes without consulting your doctor. This kit exists to make those conversations better, never to replace them.

Nothing in these pages tells you what to take, what to stop, or what your results mean. The worksheets hold your information. The education pages explain what things are. Every decision stays where it belongs: with you and your doctor, together.
