Anti-Inflammatory Food Guide

What to Eat, Limit, and Avoid
Reduce inflammation naturally with the right foods on your plate

Eat Freely

Anti-inflammatory powerhouses
Fatty Fish
Salmon, Sardines, Mackerel, Anchovies, Herring
Meat and Poultry
Grass-Fed Beef, Bison, Lamb, Pasture-Raised Chicken, Turkey, Bone Broth
Eggs and Dairy
Pasture-Raised Eggs, Ghee, Grass-Fed Butter, Full-Fat Yogurt, Kefir
Healthy Fats
Extra Virgin Olive Oil, Avocado Oil, Coconut Oil, Avocados, Olives
Vegetables
Leafy Greens, Broccoli, Cauliflower, Asparagus, Zucchini, Mushrooms, Garlic, Onions
Fruits and Berries
Blueberries, Strawberries, Cherries, Blackberries, Lemons, Limes
Nuts and Seeds
Walnuts, Almonds, Flaxseed, Chia Seeds, Hemp Hearts
Spices
Turmeric, Ginger, Cinnamon, Rosemary, Oregano
Beverages
Water, Green Tea, Bone Broth, Herbal Tea

Limit

Small amounts may be okay
Grains
White Rice, Oats, Quinoa, Sourdough Bread
Dairy
Aged Cheese, Heavy Cream, Cottage Cheese
Nightshades
Tomatoes, Peppers, Eggplant, Potatoes (may trigger inflammation for some)
Legumes
Lentils, Black Beans, Chickpeas (soak before cooking)
Starchy Vegetables
Sweet Potatoes, Beets, Carrots, Winter Squash
Fruits
Bananas, Grapes, Mangoes, Pineapple (higher sugar)
Natural Sweeteners
Raw Honey, Maple Syrup, Stevia (in moderation)
Alcohol
Red Wine (1 glass occasionally)
Coffee
Black Coffee (1-2 cups, no sugar)

Avoid

Known to increase inflammation
Seed Oils
Canola, Soybean, Corn, Sunflower, Safflower, Cottonseed, Vegetable Oil
Refined Sugar
Candy, Soda, Fruit Juice, Ice Cream, Pastries, High-Fructose Corn Syrup
Processed Foods
Fast Food, Chips, Crackers, Frozen Meals, Deli Meats with Nitrates
Refined Grains
White Bread, White Pasta, Flour Tortillas, Cereal, Muffins
Trans Fats
Margarine, Shortening, Hydrogenated Oils, Non-Dairy Creamers
Artificial Additives
MSG, Aspartame, Sucralose, Artificial Colors, Preservatives
Gluten
Wheat, Barley, Rye, Spelt (common trigger)
Excess Alcohol
Beer, Cocktails, Spirits (more than occasional use)
Dairy (for some)
Conventional Milk, Processed Cheese, Flavored Yogurt
Start simple: swap one "Avoid" food for one "Eat Freely" food each week.

Before You Start

A Quick Note About Your Health

Please talk to your doctor before making changes to your diet.

This is especially important if you:

  • Take prescription medications (especially blood thinners, diabetes, or blood pressure medication)
  • Have a diagnosed medical condition
  • Are pregnant or breastfeeding
  • Have a history of disordered eating
  • Are over 65 or have mobility concerns

Some anti-inflammatory foods (like leafy greens high in Vitamin K) can interact with medications. Your doctor can help you make safe choices based on your specific situation.

This guide is for educational purposes only and is not medical advice. It was created to help you have a more informed conversation with your healthcare provider.

Bring This to Your Appointment

Print this food guide and bring it to your next doctor visit. It gives your doctor a clear picture of what you're eating (or planning to eat) so they can give you specific, personalized advice. Most doctors appreciate when patients come prepared.

Your health is personal. This guide is a starting point, not a prescription.

How to Print This Guide

Step-by-step instructions for the best results
1

Open the PDF File

Open the food guide PDF on your computer. It should open in Adobe Reader, Preview (Mac), or your web browser. If it opens in your browser, that works fine for printing.

2

Press Print

On a PC: Press Ctrl + P on your keyboard.
On a Mac: Press Command + P on your keyboard.
This opens the print window.

3

Check Your Settings

Paper size: US Letter (8.5 x 11 inches)
Scale: Set to 100% or "Actual Size" (not "Fit to Page")
Color: Select "Color" for the best result
Orientation: Portrait (tall, not wide)

4

Print Only the Food Chart

In the print window, look for "Pages" and type 1 to print just the food chart. You don't need to print this instructions page every time.

5

Click Print

Hit the Print button. Your food chart will print in about 30 seconds.

Tips for Best Results

  • Use thicker paper (cardstock or matte photo paper) if you plan to hang it on the fridge
  • Laminate it to make it last longer in the kitchen
  • Frame it with a simple $5 frame from the dollar store for a clean look on the wall

No Printer? No Problem.

Take this file on a USB drive to Staples, Office Depot, FedEx Office, or any local print shop. Ask for a color print on cardstock. It usually costs about $1-2 per page. They can laminate it for a few dollars more.
