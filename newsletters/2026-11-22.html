What the Community Is Asking This Week
“I've been eating carnivore for a couple of weeks now and I'm still waking up two or three times a night to pee. Is something wrong with me?”
Nothing's wrong. Many people find that the first week or two brings a noticeable increase in bathroom trips, especially at night. It's your body releasing stored water as glycogen drops. Sarah's piece this week, 'Peeing All Night on Carnivore? Why Week One Does It,' walks through exactly when this tends to settle down.
“I want to tell my friends about carnivore because it's helped me so much, but I don't know how to bring it up without sounding preachy. Anyone else feel this way?”
This one comes up a lot in our community. Many people find that leading with their own results, without prescribing anything, keeps the conversation relaxed and curious rather than defensive. A short, honest 'here's what changed for me' tends to land better than any amount of explaining the science.
“Keto stopped working for me after a couple of years. My weight crept back and my energy tanked. Is carnivore actually different, or will the same thing happen?”
It's a fair question, and the honest answer is that it depends on what caused the stall. Many people find that going full carnivore removes the food variables that quietly slow progress on broader keto, things like hidden carbs or snacking on foods that feel compliant but aren't. Protein satiety tends to be stronger too, which makes sticking to it easier over time.
Try This Week

Pick one cook-once anchor protein this week. Before you go grocery shopping, choose a single large cut, a brisket, a pork shoulder, a whole chicken, that you'll cook in bulk and portion out. Even one anchor protein ready in the fridge removes the 'nothing to eat' moment that derails most people. That's the first move in Marcus's prep plan, and it works on its own even if you don't do the rest yet.
