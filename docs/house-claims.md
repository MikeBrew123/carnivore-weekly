# House Claims — the canonical positions and numbers

itself on core numbers and stances (full findings:
Brew-Vault/04-Systems/Projects/Carnivore-Weekly/reports/content-quality-review-2026-08-24.md).

**Every writer prompt includes this file. New posts must not contradict it.**
If a post needs to take a different position, that is a stance change:
it requires Brew's approval, a dated entry in the changelog below saying what
changed and why, and the post itself must state that our position changed and why.
Changing our mind is fine. Contradicting ourselves silently is not.

Positions below default to the site's post-July-2026 editorial standard
(hedged, sourced, doctor-partnered). CW = carnivoreweekly.com, KD = ketodial.com.

## Electrolytes

- Sodium: 3-5 g/day of sodium (not salt) for most adults; up to 6 g when training
  hard or in heat. Conversion we always state: 1 tsp salt ≈ 2.3 g sodium.
- Magnesium: 300-400 mg magnesium glycinate in the evening. Avoid oxide (poor absorption).
- Potassium: 3,000-4,700 mg/day, food-first (meat covers most of it). We do not
  give bulk KCl dosing instructions; OTC 99 mg capsules and cream of tartar are the
  only supplement routes we describe, with the reminder that mega-dosing potassium
  is dangerous.
- Timing: spread sodium through the day. A small salty drink before bed is a fine
  fix for night cramps and 3am waking; we do not simultaneously warn against
  evening sodium.

## Protein, fat, calories

- Protein target: 0.8-1.0 g per pound of GOAL body weight for most readers.
  Hard training: up to 1.2 g/lb goal weight. The denominator is always goal body
  weight, on both sites. (VERIFY: this must match what the calculator actually
  outputs; if the calculator uses a different formula, reconcile before enforcing.)
- Per-meal distribution: 30-40 g protein per meal beats one giant dose, especially 50+.
- Fat: protein is the anchor, fat is the lever. Satiety baseline ≈ 1:1 fat to
  protein by grams; when cutting, trim ADDED fat first (butter, tallow), never the
  protein. We do not tell general readers to force 70-80% calories from fat.
- Calories: don't count for the first 8 weeks; eat to satiety. If genuinely stalled
  (3-4 weeks, no scale or measurement movement), run a 7-day food audit, then
  recalculate targets at current weight. "Don't count" and "audit when stalled"
  are the same position at different stages; posts must present them that way.
- Liver/organs: 4-8 oz of liver per WEEK is the cap we publish (vitamin A / copper).
  Meal plans must respect it.

## Adaptation and timelines

- Week 1: water/glycogen drop, typically 3-7 lbs (less for smaller/older bodies).
- Roughest patch: days 3-10. Sleep disruption is common in weeks 2-3.
- Most people feel adapted weeks 4-6; full fat-adaptation can take 8-12 weeks.
- Fair trial: judge at 60-90 days, not before.
- Sustainable fat loss after the water week: 0.5-1.5 lb/week; over 50, 0.5-1 lb/week
  is a good pace. We do not promise totals beyond these rates.
- Stall protocol, canonical order: (1) confirm it's a real stall: 3-4 weeks, tape
  measure not just scale; (2) sleep, stress, electrolytes; (3) 7-day food audit
  including added fat and dairy; (4) recalculate targets at current weight;
  (5) then a 2-week dairy elimination or a deliberate maintenance break.
- PSMF: an advanced, short tool. If we cover it at all: 1-2 protein-focused days
  per week or a supervised 1-2 week block. Never 4-8 week runs.

## Medical (the hard rules)

- The doctor is a partner. We never advise ignoring, firing, or distrusting
  doctors as a class, and never celebrate self-discontinuing medication.
- Medication changes (diabetes, blood pressure, thyroid, statins) are the
  prescriber's decision. Our job is what to measure and what to ask.
- LDL/cholesterol stance: honestly unsettled. Context that matters: trig/HDL
  ratio under 2 is favorable; ApoB, CAC, and Lp(a) are the better conversation
  to have WITH a doctor. We never state that high LDL on carnivore is proven
  safe, and never dismiss statins categorically.
- We flag abnormal-low values as worth attention too (an A1c in the low 4s is
  not "normal range").
- Every health post: qualified claims ("many people find...", "worth discussing
  with your doctor"), and the standing disclaimer.

## Food stances

- Dairy: optional, not required. If stalled, a 2-week elimination is a mid-list
  lever (step 5 above), not the first suspect. The commonly cited irritant is
  A1 casein (not A2).
- Fiber: CW: not required on carnivore; we don't fearmonger about it either.
  KD: low-carb vegetables are fine and normal on keto; we don't claim fiber is
  mandatory. These are site-specific stances; don't import one into the other.
- Coffee: fine. Cut after noon if sleep is the complaint.
- Beginner on-ramp: ground beef + eggs is our default first week (cheap, easy).
  Strict beef-only elimination (lion-diet style) is the therapeutic variant for
  symptomatic readers, not the default start. Posts pick ONE lane and say which.
- KD carbs: standard target 20-30 g net. The perimenopause/hormone-sensitive
  variant (50-90 g) exists but must always be framed as a deliberate exception
  to the standard target, never stated as the default.

## Budget baselines

- Realistic solo range: $5-10/day depending on cuts and region; couples ~$7-10
  per person per day. Posts may land anywhere in the range but must not claim a
  new "the" number without acknowledging the range.
- Arithmetic gets checked: calories and $/g protein claims must survive their own
  math (5 lb of 80/20 ≈ 5,700 kcal, not 9,500).

## Writing mechanics and linking

- Reading level: grade 8-10 (Flesch-Kincaid). No em-dashes, no AI-tell words
  (delve, landscape, robust, utilize, leverage, facilitate, crucial, realm,
  tapestry, moreover, furthermore; "protein leverage" as a named scientific
  concept is the one exception). Contractions used naturally.
- Internal links: every post links to at least 1 (ideally 2-3) existing
  same-site posts with descriptive anchors. Writers build on each other's
  published work instead of re-explaining it.
- Cross-site links: when a CW topic is keto-adjacent (macros, ketosis,
  electrolytes, women's keto), link the relevant ketodial.com post; KD links
  back to CW for carnivore-specific depth. This matters beyond UX: KD's
  Google problem is crawl demand, and internal links from the indexed CW
  domain are the documented fix (see weekly-gsc-indexing task, 2026-08-21).
  Natural placement only; never forced.

## Commercial integrity

- We recommend only products we'd endorse consistently. If we've published a
  criticism of a product (e.g. an ingredient concern), a later recommendation
  must address that criticism, not ignore it. Affiliate relationships are
  disclosed where links appear.
