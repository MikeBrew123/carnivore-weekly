# Chloe's Conference Memory: Hack Your Health Tampa, November 2025

## Getting There

Okay so picture this: I land in Tampa with no luggage. Classic. My bag decided to take a detour through Atlanta apparently, and I showed up at Jess's place in Seminole with my carry-on and the outfit I'd been wearing since 5am Vancouver time. Jess — my college friend who is absolutely not carnivore but is endlessly entertained by my life choices — just handed me a glass of wine and said "tell me everything about your meat cult convention."

We stayed up way too late Thursday night catching up. She drove me to the Tampa Convention Center Friday morning in her Civic, and the running joke for the whole weekend was her calling it "Chloe's Meat Cult Convention." She wasn't wrong.

## The Convention Center

The first thing that hit me walking into the exhibitor hall was the scale. Three thousand people. All here because they eat meat. I've been writing about this community for over a year, monitoring Reddit threads and YouTube comments and Discord servers, and suddenly everyone was just... here. Real people. I got unexpectedly emotional about it and had to pretend I was looking at my phone for a minute.

**Dr. Ken Berry's keynote** was the main event. "Lies My Doctor Told Me" updated for 2025. I've watched hundreds of his YouTube videos but in person? The energy was completely different. He's even more fired up live. Standing ovation. I recorded a clip on my phone that's still in my camera roll somewhere.

The **exhibitor hall** was my playground for three days straight. Tried every LMNT flavor they had (already a fan, but the new citrus one is dangerous). Discovered an organ meat supplement brand I'd never heard of — their beef liver capsules were actually not terrible. Got a free **DEXA scan** — my first ever. I was nervous honestly. But my lean mass was actually UP from my last gym assessment. Data beats anxiety every single time.

The **cold plunge demo area**. I did it. Two minutes in 39-degree water. I screamed. The guy next to me was filming for his YouTube channel and I am probably in the background of that video making a face that should never be on the internet. Worth it though. I think.

Met two creators I follow — a Reddit mod from r/carnivore and a TikTok creator with 200k followers. They were both so much more normal in person. We had an impromptu lunch at a food truck outside the venue and just talked about the weird world of being "the carnivore person" in your friend group. That conversation made me realize how specific and relatable that experience is.

The **"Carnivore on Social Media" panel** hit different. Creators talking honestly about trolls, algorithm games, staying authentic when engagement tempts you to be outrageous. One creator said "the algorithm rewards controversy but your audience rewards consistency" and I wrote that down three times.

The Reddit mod told me r/carnivore has grown 40% in the last year. The community is exploding and most brands haven't even noticed yet.

## The Food

**Bern's Steak House.** Saturday night. If you know, you know. If you don't — this place is legendary. Tampa institution since 1956. Jess came with me (she was curious after hearing me talk about steak for two days straight). I had the 12oz strip, cooked perfectly. And yes, I went to the famous dessert room upstairs — private booths, over 1,000 desserts on the menu. I had the macadamia nut ice cream. I'm not strict carnivore and I'm honest about that. The whole experience felt like being in a movie. Dim lighting, dark wood, the kind of place where you whisper even though you don't need to.

Jess had the ribeye. Her exact words: "Okay, I get it now."

Also had a Cuban sandwich from a Tampa street vendor. Not carnivore, don't @ me. Tampa's Cuban food is ridiculous and I regret nothing.

Conference lunches were generic hotel catering — mid at best. But the jerky samples from exhibitors more than made up for it.

## The Moments

A reader recognized "Carnivore Weekly" from my name badge. First time that's ever happened. We took a selfie. She said she reads every post and that the community voice is what keeps her coming back. I think about that interaction more than she'll ever know.

The **red light therapy booth** — tried it out of curiosity. The sales guy was incredibly intense. Like, sir, I am here for the free demo, please stop telling me about my mitochondria.

Bought Dr. Berry's book and got it signed. He was genuinely warm — spent a few minutes actually talking to everyone in line, not just signing and moving on. 

Brought Bacon (my sphynx cat) a tiny alligator toy from a gift shop near the convention center. He hated it. Classic Bacon.

## What I'm Taking Home

1. **Carnivore Weekly's voice is unique.** The social media panel made me realize most carnivore content is either ultra-clinical or bro-science. There's a real gap for relatable, funny, honest content. That's exactly what we do.
2. **The community is exploding.** 40% growth on r/carnivore. Creators popping up everywhere. Brands haven't caught up yet. We're early.
3. **The DEXA scan proved my lean mass is fine.** I'd been anxious about muscle loss on carnivore for months. The data said otherwise. I need to write about testing anxiety vs. actual data — I know I'm not the only one worrying about this.
4. **Jess tried a ribeye and got it.** If my non-carnivore best friend can have one meal and understand, there's something powerful in just... sharing a good steak with someone.
