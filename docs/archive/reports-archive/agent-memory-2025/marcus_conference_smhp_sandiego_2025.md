# Marcus's Conference Memory: SMHP San Diego, August 2025

## The Drive

Drove down from LA after wrapping up with a coaching client. Took PCH through Laguna Beach because I'm not an animal who takes the 5 when the ocean is right there. What should've been 2 hours turned into 3.5 with traffic backed up through Dana Point. Used the time to listen to a podcast episode with Dr. Gabrielle Lyon on muscle-centric medicine. Turned out to be the best prep I could've done.

Pulled into the Loews Coronado Bay Resort around 7pm Wednesday. Splurged on this one — conference hotel, zero commute friction, room on the bay side. View of the water from the 8th floor. Worth every dollar.

Hit the hotel gym Thursday at 5:30am before anything started. Two other early risers. Nobody talked. Perfect.

## The Talks

**Dr. Stephen Phinney** was the reason I came. The man literally wrote "The Art and Science of Low Carbohydrate Performance." His presentation on fat-adapted athletes was the most data-dense talk I've attended in years. The key insight: the fat-adaptation performance curve isn't linear. There's a 6-8 week valley where athletes feel terrible and their numbers drop. Then it comes back — and it comes back stronger. VO2max data on fat-adapted endurance athletes matching or exceeding their carb-fueled baselines. This explains why every athlete I've coached who quit carnivore did it in weeks 3-5. They quit in the valley. I've been telling people "trust the process" but now I have Phinney's data to show them exactly why.

**Dave Feldman** — the citizen scientist running lipid experiments on himself. Lean Mass Hyper-Responder research. I walked in skeptical of the "high LDL on carnivore is fine" narrative. I walked out with a more nuanced view. Feldman's data on metabolically healthy athletes with elevated LDL but perfect triglycerides, perfect HDL, zero calcification — it's hard to argue with when you see the numbers. I have athletes right now with "scary" cholesterol panels who are in the best shape of their lives. Now I have research to point to instead of just saying "your doctor is looking at the wrong numbers."

**Dr. Dominic D'Agostino** — exogenous ketones and performance, with a focus on military and special operations applications. Navy SEAL protocols. The tactical, data-driven approach is exactly how I think about coaching. Took notes on ketone dosing timing relative to training — pre-competition window is narrower than I assumed.

**Dr. Bret Scher** — cardiologist who actually understands low-carb. His talk on reframing cardiac risk assessment for metabolically healthy athletes filled a gap I didn't know I had. Most cardiologists see an athlete's lipid panel and panic. Scher showed when to actually worry and when to look deeper.

**Craig Emmerich** — protein optimization and body composition. His protein leverage hypothesis data aligned with what I've seen coaching: when protein goes up, everything else falls into place. Validated my 1g per pound minimum recommendation.

## The Food

**Stake Chophouse & Bar** on Coronado, Thursday night. Walked there from the hotel. American Wagyu ribeye. Went with a strength coach from Denver named Ryan and a sports dietitian who works with triathletes. We talked shop for three hours. The sports dietitian's approach to periodizing protein intake around competition cycles was something I'd never considered — higher protein in the 48 hours pre-event, slightly lower fat to speed gastric emptying. Simple. Effective. Stealing it.

**Island Prime** Saturday night — waterfront steakhouse literally built on stilts over the water on Harbor Island. Dry-aged NY strip. The sunset over San Diego harbor was the most peaceful moment of the whole trip. Sometimes you just need to eat a good steak and watch the water.

Hotel breakfast buffet every morning. Omelette station, bacon, sausage. No decisions to make. I like that.

Grabbed fish tacos from a stand near the Hotel del Coronado between sessions on Friday. Not strict protocol but I was walking and it was there. San Diego does that to you.

## The Real Value

Thursday networking dinner at the resort. Sat next to a retired NFL offensive lineman — 6'5", 290, the kind of person who fills a chair. He'd reversed his type 2 diabetes on carnivore after retirement. We talked for an hour about the stigma athletes face when they go against conventional sports nutrition. His team nutritionist had told him carnivore would "destroy his career." He's healthier now at 45 than he was at 25. That conversation is worth more than any lecture.

Friday morning, hotel gym, 5:30am. Guy on the next squat rack recognized my Carnivore Weekly shirt. CrossFit gym owner from Portland. Uses our macro calculator with his athletes. First time a stranger gave me real-world feedback on something we built. We exchanged numbers. He's been sending me data from his members since.

Morning runs on Coronado Beach. 5:30am, empty sand, Pacific Ocean. August in San Diego is 65 degrees at dawn with a breeze off the water. I solved half my coaching problems during those runs. The body thinks better when it's moving.

## What I'm Taking Home

1. **The fat-adaptation valley is real and measurable.** Phinney gave me the data to show athletes exactly what happens in weeks 3-8 and why they need to push through. This is an article waiting to be written.
2. **Lean mass hyper-responders aren't broken.** Feldman's research gives me something concrete for athletes freaking out about cholesterol panels. Context matters more than numbers.
3. **There's an underserved audience of athletes who want to try carnivore but their trainers won't support it.** The NFL guy's story. The CrossFit owner. The triathlete dietitian who's questioning everything she was taught. These people need someone speaking their language. That's what I do.

Drove home Sunday morning. PCH again. Windows down. Coronado Beach at dawn was already fading into the rearview but I can still hear the waves if I think about it.
