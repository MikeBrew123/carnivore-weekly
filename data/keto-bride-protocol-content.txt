SECTION 1: WELCOME LETTER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You're planning one of the biggest days of your life, and somehow you're also supposed to look and feel your absolute best while choosing centerpieces, managing family opinions, and attending every tasting imaginable. That's a lot.

I wrote this guide because I've watched so many women white-knuckle their way through crash diets before their wedding — and then feel exhausted, bloated, or just plain disappointed on the day itself. There's a better way.

Keto isn't a punishment. It's not carnivore-strict (you'll still get avocado, leafy greens, and a glass of dry wine at your rehearsal dinner). It's not calorie obsession either. It's a metabolic shift — one that burns fat steadily, clears up your skin, kills the afternoon energy crashes, and cuts the bloating that makes every dress fitting feel like a verdict.

Ninety days is enough time to see real, lasting change. Not a crash. Not a stunt. Just a way of eating that works with your hormones instead of against them.

You've got this. Let's get started.


SECTION 2: WHY KETO FOR BRIDES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Here's what I've seen work over and over: the women who feel best on their wedding day aren't the ones who starved themselves. They're the ones who fixed their blood sugar.

When you eat a standard Western diet — bread, pasta, sugar, processed snacks — your blood glucose spikes and crashes multiple times a day. Those crashes are why you feel foggy at 3 PM, why you're irritable at venue appointments, why you wake up puffy. Keto eliminates that pattern entirely. Once your body adapts to burning fat for fuel, your energy becomes remarkably stable.

For brides specifically, that stability matters more than you might think. Wedding planning is cognitively demanding. You need to remember vendor contracts, navigate family dynamics, make dozens of decisions a week. Chronic blood sugar swings make all of that harder.

Beyond energy, keto delivers some specific benefits that are worth knowing about.

Fat loss on keto tends to spare muscle mass because you're eating plenty of protein and your body has a reliable fuel source in dietary fat. You lose the soft, fluffy weight — the water retention, the inflammation, the stored glycogen — while keeping the muscle tone that makes a dress look the way it should.

Skin clarity is real and it happens faster than most people expect. Sugar drives sebum production and systemic inflammation. Cut the sugar and refined carbs, and a lot of people see clearer skin within four to six weeks. Collagen production also improves when you're not constantly spiking insulin.

Bloating drops significantly on keto. Most chronic bloating comes from fermentable carbohydrates — grains, beans, high-sugar fruits — and when those go away, so does a lot of the puffiness.

And here's something the carnivore-only crowd won't tell you: keto is socially manageable. You can eat at almost any restaurant. You can go to your bridal shower brunch and find something on the menu. You can have a small amount of berries or a piece of dark chocolate without derailing everything. That flexibility matters when you're trying to hold this together for 90 days.

I'm not a doctor, and this guide isn't medical advice. If you have any health conditions or take medications, talk to your healthcare provider before changing the way you eat. What I'm sharing is based on research and what I've seen work — your situation might be different.


SECTION 3: THE 90-DAY COUNTDOWN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PHASE 1: DAYS 90-61 — THE RESET

This is the hardest phase, and it's also the most important. You're breaking a lifetime of carbohydrate dependence, and your body is going to notice.

Start by removing the obvious: sugar, grains, processed carbs, and starchy vegetables. That means bread, pasta, rice, oats, potatoes, most fruit, and anything with added sugar. Don't try to taper. Cut it.

Your macros during this phase: roughly 70-75% of calories from fat, 20-25% from protein, and 5% from carbohydrates. In practical terms, that means keeping net carbs under 20 grams per day. Net carbs are total carbohydrates minus fiber — so a cup of spinach has almost zero net carbs even though it technically has carbohydrates.

What you're eating: fatty cuts of meat (ribeye, lamb, pork belly, skin-on chicken thighs), eggs cooked in butter, avocado, full-fat dairy (heavy cream, hard cheeses, Greek yogurt in small amounts), olive oil, leafy greens, and low-carb vegetables like zucchini, asparagus, and cucumber.

Days 3 through 7 are often called the "keto flu" window. You might feel tired, headachy, irritable, or foggy. This isn't your body rejecting keto — it's your body adjusting to a new fuel source while also dumping water weight and electrolytes at a rapid rate. The fix is simple and it works: sodium, potassium, and magnesium.

Add salt to your food liberally. Drink broth. Eat avocado (high in potassium). Take a magnesium supplement at night. Most keto flu is actually electrolyte deficiency, and once you address it, the symptoms clear fast.

The scale will drop quickly in week one — sometimes 5 to 8 pounds. This is mostly water weight and glycogen stores, not fat. Don't get too excited, and don't get discouraged when it slows down. Week one is a water flush. Weeks 3 through 12 are where the real body composition changes happen.


PHASE 2: DAYS 60-31 — FAT ADAPTATION

Something shifts around weeks 4 to 6. Your energy stabilizes. Your hunger decreases. You stop thinking about food constantly. This is fat adaptation — your body has built the metabolic machinery to run efficiently on fat, and it's doing its job.

This is also when body composition really starts to change. You're burning stored fat between meals. You're holding muscle because protein intake is adequate. Your clothes start fitting differently even if the scale moves slowly.

Protein targets during this phase: aim for 0.8 to 1 gram per pound of lean body mass. If you weigh 150 pounds and estimate 30 pounds of body fat, your lean mass is roughly 120 pounds — so you want 96 to 120 grams of protein per day. This keeps muscle synthesis running without tipping you out of ketosis.

You can add more variety now. Salmon and sardines bring omega-3s that support skin health. Lamb is rich in zinc. Hard cheeses like cheddar, parmesan, and gruyere fit well within keto macros. A small handful of berries (about a quarter cup) adds antioxidants without blowing your carb budget.

Track by how your clothes fit, not just the scale. Body recomposition means you might be losing fat and building a little muscle simultaneously — the scale might not move for a week even while you're losing inches. Take measurements. Take photos. The dress fitting is your real benchmark.

Sleep matters more than most people realize at this stage. Cortisol — the stress hormone — directly inhibits fat loss and can cause you to hold water. Wedding planning is stressful by nature, so you need to be intentional about recovery. Aim for 7 to 9 hours. Keep a wind-down routine. Magnesium glycinate at night helps significantly.

For meal prep during busy weeks: batch-cook proteins on Sunday. Hardboil a dozen eggs. Keep avocados and good quality cheese on hand. A lunch of tuna salad made with mayo over arugula takes four minutes. You don't need to be precious about it.


PHASE 3: DAYS 30-15 — POLISH

The dress fitting is coming. This is the refinement phase.

If you've hit a plateau, tighten carbs to under 15 grams net per day. Sometimes the body needs a little metabolic push after weeks of adaptation. Cutting a few more carbs often breaks a stall without changing anything else.

If you're experiencing persistent bloating, try dropping dairy for two weeks. Dairy is a common individual sensitivity — some people do beautifully with it on keto, and others find that removing it eliminates the last layer of puffiness they're carrying. Hard cheeses tend to be better tolerated than soft cheeses or cream.

Start collagen peptides now if you haven't already. Collagen supports skin elasticity, hair strength, and nail health — but it takes 8 weeks or more to see measurable effects. If you're 30 days out, start immediately. Add one to two scoops to your morning coffee or a smoothie made with full-fat coconut milk.

Alcohol should be limited or eliminated. If you're going to drink, stick to dry wine (a standard pour has about 3-4g net carbs) or straight spirits like vodka, tequila, or whiskey with sparkling water. Beer and cocktails made with mixers are essentially carb bombs. Alcohol also slows fat metabolism for 24 to 48 hours — your liver prioritizes clearing it over everything else.

How you feel getting dressed in the morning, how your energy holds through long planning days, how clear your skin looks in your bathroom mirror — that's what this phase is about. The data is in the results you're already seeing.


PHASE 4: DAYS 14-1 — LOCK IT IN

You're almost there. This is not the time to experiment.

Don't introduce any new foods or supplements. You don't know how you'll react to something new, and you don't want to find out in week two before your wedding.

Keep carbs strictly under 20 grams net. No "just this once" meals. Not because one meal would ruin everything — it wouldn't — but because keto works best as a consistent metabolic state, and you want to be at your best.

Hydration becomes more important now. Aim for pale yellow urine as your benchmark — not clear, not dark yellow. Electrolytes remain essential. Don't stop salting your food.

Sleep is non-negotiable at this stage. Seven to nine hours. If anxiety is keeping you up, magnesium glycinate in the evening helps, as does keeping your phone out of the bedroom. You've done the work. Your job now is to recover and show up rested.

Mentally, your work is done. The physical changes that are going to happen have already been set in motion. Trust your body. The two weeks before the wedding are for logistics, not last-minute transformations.


WEDDING WEEK: DAY-BY-DAY

Days 7-3: Maintain your normal keto meals. Drink plenty of water with electrolytes. Don't reduce calories. Don't try to look "drier" by cutting water — that's not how it works, and dehydration will make you feel terrible. Sleep is your priority.

Day 2: Keep meals familiar and simple. Scrambled eggs and bacon for breakfast. A salad with grilled protein for lunch. Salmon or steak for dinner. Nothing exotic. Nothing that might cause GI distress.

Day 1 (rehearsal dinner): Eat before if there's any chance the menu is carb-heavy. Have protein and fat before you arrive. At the rehearsal dinner, prioritize the protein on the menu and eat bread rolls last if at all. A glass of dry wine is fine. Drink water between glasses. Get to sleep at a reasonable hour.

Morning of the wedding: Eat within an hour of waking. Eggs are ideal — easy on the stomach, protein-rich, familiar. Avoid heavy fat sources that sit in your stomach (no bulletproof coffee if you're not used to it). If your ceremony is later in the day, eat a real meal a few hours before. Low blood sugar during vows is a real thing and it's preventable.

The reception: You're going to cut the cake. You're going to have a bite. That is fine. Truly. One slice of wedding cake after 90 days of disciplined eating will not undo a thing. Enjoy it. What matters is that you're going into that moment feeling energized, clear-headed, and comfortable in your dress — and after 90 days of keto, you will be.


SECTION 4: MACROS BY GOAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Keto has one non-negotiable: keep net carbs under 20-25 grams per day. Everything else — the fat-to-protein ratio — can be adjusted based on what you're trying to accomplish.

Net carbs are total carbohydrates minus fiber. A food's fiber content doesn't spike blood sugar, so it doesn't count toward your daily carb budget. An avocado has about 12 grams of total carbs but 10 grams of fiber — so it's just 2 grams net carbs. Leafy greens are similar. This is why keto works alongside real vegetables.

Fat Loss Focus (70% fat / 25% protein / 5% carbs): This is the classic ketogenic ratio. Most of your calories come from fat — butter, olive oil, fatty meats, avocado — which keeps you satiated without spiking insulin. Protein is adequate but not excessive. This is the approach for brides with a meaningful amount of fat to lose.

Maintenance and Recomp (65% fat / 30% protein / 5% carbs): If you're already close to your goal weight but want to improve body composition — more muscle, less soft tissue — bump protein slightly. This supports muscle retention while still maintaining ketosis for most people.

Active and Training (60% fat / 35% protein / 5% carbs): If you're lifting weights, doing regular intense cardio, or training for something, protein needs go up. Higher muscle mass requires more protein for maintenance and repair. Keep carbs the same — you don't need them for performance as much as the fitness industry has told you.

A practical note: most apps that track macros calculate net carbs automatically. Cronometer and Carb Manager are both accurate tools. You don't need to track obsessively forever, but tracking for the first 4 to 6 weeks helps you understand where carbs are hiding in your diet.


SECTION 5: SUPPLEMENT STACK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The supplements that actually matter on keto are the ones that replace what you're losing.

Electrolytes are the most important thing you can take. When you cut carbs, your kidneys excrete more sodium, which pulls potassium and magnesium with it. This is why keto flu happens, and it's why electrolyte deficiency causes muscle cramps, headaches, and fatigue that people mistakenly blame on keto itself. Target 3 to 5 grams of sodium per day (from food and supplementation), 2 to 3 grams of potassium, and 300 to 400 milligrams of magnesium. Salt your food generously, drink broth, and use an electrolyte supplement without sugar (LMNT and Redmond Re-Lyte are both reliable).

Collagen peptides are genuinely worth it for brides. Type 1 and Type 3 collagen support skin firmness, hair strength, and nail health. The catch is that they take time — 8 to 12 weeks of consistent use before you see visible effects. Start as early as possible. One to two scoops per day mixed into coffee or any warm liquid is the standard dose.

Magnesium glycinate at night is worth its own mention. It's the most bioavailable form, it doesn't cause the GI distress that magnesium oxide can, and it supports deep sleep and muscle recovery. 200 to 400 mg before bed.

MCT oil is optional but useful during the adaptation phase. Medium-chain triglycerides convert to ketones quickly and can ease the transition for people who struggle with the first two weeks. Add a tablespoon to coffee or use MCT oil in salad dressings. Don't go overboard early — it can cause GI issues until your body adjusts.

What to skip: most fat burner supplements are marketing, not science. Raspberry ketones, garcinia cambogia, "thermogenics" — these don't do what the labels claim. Your actual fat-burning system is already running if you're in ketosis. Don't waste money on products that claim to enhance something your body is already doing naturally.


SECTION 6: NAVIGATING SOCIAL SITUATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Keto doesn't have to be invisible, but it also doesn't need to be a declaration. Here's how to handle the situations that actually come up.

Wine and cake tastings: These are part of planning, and you don't have to refuse all of them. At a wine tasting, a few small sips won't derail your ketosis. At a cake tasting, you can have a small bite of a couple of options. The goal is information, not indulgence. If you're worried, eat a keto-friendly meal beforehand so you're not hungry when you arrive.

Bridal shower brunches: Most brunch menus have eggs, smoked salmon, bacon, avocado, or some combination of the above. Eat those. If the spread is overwhelmingly carb-heavy, eat beforehand and graze on whatever protein is available. Nobody is monitoring your plate as closely as you think they are.

Bachelorette weekends: These require more strategy. Eating out is actually easier on keto than most people expect — at almost any restaurant you can order protein and vegetables. The harder part is the drinking. Spirits are your lowest-carb option (tequila, vodka, whiskey with sparkling water or soda). A couple of glasses of dry wine works too. What doesn't work: margaritas made with mix, frozen drinks, craft beers. If people pressure you to drink more than you want, soda water with lime looks identical to a vodka soda. Nobody needs to know.

Pushy future in-laws: This is the social situation people rarely talk about but everyone experiences. You don't owe anyone an explanation of how you eat. "I'm eating pretty simply right now" is a complete sentence. "I'm trying to feel good before the wedding" usually gets a sympathetic nod from anyone who's ever been a bride. If someone persists, redirect. "The roast looks amazing — is that your recipe?"

The wedding reception itself: You've done 90 days of work. The reception is not the moment to be rigid. Eat the protein at the dinner service, enjoy the celebration, have your slice of cake, dance. One evening of relaxed eating after months of discipline doesn't matter metabolically. What matters is that you feel good, you're present, and you're not spending your wedding night calculating net carbs.

The underlying principle: grace over guilt, every time. Keto is a tool, not a moral position. You're using it because it works, not because eating a bread roll makes you a bad person. Handle slip-ups by returning to your next meal, not by treating a bite of toast as a reason to eat carbs for the rest of the week.


SECTION 7: TROUBLESHOOTING FAQ
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

"I'm losing weight but my energy is terrible. Is that normal?"

Yes, especially in weeks one through three. This is the adaptation window, and it's uncomfortable because your body is learning to run on a different fuel. The most common culprit isn't the diet itself — it's electrolyte loss. Add more sodium (salt your food aggressively), increase potassium (avocado, leafy greens), and make sure you're taking magnesium. If your electrolytes are solid and you're still dragging after week three, look at whether you're eating enough total calories. Keto suppresses appetite, and some people accidentally under-eat, which makes everything feel terrible. Eat until satisfied.

"I cheated with cake at my bridal shower. What now?"

Eat your next meal the way you normally would. That's it. One serving of cake isn't going to undo weeks of metabolic adaptation. You'll likely kick yourself out of ketosis temporarily and feel it — bloating, maybe some brain fog, a water weight spike on the scale the next morning. All of that passes within 24 to 48 hours. What you don't want to do is use one slip as permission to eat off-plan for several days because "I already blew it." That's the pattern that derails people, not the cake itself.

"Everyone keeps saying keto is bad for your heart. Should I be worried?"

This is a reasonable question and deserves a real answer. The research on keto and cardiovascular risk is more nuanced than headlines suggest. Most studies show that keto improves triglycerides and HDL cholesterol — two of the more predictive markers for heart health — and reduces fasting insulin, which is closely linked to metabolic disease. LDL cholesterol does rise in some people on keto, but the type of LDL particle matters, and that story is complicated. If you have a personal or family history of cardiovascular disease, you should have a conversation with your doctor before starting. I'm not a doctor, and I'd never tell you to ignore legitimate medical history. But the blanket "keto is bad for your heart" claim isn't supported by the current research.

"My hair is shedding more than usual. Is keto causing this?"

Possibly, and it's more common than people talk about. There's a condition called telogen effluvium — basically stress-related hair shedding that happens 2 to 3 months after a significant physical change. The shift to keto can trigger it, especially if you also dropped calories significantly. The good news: it's temporary, and it stops. Make sure you're eating enough protein (hair is made of it), getting adequate biotin from eggs and meat, and not running a large calorie deficit. Collagen peptides can also help support hair during this phase. The shedding typically resolves on its own within a few months.

"I only have 60 days, not 90. Is it too late?"

Absolutely not. The most dramatic changes on keto happen in the first 30 to 45 days — the water weight loss, the initial fat loss, the skin clearing, the energy stabilization. Sixty days gives you enough time to go through full adaptation and into the fat-burning phase. Start Phase 1 now, run Phase 2 for about three weeks instead of four, then move into Phase 3 and 4 as written. You won't have quite as much time in the polishing phase, but you'll still see meaningful results. Start today, not next Monday.

"How do I handle the wedding cake without completely falling apart?"

Have a bite. Have a slice if you want one. Your wedding cake is not a threat to the work you've done — it's a tradition that marks one of the most significant moments of your life. Ninety days of eating well does not obligate you to refuse food at your own wedding. The goal of this whole protocol is to feel good, look good, and be present on your wedding day. Part of being present is actually enjoying the celebration instead of standing next to your cake thinking about macros. Eat the cake. Dance. That's what the 90 days was for.
