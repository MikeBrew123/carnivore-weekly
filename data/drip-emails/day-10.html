30-Day Carnivore Starter Plan | Day 10 of 30

Week 2 is where people quit. Let's make sure you don't.

Hey,

I need to tell you something important, because you're in the exact window where it matters most. The number one reason people quit carnivore in week 2 isn't willpower. It isn't cravings. It's electrolytes.

Here's what's happening. When your insulin drops (which is the whole point), your kidneys stop holding onto sodium. They flush it out, and water goes with it. Sodium then drags potassium and magnesium balance along for the ride. The result: headaches, leg cramps at 2 AM, dizziness when you stand up, heart flutters, and a bone-deep tiredness that makes you think the diet stopped working.

The diet didn't stop working. You're just low on minerals. And the fix costs almost nothing.

Your daily electrolyte targets:

  • Sodium: about 2 teaspoons of salt per day, total, spread across your meals. That sounds like a lot. It's what your body actually needs right now. Add more on days you sweat.
  • Potassium: meat covers most of it, which is why eating enough matters. If cramps show up anyway, grab a salt substitute like Lite Salt or No Salt (it's potassium chloride) and stir 1/4 teaspoon into a glass of water once or twice a day.
  • Magnesium: this one's hard to get from food alone. Around 300 to 400 mg of magnesium glycinate in the evening works well for most people. Skip magnesium oxide. It's the cheap one, and it mostly just runs through you.

My DIY mineral drink (costs pennies):

A big glass of water, 1/2 teaspoon of salt, 1/4 teaspoon of Lite Salt, and a squeeze of lemon if plain salt water makes you gag. Drink one in the morning and one mid-afternoon. That's it. Most week 2 misery disappears within a day or two of doing this consistently.

If mixing your own isn't your thing, some people prefer a pre-made packet like LMNT for the convenience. It's salty enough to actually work (most electrolyte drinks aren't), and it's handy for travel or keeping in a desk drawer. But honestly, the DIY version above does the same job for a fraction of the cost. Either way works. Just pick one and do it daily.

One important note. If you take blood pressure medication, have kidney issues, or manage any diagnosed condition, talk to your doctor before changing your sodium or potassium intake. Electrolytes interact with medications, and your doctor knows your full picture. I don't.

Here's my challenge for you this week: hit those salt targets every single day for the next 5 days, and notice how you feel by the weekend. Most people are shocked at the difference. The wall you're hitting isn't a wall. It's a saltshaker away from gone.
