Your First Weeks on Carnivore

Week 2 is where people quit. Let's make sure you don't.

Hey,

I need to tell you something important, because you're in the exact window where it matters most. The number one reason people quit carnivore in week 2 isn't willpower. It isn't cravings. It's electrolytes.

Here's what's happening. When your insulin drops (which is the whole point), your kidneys stop holding onto sodium. They flush it out, and water goes with it. Sodium then drags potassium and magnesium balance along for the ride. The result: headaches, leg cramps at 2 AM, dizziness when you stand up, heart flutters, and a bone-deep tiredness that makes you think the diet stopped working.

Often, the diet didn't stop working. The minerals just ran low, and that is usually straightforward to put right, and cheap. Here is what is worth understanding about it.

What actually helps, in order:

  • Salt your food, then salt it again. On the meat, at every meal, until it tastes right to you. Not measured into a glass. Most people coming off carbs find their taste for salt climbs for a few weeks and then settles down on its own. Let it. If your food tastes flat, that is information.
  • Eat full meals. This is the part people skip in week 2, and it is the one that does the most. Beef, lamb and pork are already rich in potassium, so a proper meal does more for you here than anything you can stir into water. Half a meal because you are not that hungry is one of the most common reasons this week feels like a wall.
  • Drink to thirst, not to a number. Pouring plain water on top of falling sodium is how people make this worse rather than better. Water when you are thirsty, salted food alongside it.
  • Magnesium is the honest exception. It is the one that is genuinely hard to get from meat alone. It is also the one where the right amount depends on you and on what else you take, so it is a good question for your pharmacist, who can look at your actual list in about two minutes. I am not going to hand you a number for it in an email.

What I reach for on a rough afternoon:

A mug of hot broth, salted the way you would salt soup. It is warm, it is savoury, it goes down when nothing else appeals, and it gets the job done through food instead of through a measuring spoon. Beef or chicken, homemade or a decent boxed one. Whistler winters made me a broth person, and week 2 is exactly when it earns its keep.

Some people would rather not think about any of this and just tear open a packet. Pre-mixed electrolyte drinks like LMNT exist for that, and they are genuinely handy for travel or a desk drawer. Worth knowing that each packet is built around a set amount of sodium and potassium, which suits a lot of people and does not suit everyone, so read the label and read the next paragraph before you make one a daily habit.

Now the part I would want a friend to tell me. Potassium is the one that catches people out. If you take a daily pill for blood pressure or for your heart, a water pill, or anything to do with your kidneys, talk to your doctor or pharmacist before you add potassium from a supplement, a salt substitute, or a daily electrolyte packet. Some of those medicines already hold potassium in, so extra on top is the problem rather than the help. Same conversation if a blood test has ever come back with a note about your potassium or your kidneys. And if you are reading this thinking you are not sure whether any of it applies to you, that is a reason to ask, not a reason to assume you are fine. Salting your food to taste is a smaller and different question, and for most people it is not the one at issue here.

So here is the week. Salt every meal properly, eat full meals, and do not let the water run ahead of the salt. Give it five days and notice how you feel by the weekend. Most people are surprised by how quickly it turns. If it does not turn, or the flutters and the dizziness are still there once you are eating and salting properly, stop tinkering and get it looked at. That is not caution for the sake of it. Those symptoms have other causes that are worth ruling out, and week 2 of a new diet is a convenient thing to blame them on.
