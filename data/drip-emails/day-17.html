Your First Weeks on Carnivore

The hard moments are usually the moments the food isn't ready.

Hey,

When I ask readers what the hardest part is, two of the answers that keep coming back are "I'm bored of my meals" and "the cravings." Then people tell me when it hits, and the same few moments show up again and again. Mid-afternoon. The first half hour after you get home from work. Travel days. Saturdays.

Here's what those moments have in common. You're hungry, and nothing is ready. Dinner is forty minutes away, or you're in an airport, or the day has no shape to it. Hungry plus nothing ready loses to whatever is ready, almost every time. That isn't a character flaw. It's just how tired, hungry people work.

On day 14 I talked about getting the plan out of your phone and onto the fridge. Today is the next step. A plan on the fridge doesn't help much if the food behind it still needs cooking.

Give one fridge shelf a single job:

Food you can eat within two minutes, cold or quickly warmed. A good one has some of these:

  • Hard-boiled eggs, already peeled. Peeling is the step that stops people.
  • Cooked burger patties, a few to a container.
  • Sliced leftover roast, or a tub of pulled pork.
  • Chicken thighs or drumsticks, cooked and cold.
  • A jar of broth to heat in a mug.

The habit that keeps it full is simple: cook double at dinner. Half goes on your plate, half goes on the shelf. No separate cooking day needed.

How the shelf covers the four tricky moments

Mid-afternoon. If the craving shows up around three, the fix usually starts at lunch. A bigger lunch, with enough fat and properly salted, often means the afternoon never gets loud. On a day when lunch was small, eat something off the shelf before the usual time, not after the craving has already started talking.

Walking in the door. This is the one people describe most. You're home, you're tired, dinner isn't started, and you're standing in the kitchen eating whatever's handy. So eat first, cook second. Two eggs or a patty off the shelf, standing at the counter, then make dinner. Or start dinner in the morning in a slow cooker, so it's done when you get home.

Travel days. Don't count on finding something on the road. Pack the shelf into a small cooler bag: eggs, patties, sliced meat. Here in Whistler, a ski day with no food packed is how people end up hungry and grumpy at the bottom of the mountain. Hotel breakfasts nearly always have eggs and bacon. And eat a proper meal before you leave the house, so you start the day fed.

Weekends. Weekdays have a shape. Saturdays drift, and a drifting day is easy to graze through. Pick your two meal times for the weekend now, and eat the first one properly before errands, not after.

None of this is about finding something to stand in for the old sweet thing. It's about being fed before the old habit gets loud. Protein, fat, salt, and a little time still do the work. The shelf just makes sure they're there when you need them.

And the boredom

Here's something I've noticed. A lot of the boredom lives in the leftovers, not the dinners. Dinner gets your attention. The ready food tends to be the same cold patty, day after day.

Same food, different meal:

  • Change the temperature. Cold sliced roast is one meal. The same slices seared in a hot pan until the edges crisp is another.
  • Change the texture. Pulled pork pressed flat in a hot pan until it browns tastes like a different dish.
  • Warm it in broth. Sliced meat dropped into a mug of hot salted broth turns leftovers into something like soup.
  • Finish it differently. A pat of butter melting on top, a spoon of bacon fat, or a coarser salt sprinkled at the end.

One honest thing. Sometimes "I'm bored" shows up at three in the afternoon and only something sweet sounds good. That's usually the craving in a different coat. The answer is the same one: a real meal, eaten now.

And it's okay for some meals to be plain. Food doesn't have to entertain you every time. Save the effort for a couple of dinners a week, and let the shelf be easy.
