The plan you don't have to think about

Marcus here. Three weeks in. Today I want to talk about the thing that actually decides whether you're still doing this in six months.

It's not knowledge. You know what to eat by now. It's not willpower either. The real enemy at week three is decision fatigue. Most people who quit around now don't quit because keto stopped working. They quit because they got tired of thinking about it. Every meal a fresh decision, every grocery run a research project.

The fix is to stop optimizing and start defaulting. Here's what I mean.

The fridge meal plan

Your defaults

  • One default breakfast. Same thing every weekday. Eggs however you like them.
  • One default lunch. The boxed lunch from your prep session. Zero thinking required.
  • Three or four dinners on rotation. That's your whole menu, and that's plenty.
  • One standing grocery list. Restock the same items every week, done.
  • One emergency meal always in the fridge for the nights cooking isn't happening.

This sounds boring until you remember you already ate the same handful of meals before keto. Almost everyone does. Novelty is for restaurants. Defaults are for results.

Stop optimizing

Week three is when people start fiddling. New supplements, complicated recipes, tighter macros, a different eating window. Resist it. There's a whole debate about lazy keto versus strict keto, and my take for your first couple of months is simple: consistent beats perfect. A plan you repeat without thinking will beat a perfect plan you abandon by Friday.

When boredom does show up, change one thing at a time. Swap a single dinner in the rotation and leave the rest alone. The recipe library has 70+ keto recipes with the macros already worked out, so a swap doesn't turn into homework.

One exception to the no-optimizing rule. If you've lost weight since day one, your numbers have moved. Rerun the free calculator with your current weight. Takes about 3 minutes, and it keeps your defaults honest.

Here's the shift I want you to notice. You're not running a 30-day diet anymore. You're building the version of keto that hums along quietly in the background a year from now. Defaults are how you get there.
