The hard part isn't the food, it's the people

Sarah's handing me the mic for a day. I'm Chloe, I run the community side of KetoDial, which mostly means I read thousands of comments from people doing exactly what you're doing right now.

And there's one thing that comes up constantly around the first weekend: other people. Your kitchen is handled by now. Then a restaurant happens. Or a family dinner. And suddenly the hard part isn't the food, it's the people around the food.

Restaurants are easier than they look

Almost every menu has a keto meal hiding on it. You just have to order like you mean it.

The restaurant playbook

  • Pick the protein first. Steak, burger, grilled chicken, salmon. Something's always there.
  • Swap the starch. "Can I get vegetables or a side salad instead of the fries?" Servers hear this fifty times a night. Nobody blinks.
  • Ask for sauces and dressings on the side. That's where the sugar hides.
  • Ask them not to bring the bread basket. Way easier than staring at it for an hour.

Notice what's not in that list: the word keto. You don't have to announce anything. "Burger, no bun, side salad" is a complete sentence.

Family dinners

Family is trickier, because family takes your plate personally. Grandma's potatoes aren't just potatoes, they're love. So here's what works: fill your plate with the things you can eat, compliment the cook loudly, and keep the conversation about anything except your diet.

If you're worried there won't be anything for you, bring something. A meat and cheese board disappears at any gathering, and it guarantees you a meal.

The "just one bite" people

Every gathering has one. They usually mean well. But explaining keto to someone holding a slice of pie never goes the way you hope. The more you explain, the more it sounds like an invitation to debate.

So don't explain. Use these instead.

Scripts that end it kindly

  • "I'm good, thanks. It looks amazing though."
  • "Not tonight, but save me the recipe."
  • "Honestly, I'm full." Then change the subject. Ask about their kids. Works every time.
  • If they push: "I appreciate it, I'm all set." Same warm tone, same answer, as many times as it takes.

You're not being rude. You're allowed to just not eat something. Most people drop it after one polite no, and the repeat offenders drop it after two. I wrote more about handling social pressure on keto if this is your sticking point, including the coworker who keeps leaving donuts within arm's reach.

One more thing. The awkwardness fades. The first restaurant order feels weird. The fifth one is automatic. And once the people around you see this isn't a phase, most of them quietly stop commenting.
