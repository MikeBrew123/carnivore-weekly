The email that saves most people's keto

Quick question. How are you feeling today?

If the answer is "honestly, a little rough," you're right on schedule. Somewhere between day 2 and day 4, most new keto folks get hit with headaches, fatigue, brain fog, irritability, and cravings that seem to come from nowhere. It has a name, keto flu, and it's the single biggest reason people quit.

Day 3 is the classic quit point. People feel awful, decide keto "doesn't work for their body," and go back to carbs. They feel better within hours and take that as proof. It isn't proof. It's minerals.

What's actually happening

When you cut carbs, your insulin drops. When insulin drops, your kidneys start flushing sodium at a much faster rate. That's the water weight coming off this week, and the sodium leaves with it. As sodium falls, your body loses potassium and magnesium too. Within two or three days, you can be low on all three.

Every classic keto flu symptom traces back to that depletion. Not weakness. Not "toxins leaving your body." Minerals. And salting your steak alone won't make up the difference.

The fix, in three numbers

  • Sodium: 4,000 to 7,000 mg per day. A half teaspoon of salt is about 1,150 mg. Add a quarter teaspoon to your water bottle twice a day, and salt your food generously on top of that.
  • Potassium: 2,600 mg per day for women, 3,400 mg for men. Those are the 2019 National Academies Adequate Intake figures, not ceilings. Food first: avocados, spinach, salmon, mushrooms. A quarter teaspoon of Lite Salt adds about 350 mg if you need help getting there.
  • Magnesium: 300 to 500 mg per day. Look for glycinate or citrate, not oxide. Take it in the evening.

That's the whole routine. Salted water twice a day, generous salt at meals, potassium-rich food on the plate, magnesium with dinner. Boring, cheap, and it works.

One important caution. If you take blood pressure medication, or any prescription, talk to your doctor before adding this much sodium. Keto itself often lowers blood pressure, and your medication may need adjusting. Your doctor needs to know what you're doing.

I wrote up the full explanation, including which magnesium types to buy and which to skip, in the complete electrolyte guide. Bookmark it. You'll want it again around day 3.

And if the slump hits tomorrow anyway? Don't decide anything big on a rough day. Drink the salted water, eat a real meal, go to bed early. Rough days pass. Quitting on one is the only mistake that sticks.
