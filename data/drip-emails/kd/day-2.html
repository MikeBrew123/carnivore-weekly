The email that saves most people's keto

Quick question. How are you feeling today?

If the answer is "honestly, a little rough," you're right on schedule. Somewhere between day 2 and day 4, most new keto folks get hit with headaches, fatigue, brain fog, irritability, and cravings that seem to come from nowhere. It has a name, keto flu, and it's the single biggest reason people quit.

Day 3 is the classic quit point. People feel awful, decide keto "doesn't work for their body," and go back to carbs. They feel better within hours and take that as proof. It isn't proof. It's minerals.

What's actually happening

When you cut carbs, your insulin drops. When insulin drops, your kidneys start flushing sodium at a much faster rate. That's the water weight coming off this week, and the sodium leaves with it. As sodium falls, potassium and magnesium tend to follow. Within two or three days, a lot of people are running low on all three.

Every classic keto flu symptom traces back to that depletion. Not weakness. Not "toxins leaving your body." Minerals. Which is good news, because minerals are the most fixable thing on this list, and mostly you fix them with food.

What actually helps, in order

  • Salt your food, and then salt it a bit more. On the eggs, on the steak, in the vegetables, at every meal, until it tastes right to you. Your taste for salt tends to climb for a few weeks after you cut carbs and then settle down on its own. If your food has started tasting flat, that is information, not a craving to resist.
  • Put potassium on the plate. Avocado, spinach, salmon, mushrooms, plain unsweetened yogurt if you eat dairy. Real meals do the heavy lifting here, and this is the one place where "eat the food" genuinely beats anything you can stir into a glass.
  • Eat full meals, not half ones. This is the step people skip in week one, and it is the one that does the most. Half a dinner because you are not that hungry yet is one of the most common reasons day 3 feels like a wall.
  • Magnesium is the honest exception. It is the hardest of the three to get from food, so it is the one people most often ask me about. It is also the one where the right amount depends on you and on everything else you take, so it is a good two-minute question for your pharmacist, who can look at your actual list. I am not going to put a number for it in an email that goes to thousands of people.
  • Drink when you are thirsty. Plain water on top of falling sodium is how people accidentally make this week worse. Water alongside salted food, not instead of it.

That's the whole routine. Salt at every meal, potassium-rich food on the plate, full meals rather than half ones, water when you're thirsty. Boring, cheap, and it's most of what people are missing when this week goes badly.

Now the part I'd want a friend to tell me, because it's the one people don't see coming. Potassium is the one that catches people out. If you take a daily pill for your blood pressure or your heart, a water pill, or anything to do with your kidneys, check with your doctor or your pharmacist before you add potassium from a supplement, a salt substitute like Lite Salt or No Salt, or a daily electrolyte packet. Some of those medicines already hold potassium in, so extra on top is the problem rather than the help. Same conversation if a blood test has ever come back with a note about your potassium or your kidneys. And if you're reading this thinking you're honestly not sure whether any of that is you, that's a reason to ask, not a reason to assume you're fine. Salting your food to taste is a smaller and different question, and for most people it isn't the one at issue here. One more thing while we're here: keto often lowers blood pressure on its own, so if you take medication for it, your doctor should know you've started.

If you want the longer version of why any of this happens, I wrote it all out in the complete electrolyte guide. Bookmark it. You'll want it again around day 3.

And if the slump hits tomorrow anyway? Don't decide anything big on a rough day. Salt your food properly, eat a real meal, go to bed early. Rough days pass. Quitting on one is the only mistake that sticks.
