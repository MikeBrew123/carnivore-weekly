Stop being scared of the thing that's supposed to feed you

Let me guess what's on your plate this week. Chicken breast. Some greens. Maybe eggs. No bread, no pasta, no sugar. You're proud of it, and you should be.

But you're also tired, a little cold, and thinking about food all day.

If that sounds familiar, you've found the number one beginner mistake. It isn't carbs sneaking back in. It's fear of fat.

Why this happens to almost everyone

Most of us spent decades hearing that fat clogs arteries and makes you fat. That messaging doesn't vanish because you decided to try keto. So people cut carbs, then quietly keep fat low too, because eating butter still feels like breaking a rule.

Here's the problem. On keto, fat is the fuel. You took away sugar, which was your old fuel. If you hold back on fat as well, your body has neither. That's not discipline, that's running on fumes. And it usually comes with a second mistake stacked on top: eating too little food overall.

Signs you're under-eating right now: you're dragging even though your electrolytes are dialed in, your hands are cold, you can't stop thinking about food, and you're snappier than usual. That's not keto failing. That's a body asking to be fed.

Easy ways to actually eat enough fat

  • Cook in butter or olive oil instead of dry-pan everything
  • Buy the fattier cut: chicken thighs over breast, 80/20 beef over extra-lean
  • Add half an avocado to lunch
  • Salmon counts twice: protein, fat, and potassium in one
  • Eat until you're satisfied, not until some tiny calorie number says stop

"But I'm trying to LOSE fat"

I hear this one every week, and I get it. Eating fat to lose fat feels backwards. But here's what I've seen over and over: the people who eat enough are the ones still here in month three. When you feed yourself properly, hunger settles, cravings quiet down, and staying on the plan stops taking heroic effort.

Under-eating feels productive for about a week. Then it collapses, usually face-first into whatever's in the pantry at 9pm. If your evenings are where the wheels come off, that's almost always a daytime eating problem, and I broke down exactly how to fix it in this post on night cravings.

So here's your day 4 assignment, and it's a nice one. At your next meal, add the butter. Pick the fattier cut. Eat until you're actually done. Then notice how the rest of your day feels.
