The honest timeline nobody gives you

By day 5, everyone asks me the same question. When do I start feeling good?

It's a fair question, and you deserve a straight answer, not a highlight reel. So here's the pattern I've seen most often, with the honest caveat up front: your body is running its own schedule, and it didn't read anyone's timeline.

What tends to improve, and when

  • Days 1 to 3: The scale drops fast, but it's mostly water. This is also peak keto flu territory, which is why we handled electrolytes on day 2.
  • Days 4 to 7: Appetite starts to settle. You get your first stretches of the day without cravings. Energy is still up and down, and that's normal.
  • Week 2: Energy steadies out. The afternoon crash softens or disappears. Cravings get noticeably quieter.
  • Weeks 3 to 4: This is where the good stuff concentrates. Clearer head, hunger that feels genuinely different, and for a lot of people, better sleep.

Now the honest part

Some people feel great by day 4. Others need three weeks or more before things click. Your age, your medications, how many carbs you were eating before, and how well you're sleeping all move the timeline. Neither the fast version nor the slow version predicts how well keto ends up working for you.

Two things worth knowing so they don't scare you. First, sleep sometimes gets worse before it gets better in the first week or two. Annoying, usually temporary. Second, if you're several weeks in and still exhausted, that's not "just how keto is." It's nearly always something fixable, and I wrote a checklist for exactly that situation: still exhausted after adaptation.

If you want the longer view, month by month instead of week by week, I laid the whole thing out in the keto results timeline. It's a good one to send to the skeptic in your life, too.

Here's the real point of today's email. Feeling "meh" on day 5 is not a sign that keto is failing you. It's a sign you're in the middle of the switch, which is exactly where day 5 people are supposed to be. The worst move right now is grading a 30-day change on a 5-day report card.

You're closer to the good part than you think.
