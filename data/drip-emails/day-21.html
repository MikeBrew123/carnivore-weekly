Let's talk about the meat bill.

Hey,

Three weeks in, and if you're like most people, you've had this thought at the checkout: "Can I actually afford to eat this way long term?"

Fair question. Here's the first thing to notice, though. Add up what you're NOT buying anymore. Snacks, cereal, drinks, takeout, the random $6 impulse stuff. Most people find their total food spending stays flat or drops, even though the meat line item went up. But you can still shrink that meat bill a lot. Here's how.

Five ways to cut your meat costs:

  • Buy the cheap cuts. Chuck roast, brisket, pork shoulder. They're half the price of ribeye, and a slow cooker turns them into something better. Fat content is just as good.
  • Ground beef in bulk. The big 3 to 5 lb family packs are almost always cheaper per pound. 80/20 is still your workhorse.
  • Hunt the markdowns. Meat close to its sell-by date gets marked down 30 to 50%. Ask your store what time they sticker it, show up then, and buy everything that looks good. It goes straight in the freezer, so the date doesn't matter.
  • Get a freezer. A small chest freezer runs about $150 to $200 and pays for itself in a few months, because now every sale is an opportunity.
  • Buy direct from a farm. A quarter or half cow from a local rancher often lands between $5 and $8 per pound. That price covers everything, steaks included. Search "[your town] cow share" or ask at a farmers market.

Now, the honest trade-off: every one of those tactics costs time. Driving around, timing markdowns, finding a rancher, waiting on a cow share. If you've got more time than money, do those first. They're the best deals going.
