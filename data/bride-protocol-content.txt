SECTION 2: WHY CARNIVORE FOR BRIDES
---

There are a lot of diets out there. Keto, low-carb, calorie counting, intermittent fasting. So why carnivore, and why for this specific window of your life?

The short answer is this: carnivore removes everything your body has to fight against and replaces it with everything it actually needs.

Body composition changes fast on carnivore. When you cut carbohydrates completely and center your eating around animal protein and fat, your insulin levels drop dramatically. Low insulin is the signal your body needs to start releasing stored fat. At the same time, the high protein intake tells your body to hold onto muscle. The result is fat loss with muscle preservation, which is the combination that changes how you look in a dress far more than the number on a scale.

Skin is one of the most visible changes people notice. Processed foods, seed oils, and sugar are all inflammatory. When you eliminate them, the inflammation that shows up as puffiness, redness, clogged pores, and dull tone often clears significantly. Women regularly report that their skin looks better between weeks six and ten than it has in years. Collagen production improves when you're eating the amino acids that support it, and animal foods are the richest source of those building blocks.

Bloating and puffiness are dress-fitting enemies. Most bloating comes from carbohydrates, particularly grains, legumes, and certain vegetables. On carnivore, that source of bloating is gone. Many women find their waist measurement drops noticeably just from reduced inflammation and water retention, independent of fat loss.

Energy is stable all day. There are no blood sugar spikes and crashes. You don't hit a wall at 3pm. On your wedding day, when you're up early and going until midnight, this matters more than people realize.

Mental clarity follows. Wedding planning is cognitively demanding. When your brain is running on stable fuel, decisions feel clearer and anxiety often decreases.

I want to be direct here: results vary. Your starting point, your hormones, your sleep, your stress levels, and how consistently you follow the protocol all affect your outcome. I'm not a doctor, and I'm not your doctor. If you have health conditions or take medications, please involve your healthcare provider in any significant dietary change. What I'm sharing is based on research and what I've seen work, not a personalized medical plan.

SECTION 3: THE 90-DAY COUNTDOWN
---

PHASE 1: DAYS 90-61
Foundation (The Adaptation Phase)

This phase has one job: get you fat-adapted. Everything else comes after.

Fat adaptation means your body has made the metabolic switch from burning glucose as its primary fuel to burning fat. Most people have never experienced this because they've been eating carbohydrates their whole lives. Making the switch takes time, and it doesn't always feel good at first. That's normal and it passes.

What to eat during Phase 1: beef, salt, and water. Keep it that simple. Ribeyes, ground beef, chuck roast, brisket. Fatty cuts. Salt your food generously. Drink water consistently. This isn't deprivation, it's elimination of variables so your body can adapt cleanly.

What to expect: many people experience what's called keto flu in the first one to two weeks. Headaches, fatigue, brain fog, muscle cramps, irritability, and cravings. This happens because your body is excreting stored glycogen along with large amounts of sodium, potassium, and magnesium. It's not dangerous. It's a transition.

The electrolyte protocol for Phase 1 is essential. Get 2,000-3,000mg of sodium daily (salt your food liberally and add a pinch to your water), 1,000-3,500mg of potassium (cream of tartar in water works, or NoSalt), and 300-400mg of magnesium glycinate before bed. These three minerals are the difference between feeling terrible for two weeks and feeling rough for three or four days.

The scale will do confusing things in Phase 1. When you cut carbohydrates, your body releases stored glycogen, and glycogen holds water. You may lose four to eight pounds in the first week. Then the scale might go up slightly as your body adjusts. Don't chase that number. It's water, not fat. Your clothes will tell you more truth than the scale during this phase.

The goal of Phase 1 is simple: survive adaptation and emerge fat-adapted. You're building the foundation that makes everything in Phase 2 actually work.


PHASE 2: DAYS 60-31
Optimization (The Body Recomposition Phase)

You're fat-adapted now. Your energy is more stable. The cravings have quieted. This is when the real work happens.

Phase 2 is about dialing in your protein intake and starting to expand your food variety strategically. Your protein target is approximately one gram per pound of bodyweight per day. If you weigh 150 pounds, you're aiming for 150 grams of protein daily. This is your anchor. Everything else works around it.

Now you can introduce more variety. Lamb chops, pork belly, chicken thighs, whole eggs, butter, bone broth, bacon. Fatty fish like salmon if you enjoy it. These foods add nutritional richness and keep the protocol sustainable. You're not restricted to just beef, though beef remains the foundation.

During Phase 2, pay more attention to how your clothes fit than to the scale. Body recomposition means fat loss and muscle maintenance happening simultaneously. The scale might not move much even as your body is changing shape. A pair of jeans that fits differently tells you more than a pound of weight loss or gain.

Skin changes often become visible around weeks six to eight. The puffiness reduces. The tone improves. People start commenting. This is the payoff of Phase 1's discipline showing up on your face.

Sleep and stress are not optional factors during this phase. Cortisol, the stress hormone, directly competes with fat loss. High cortisol tells your body to hold onto fat, especially around the midsection. Eight hours of sleep is not a luxury right now. It's part of the protocol. If wedding planning is stressing you out (and it probably is), find one thing you can do each day to actively manage that stress. A walk, a bath, ten minutes with no phone. It matters more than any supplement.


PHASE 3: DAYS 30-15
Refinement (The Polish Phase)

You're in the home stretch and your body has changed. Now you refine.

If you've been including dairy (butter, heavy cream, hard cheese), this is the time to evaluate it honestly. Some people tolerate dairy well. Others retain water from it, experience mild bloating, or notice it stalls their progress slightly. If your dress fitting is coming up and you want to look as tight as possible, eliminating dairy for two to three weeks before that fitting is worth trying.

Meal timing matters more now. Two meals per day with no snacking works well for most women in this phase. It keeps insulin low between meals and gives your digestion a proper rest. You don't have to count calories. Just eat two satisfying, protein-rich meals and stop there.

Start collagen supplementation if you haven't already. Collagen peptides (hydrolyzed) support skin elasticity, hair thickness, and nail strength. The visible results take eight to twelve weeks, so if you're in Phase 3, you're at the edge of the window for noticeable skin changes. Start now and be consistent. Add it to coffee, water, or bone broth.

Alcohol comes off the table completely during this phase. Alcohol impairs sleep quality, raises cortisol, stalls fat loss, and causes facial puffiness. This isn't forever. It's thirty days. Your photos will thank you.

The dress fitting mindset: when you walk in for your fitting during this phase, go in focusing on how you feel, not a number on a scale or a dress size. How do you feel in your body? How do you move? That's the metric that actually matters.


PHASE 4: DAYS 14-1
The Final Stretch

Two weeks out. Don't start anything new.

This is not the time to experiment with a new supplement, try a fasting protocol you read about online, or make dramatic changes to your eating. Your body has been adapting for nearly three months. Trust the work you've done.

If you want maximum tightness in this final stretch, the simplest approach is to return to strict beef, salt, and water. No dairy, no pork, no eggs. Just beef and salt and water for two weeks. This eliminates any remaining sources of bloating or water retention and puts you at your leanest.

Sleep is non-negotiable during these two weeks. Eight hours minimum. If wedding prep is crowding out your sleep, something else has to give. Sleep is when your body repairs, regulates hormones, and manages cortisol. Skimping on it the week before your wedding will show on your face.

Hydration matters more than ever. Drink your water consistently. Aim for half your bodyweight in ounces per day (if you weigh 150 pounds, drink 75 ounces). Hydrated skin looks plumper, healthier, and more luminous in photos.

Trust the process. You've done the work. These final two weeks are about maintaining, not chasing. Your body is working even when you can't see it.


WEDDING WEEK: 7 DAYS OUT

Seven days out: continue eating as you have been. Beef, salt, water if you're doing the strict final stretch. Two meals per day. No alcohol. Eight hours of sleep.

Five days out: the temptation to restrict dramatically will be high. Resist it. Severe restriction now will leave you tired and depleted on your wedding day. Eat your protein. Eat your fat. You need energy for this week.

Three days out: if you're prone to water retention, this is a good day to increase your salt intake slightly and make sure you're drinking plenty of water. Counter-intuitive, but adequate sodium and hydration helps your body release excess water rather than hold onto it.

The night before: eat a meal you know agrees with you. Ribeye and salt. Ground beef patties. Something simple and familiar. Don't try anything new. Don't eat a huge portion because you're nervous. Eat until you're satisfied and stop.

Morning of: eat something small and protein-rich two to three hours before the ceremony if your schedule allows. A few eggs and some bacon. A small portion of ground beef. Something that will hold you without leaving you feeling heavy. Avoid drinking excessive water right before the ceremony if you're nervous about bloating. Stay consistently hydrated throughout the morning rather than drinking large amounts at once.

The day itself: you've prepared for this. Your energy is stable. Your skin has been glowing for weeks. You didn't starve yourself. You fed yourself well and consistently, and your body shows it.

SECTION 4: PROTEIN TARGETS BY GOAL
---

Protein is the anchor nutrient of this entire protocol. Getting it right makes everything else work better.

Here's how to set your target:

Fat loss focus: 0.8 grams of protein per pound of bodyweight per day. If you weigh 160 pounds, aim for 128 grams of protein. This keeps you in muscle-preserving territory while allowing your body to draw more from fat stores.

Muscle preservation (most brides): 1 gram of protein per pound of bodyweight per day. At 160 pounds, that's 160 grams. This is the standard starting point for most women and works well across the 90 days.

Active or training regularly: 1.1 to 1.2 grams per pound of bodyweight per day. If you're lifting weights, running, or doing significant exercise throughout the protocol, your body needs more protein to recover and preserve muscle.

Why protein is the anchor: protein is satiating, which means you stay full longer and aren't fighting cravings all day. It preserves lean muscle while you're losing fat, which is what keeps you looking toned rather than just thin. And it has a high thermic effect, meaning your body actually burns more calories digesting protein than it does digesting fat or carbohydrates. Hitting your protein target daily is the single most important number to track in this protocol.

Practical note: a pound of ground beef contains roughly 70-80 grams of protein. Three eggs contain about 18 grams. A ribeye steak (twelve ounces cooked) contains roughly 60-70 grams. You can hit your targets without obsessive tracking once you have a feel for the protein density of what you're eating regularly.

SECTION 5: THE SUPPLEMENT STACK
---

The rule here is food first, supplements second. Carnivore is nutritionally complete in ways most diets aren't. You don't need a cabinet full of pills. But a few targeted supplements make a real difference during this protocol.

Electrolytes: this is the most important supplement category for the first thirty days especially. Sodium, potassium, and magnesium are the three you need. Sodium: 2,000-3,000mg daily from food and water (salt your food liberally). Potassium: 1,000-3,500mg daily (cream of tartar, NoSalt, or a quality electrolyte supplement). Magnesium glycinate: 300-400mg before bed. These keep your energy up, reduce keto flu symptoms, prevent muscle cramps, and support sleep. Don't skip them.

Collagen peptides: start these by day sixty at the latest for visible results by the wedding. Earlier is better. One serving (typically 10-20 grams) daily in coffee, broth, or water. Collagen supports skin elasticity, hair thickness, and nail strength. It won't transform you overnight, but consistent use over eight to twelve weeks makes a noticeable difference.

Magnesium glycinate at night: even if you're getting some magnesium from your electrolyte routine, magnesium glycinate specifically before bed supports deep sleep and muscle recovery. Sleep quality directly affects how your body looks and how your face looks. This one is worth prioritizing.

Beef liver: optional but worth mentioning. Beef liver is the most nutrient-dense food on the planet. It contains vitamin A, B12, folate, iron, zinc, copper, and CoQ10 in quantities that are hard to match anywhere else. If you can stomach it (and not everyone can), two to four ounces two or three times per week covers most micronutrient bases without supplements. Frozen then thawed liver is milder in flavor if the fresh version puts you off.

Keep it simple. Electrolytes, collagen, and magnesium glycinate. That's the core stack. The rest is noise.

SECTION 6: NAVIGATING SOCIAL SITUATIONS
---

Ninety days is a long time, and your social life doesn't pause for a diet protocol. Here's how to handle the situations that will come up.

Bridal shower food: bridal showers typically involve a lot of finger foods, pastries, and mimosas. You can eat before you go and then focus on any meat, cheese, or egg-based options at the spread. Most people won't notice what you're eating because they're too busy catching up. If someone asks, "I'm just being mindful before the wedding" covers it without inviting debate.

Bachelorette weekend: this one is harder because it often involves multiple meals and a lot of drinking. Do your best. Eat the steak at dinner. Skip the pasta. Choose spirits over beer or sugary cocktails if you drink. If you end up eating pizza at 2am, it's not the end of the protocol, it's one night. Get back on track the next morning without guilt. Bachelorette weekend should be fun. Don't let dietary perfection steal that.

Family dinners with future in-laws: this is a delicate one. You don't want to be the person who makes mealtimes complicated, especially with family you're still building a relationship with. Choose the protein-forward options on the table and eat everything else in small amounts. A little roasted potato isn't going to derail 90 days of solid work. Grace over guilt.

Restaurant ordering: this is actually the easiest situation. Steak, burger (no bun), salmon, roasted chicken. Most restaurants have something that works. Ask for extra meat instead of a side if you want more food. Skip the bread basket by telling the server you'd prefer not to have it at the table.

The open bar question: this is your call and I won't moralize about it. Alcohol stalls fat loss and disrupts sleep, and those are simply facts. If you choose to drink, spirits (vodka, tequila, whiskey) without sugary mixers are the lowest-impact option. If you're in the final thirty days, consider making a decision in advance rather than in the moment at an open bar.

Honest summary: strict isn't always possible across ninety days of life events. Do your best, return to the protocol after any slip without drama, and remember that consistency over time matters far more than perfection on any single day.

SECTION 7: TROUBLESHOOTING FAQ
---

I FEEL TERRIBLE IN WEEK 1. IS THIS NORMAL?

Yes, completely. What you're feeling is called keto flu, and it happens because your body is rapidly excreting glycogen and the water it holds, along with sodium, potassium, and magnesium. The headaches, fatigue, brain fog, and irritability are all real symptoms of a real physiological transition. They typically peak around days three to five and start resolving by days seven to ten. The fix is electrolytes, every day, religiously. Sodium in your food and water, potassium (cream of tartar or NoSalt), and magnesium glycinate before bed. Most people who describe severe week-one symptoms are significantly under-salting their food. Salt more than you think you should. This phase passes.

THE SCALE ISN'T MOVING. WHAT'S WRONG?

Probably nothing. The scale measures everything in your body including water, food currently being digested, hormonal fluctuations, muscle, and fat. It's not a precise instrument for tracking fat loss over short periods. Women also experience significant scale fluctuations across their menstrual cycle, sometimes five to eight pounds within a single month, none of which represents actual fat gain or loss. What the scale misses entirely is body recomposition, where you're losing fat and preserving or building muscle simultaneously. Your clothes, how you look in photos, and how you feel are more reliable data. If you've been consistent for three to four weeks and nothing is changing in how your clothes fit either, look at your protein intake first (are you hitting your target?), then look at dairy (are you consuming large amounts?), then look at sleep and stress.

MY PERIOD IS LATE OR IRREGULAR. SHOULD I BE WORRIED?

Significant dietary changes can temporarily affect menstrual cycles, particularly in the first one to two months. This is because the hormonal systems that regulate your cycle are sensitive to changes in body fat, calorie availability, and stress. If you were already in a calorie deficit before starting, or if you're losing weight quickly, cycle changes are more likely. Most women's cycles normalize once the body adapts to the new way of eating. That said, a late or significantly irregular period always warrants a conversation with your doctor. I'm not a doctor and I can't tell you what's causing it or whether it's concerning in your specific situation. If it persists beyond eight weeks or if you have other symptoms, please see a healthcare provider.

I'M GETTING MARRIED IN LESS THAN 90 DAYS. CAN I STILL DO THIS?

Yes. Sixty days is still meaningful. Forty-five days is still meaningful. The 90-day framework gives you the full arc, but the principles apply regardless of your timeline. If you have less time, condense. Skip to Phase 2 immediately. Be strict with the basics from day one. Focus your effort where it shows most: eliminate the highest-inflammation foods (seed oils, sugar, grains), hit your protein target, sleep eight hours, drink your water. You won't have the full adaptation benefit that comes from ninety days, but you'll still see real changes in how your body looks and how you feel.

MY FIANCE THINKS THIS IS EXTREME. HOW DO I HANDLE IT?

This comes up a lot. Here's what I'd say: you don't need to justify your health choices to anyone, including your partner. That said, sharing your reasoning calmly and specifically is more effective than defending yourself. "I want to feel energetic and clear-headed on our wedding day, and this is the approach I've decided to try" is hard to argue with. If he's worried about your health, that's worth acknowledging: "I appreciate that you care. I've researched this and I feel confident it's safe for me." If he's concerned you won't enjoy the engagement period, you can point out that you'll still be present at every event, sharing meals (you'll just be eating meat), and celebrating everything with him. You don't have to convince him. You just have to follow through consistently enough that results speak louder than arguments.

I CHEATED AT THE BACHELORETTE PARTY. NOW WHAT?

You get back on the protocol tomorrow morning. That's it. One night, even one full weekend, is not enough to undo weeks of consistent work. Your body's fat-adaptation mechanisms don't shut off the moment you eat a slice of cake. You might retain a couple of pounds of water from the carbohydrates, you might feel a little foggy for a day, but you will not have erased your progress. The thing that actually derails people isn't the slip, it's the shame spiral that follows. One bad weekend followed by three more bad weeks is a problem. One bad weekend followed by a clean Monday morning is just called having a life. Return without drama.

HOW TO USE YOUR WEEKLY TRACKER

Print one page per week. At the end of each day, spend two minutes filling it in honestly. You're not being graded. You're building a picture of your progress over time that the scale can't give you.

The energy and mood scores are just numbers from 1 to 5. Don't overthink them. 1 is rough, 3 is okay, 5 is great. The pattern over weeks tells you more than any single day's score.

The "how clothes fit" column is where you note anything specific. "Jeans looser in the waist." "Arms look more defined." "Same as last week." Even when the scale isn't moving, this column often shows you something is changing.

The win of the day doesn't have to be food-related. It can be "slept eight hours," "said no to the office donuts," "made it through a stressful day without stress eating," or simply "drank my water." Looking for a win each day builds the mindset that carries you through ninety days.
