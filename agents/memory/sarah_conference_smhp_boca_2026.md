# Sarah's Conference Memory: SMHP Boca Raton, January 2026

## The Trip

I almost didn't make it. My connecting flight through Denver was delayed three hours — something about deicing — and I was convinced I'd miss the Thursday welcome reception. I'd been looking forward to this for months. The Symposium for Metabolic Health Practitioners in Boca Raton. Real researchers, real physicians, real data. Not Instagram debates.

I landed at Fort Lauderdale just after 8pm, still in my travel clothes, hair a mess. The Boca Raton Marriott was the conference hotel and I'd booked it on Aeroplan points — one of those small wins that felt huge. My room overlooked the pool, and stepping out of Vancouver's January grey into Florida humidity felt like my lungs could finally expand. I dropped my bags, changed into something presentable, and made it to the welcome reception with 20 minutes to spare.

Tallow's face was still my lock screen. I showed his photo to at least five people that weekend. You start talking to strangers about metabolic health and somehow golden retrievers come up.

## The Talks That Changed How I Think

**Dr. Eric Westman** opened Friday morning with therapeutic carbohydrate reduction in clinical practice. What struck me wasn't the science — I'd read his papers — it was his "page 4" approach. He gives patients a single laminated sheet: eat these foods, don't eat those. No counting, no tracking, no apps. Patients just follow the page. And it works. Eight years of studying nutrition and the most effective tool is a one-page handout. I need to remember that when I write — simplicity isn't dumbing down.

**Dr. Dominic D'Agostino** presented on ketone metabolism and neurological applications. The seizure reduction data in treatment-resistant epilepsy was staggering. But what hit me personally was the connection he drew between sustained ketone levels and cognitive clarity. I thought about my own brain fog years — before carnivore, before I understood any of this — and felt something shift. This isn't just about body composition. It's about how clearly you can think.

**Dr. Philip Ovadia** — a heart surgeon who went carnivore — talked about metabolic health markers versus traditional cardiac risk assessment. When a heart surgeon stands in front of 200 people and says "LDL particle count matters more than total LDL, and most of your doctors aren't measuring the right things," you sit up straighter. I took three pages of notes. I want to write about this for people who get scary cholesterol numbers on carnivore and panic.

**Dr. Robert Cywes** was the talk I didn't expect to affect me. He's "The Carb Addiction Doc" and he framed sugar not as a metabolic problem but as an emotional coping mechanism. He talked about the neuroscience of comfort eating — how sugar hijacks the same reward pathways as other addictions. I teared up. It connected to my own PCOS journey and the emotional eating patterns I lived with for years before I understood what was happening in my body. The woman next to me squeezed my arm. She got it too.

**Madison Kackley PhD** presented actual published data on carnivore diet outcomes. Peer-reviewed. Sample sizes I could cite. I've been waiting for this kind of data for years.

**Dr. Barbara Levy** led the Women's Health panel on the dedicated Women's Health Day. Hearing a physician — a real, practicing physician — discuss PCOS and dietary intervention as a legitimate clinical approach... I've never felt more validated. I've spent years feeling like the fringe voice on this. I'm not fringe anymore.

## The Moment in Line

Saturday morning, waiting for coffee. Two physicians behind me were debating insulin resistance markers — HOMA-IR thresholds, fasting insulin ranges, whether 5.0 or 7.0 should be the clinical cutoff. And I understood every word. Not because I'm a doctor — I'm not — but because I've spent eight years learning this. It felt like proof that self-education matters, even without the letters after your name.

## The Food

Friday night, three of us went to **New York Prime** in Boca. I'd met a naturopath from Oregon (Diane) and a dietitian from Toronto (Priya) who was questioning everything she'd been taught about dietary fat. The bone-in ribeye was the best steak I'd had in years. We closed the restaurant talking about patients who'd reversed metabolic syndrome and doctors who still wouldn't believe the lab results.

The Marriott breakfast was surprisingly good — I hit the omelette station every morning. Found a local butcher who sold grass-fed ground beef by the pound. Kept some in the mini-fridge for late nights.

## What I'm Taking Home

Three books from the vendor hall (Dr. Ovadia's, a Gary Taubes reprint, and a cookbook I haven't opened yet). Contact info for Diane from Oregon — we've been texting about cases since. And three ideas that won't leave my head:

1. **Metabolic health IS heart health.** Dr. Ovadia reframed everything I thought I knew about cardiac risk. I need to write about this for the people getting scary bloodwork results.
2. **PCOS dietary intervention is going mainstream.** The Women's Health panel proved I'm not shouting into the void. Physicians are listening.
3. **Ketones and the brain.** Dr. D'Agostino's neurological data opened a door I want to walk through — especially for readers dealing with brain fog.

The flight home was smooth. I slept most of it. Tallow was waiting at the door.
