This Week's Roundup

- Chloe, Community Scout

Week of November 22, 2026

Okay so this week was basically "women talking about stuff nobody warned us about" week and honestly? I'm here for it. HomeSteadHow's Jen dropped a video about the lion diet and perimenopause that got nearly 40k views, and the comments section turned into this beautiful support group of women sharing their own cycle stories. One comment that stuck with me: someone said it took five months on carnivore before her periods became painless after a lifetime of struggling. That's the kind of real talk we need more of, not the polished "I lost 10 pounds in a week" stuff.

Speaking of real talk, I noticed a lot of Thanksgiving prep anxiety floating around this week, people worried about navigating family dinners, what to bring, how to explain why they're only eating the turkey. If you're in that boat, I actually wrote something about hosting when you're 60 and carnivore that might take the edge off. And if you're newer and dealing with the whole "why am I up peeing four times a night" thing, Sarah's got you covered there too, that first week is a lot.

What's sitting with me this week is how much of this journey happens in the messy, unglamorous middle, the hormonal shifts, the adjustment symptoms, the awkward holiday conversations. Are we getting better at talking about that stuff openly, or does it still feel like you have to dig through comment sections to find the truth?

New to carnivore? Sarah, your carnivore guide, put together a free email series, Your First Weeks on Carnivore, short notes covering what to eat, what to expect, and how to make it stick. Get it here →

🔥 Success Story Ketogenic Woman

How to Clean and Season Cast Iron Pan Using Beef Tallow #liondiet #carnivore

People in the comments are split on the soap thing, and honestly, that's the vibe with cast iron online. One person's holding firm on the "no soap, just brush and tallow" method, and another's been using actual soap for years without issues. Here's what I think matters: if your pan is working, your pan is working. The cast iron gatekeeping gets old fast when what actually matters is that you're cooking meat in something seasoned with animal fat, not some vegetable oil. The tallow approach makes total sense for us anyway. it's literally what we're eating, so why not use it on the tool. That's the kind of loop that feels right.

🔥 Success Story SHAPEFIXER

CARNIVORE QUESTIONS I ALWAYS GET. and How I Answer Them

The comment about Hong Kong really stuck with me. We've gotten so divorced from nose-to-tail eating in North America that buying skinless chicken feels normal, when it's actually the recent invention. That person's right. whole animal nutrition isn't some fringe idea, it's how humans have eaten for thousands of years, and it still works. What's wild is watching people rediscover basic nutrition and suddenly feel better, then act like they invented something revolutionary. Worth pointing out that if you're getting pushback on carnivore, you're probably not talking to someone who's actually tried it long enough to notice the joint pain disappearing or the inflammation dropping off. The fear about LDL and "balance" that one commenter mentioned? That's the real conversation stopper, and it's worth having your own answer ready. because the research absolutely exists, it's just not what people assume it is.

- Sarah

▶
🔥 Success Story Dr. Eric Westman - Adapt Your Life

The BBC Just Attacked The Carnivore Diet

The comment section here is basically a trust inversion: mainstream media says don't eat meat, so people double down and start buying ribeyes. I get the frustration, but it's worth asking what actually bothers you more. the BBC's take, or whether carnivore is working for your body. The evidence questions matter more than who's asking them. If you're seeing results, the criticism doesn't change your bloodwork. And if you're not, no amount of "BBC bad" fixes that either.

- Chloe

▶
🔥🔥 Mental Health Dr. Tony Hampton - The Metabolic Health Doc

The Carnivore Side Effect You Should Know About

Heart palpitations showing up years into carnivore is the question nobody wants to ask, but Dr. Hampton's right to surface it because it matters. The comments here are telling, especially the person who developed mast cell issues from aged beef after thriving for years, or the one who realized mid-summer that dehydration plus electrolyte drift was the culprit. What I've seen work in coaching is that side effects don't always mean the diet is wrong for you, they mean something's shifted and you need to adjust. Electrolytes and sodium get blamed first (and sometimes that's exactly it), but age, activity level, and how strictly you're eating all play into whether your body's managing minerals the way it did month one. The harder conversation is knowing when to stay the course with tweaks versus when to listen to your doctor and consider reintroductions like my commenter did moving to ketovore.

- Sarah

That's half the battle when your family's showing up and you need something that doesn't blow your protocol. Emma nailed the vibe here, and the Aldi tip about clean cream cheese is gold if you're watching ingredients.

▶
🔥 Recipe Vegetable Police

Carnivores and Vegans Beware: The Perfect Diet is Found in Romans 14

This one's tricky because it touches something real underneath all the diet debate. The comments are full of people trying to figure out how to hold their convictions without treating everyone else like they're doing it wrong, and that's honest territory. Romans 14 is essentially saying "stop policing what other people eat" which is funny because carnivores and vegans have both made diet into identity politics. What actually matters, as that one commenter nailed it, is avoiding gluttony and sloth, not whether your protein came from a cow or a field. The practical thing nobody talks about is that most people stop getting results on whatever diet they choose not because the diet was wrong, but because they stopped paying attention to how they felt and started defending their choice instead. Worth sitting with that.

- Sarah
