This Week's Roundup

- Chloe, Community Scout

Week of October 11, 2026

Okay so this week the carnivore internet collectively decided it's soup season and honestly I'm not mad about it. Meet Carnivore dropped a chicken soup video that racked up 140k views in like a week, and the comments section turned into an impromptu cooking class, someone explained the whole egg tempering technique and now I feel like I should've paid more attention in culinary school (I didn't go to culinary school). There's something about watching someone make a creamy bacon cheddar chicken soup that just hits different when it's getting dark at 5pm.

The vibe this week was definitely comfort food and low-effort meals, which tracks because everyone I know is suddenly exhausted. If you're in that "I cannot look at another pan" mood, I wrote a whole thing about dinners that require basically zero cooking, rotisserie chicken and canned fish are carrying us through autumn, no shame. Also noticed a lot of chatter about chicken and pork being more budget-friendly right now, and Marcus actually broke down how to make those work if beef prices are making you wince: worth a read.

Honestly though, I'm curious, are you guys leaning into the cozy soup/stew era or still grilling like it's July? Because my feed is split down the middle and I genuinely can't tell if soup discourse is just algorithm brain or if we're all actually craving warm bowls of meat right now.

New to carnivore? Our health coach Sarah put together a free email series, Your First Weeks on Carnivore, short notes covering what to eat, what to expect, and how to make it stick. Get it here →

🔥 Success Story Ketogenic Woman

Brown Butter Pumpkin Spice Cake for Carnivore-ish Thanksgiving Dessert

Here's the thing about Thanksgiving: it's the one meal where everyone's watching what you eat, and the dessert table feels like a landmine. This brown butter pumpkin spice cake is exactly what people in our community need right now, because it lets you actually participate without the guilt or the blood sugar crash. Anita nailed the execution here, and you can tell from the comments that folks are already planning to make this early and bring it to their family tables, which means they're not showing up as "the person on a diet" anymore, they're just bringing a delicious dessert. That's the real win.

▶
🔥 Success Story The Daily Ribeye

We Tried The Carnivore Diet For 90 Days… Would We Do It Again??

The comments here tell the real story, and it's way more interesting than a simple "yes or no" answer. You've got people celebrating the glow-up, sure, but you're also seeing folks struggle with hormones, cravings, and the friction between what works in theory and what actually sticks in real life. That Texas comment hit different, right? "Some days are better than others." That's honest. What I've noticed coaching is that carnivore works brilliantly for blood sugar control and inflammation, but if someone's hormones are already dysregulated or their relationship with food is tied to emotional stuff, this diet alone won't fix that. The women commenting about cycles and mood. that's a real thing, and it usually means timing, nutrient density, or stress management needs attention too. Stick with it if it's working, but don't expect one diet to solve everything a body's been holding onto for years.

- Sarah

🔥🔥 Trending Meet Carnivore

This Carnivore Soup Is All I Want to Eat

Community's got the tempering technique locked in, and honestly, watching people geek out about culinary fundamentals on a carnivore video is kind of beautiful. The egg yolk-and-cream liaison isn't some fancy secret. it's just preventing scrambled eggs in your soup. but it matters because it changes the texture completely. Everett's casual approach to talking through it while making people feel welcome (shoutout to that grandma call) is exactly why this video hit 140K views. If you're tired of basic broths, this one's worth the three extra minutes to do it right.

6 Years on Carnivore — THIS is the meal I still crave and eat EVERY WEEK

After six years, the fact that she's still reaching for this same meal every week says something most diet trends can't claim. It's not exotic or complicated, which is exactly why it works. The comment about losing 60 pounds in six months on ground beef alone resonates here too, because this isn't about finding the "perfect" recipe. it's about finding what your body actually wants to eat repeatedly. One-pot meals like this are the backbone of real carnivore sustainability, not the exception.

▶
🔥 Motivation Dr. Tony Hampton - The Metabolic Health Doc

They Are LYING To You About GROUND BEEF

The community's already spoken, and they're right. Ground beef isn't the "inferior choice". it's the practical choice that actually gets people through their week. You've got someone eating it raw for four years with zero issues, a retired guy hitting $100/week on meat by shopping markdowns, and plenty of folks saying "I can't cook, so ground beef saved my life." That's not a compromise. That's a protocol that works. Dr. Hampton's right to push back on the ribeye gatekeeping, because the math doesn't lie: if ground beef gets you consistent protein intake at a price you can sustain, it beats the expensive cut you skip because of cost. The real nutrition win isn't what's on the menu. it's what you actually eat.

- Marcus

▶
🔥 Recipe Vegetable Police

I Found The Perfect Diet

The comments are doing something important here. they're moving past the "which diet is best" argument and landing on what actually matters: absorption, hormones, and how you feel day to day. One person nailed it: "Every diet worked for a week then failed. My hormones were the real issue all along." That's the carnivore conversation in a nutshell. We see it constantly in coaching. Women especially come to us after cycling through every approach, only to realize their digestion or hormone balance was the real blocker, not the macros. The "perfect diet" isn't about the foods themselves. it's the one your body can actually use and that keeps your energy and mood stable long enough to stick with it.

- Sarah
