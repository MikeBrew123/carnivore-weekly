This Week's Roundup

- Chloe, Community Scout

Week of October 28, 2026

Okay so this week felt like everyone collectively decided to stress about the same things at once, processed meat fears and holiday panic. Dr. Berry dropped a video reading through every ingredient on cheap deli meats and hot dogs, basically asking "compared to what?" when people freak out about bologna being deadly. Over 340k views and the comments are full of people saying "I grew up on this stuff and we weren't all sick," which. Yeah, context matters. It's a good reminder that perfect shouldn't be the enemy of good enough, especially if budget is a real factor.

Speaking of not being perfect, I wrote about getting through the holidays without starting over because I know some of y'all are already sweating Thanksgiving. And if you're dealing with the fatigue that makes holiday prep feel impossible anyway, Sarah's piece on being tired all the time after 50 is worth a read. Oh, and exciting news, Sarah posted her first YouTube Shorts! One's about the "only down 3 pounds" frustration in week one, and the other shows how to cook flank steak so it's not chewy shoe leather. We're brand new at this video thing, so check them out and let us know what you think in the comments or just reply to this newsletter.

Honestly, I keep thinking about how much of this lifestyle is just. Permission to stop overthinking. Eat the hot dog, survive the holidays, don't quit over a weird week on the scale. What's the thing you're currently overthinking?

New to carnivore? Sarah, your carnivore guide, put together a free email series, Your First Weeks on Carnivore, short notes covering what to eat, what to expect, and how to make it stick. Get it here →

🔥 Success Story Meet Carnivore

Why I Almost Quit Carnivore (And The 1 Change That Saved Me)

Here's the thing that jumped out at me from the comments: that person who hit 90 days, "rewarded" themselves with pizza and cupcakes the same day, and then spiraled into failure thinking. Sound familiar? It's not about the cupcake. It's about the story we tell ourselves after. The comment thread is full of people saying the same thing in different ways. Keep it simple. Eat meat. Don't overthink it. One person mentioned they dial in 7 ounces per meal, prep 100 meals at a time, and that's their entire system. Another said old narratives about fat and salt were the actual weight they were carrying. Nobody here is talking about willpower or discipline. They're talking about removing the noise. That's what makes this video land with people. It's not "carnivore is easy." It's "stop making it harder than it needs to be." The person who almost quit, came back, and stuck with it because they simplified? That's the real story. Not the plan, not the macros. The part where you stop treating one slice of pizza like a personal failure and just eat a hamburger instead.

- Chloe

🔥 Success Story kierunek zdrowie

Przykładowy dzień na keto, carnivore - foodbook

What I love about this is how straightforward it is. One viewer mentioned their entire carnivore diet comes down to four things: beef broth, ground pork, eggs, and sardines. That's it. No overthinking, no special supplements or fancy ingredients, just real food that costs reasonable money and keeps them satisfied. The soft scrambled eggs over sardines with passata is a perfect example of what actually works for people long-term. simple combinations you can repeat without getting bored. If you're still stuck in the idea that carnivore has to be complicated or expensive, this is the reminder you need.

🔥 Science Anthony Chaffee MD

The Amazing Maggie the 85 year Carnivore!

The comments are telling you everything you need to know here. People aren't watching Maggie because she's some unicorn exception. They're watching because at 85 she's doing the things most people assume they'll lose by 65: riding a bike, driving, climbing stairs, keeping her teeth. That's not inspirational fluff, that's a completely different retirement story than what most people have been told to expect. What's really resonating in these comments is the permission it gives. One person said they don't want to be a burden on their family as they age, and suddenly it doesn't feel like a diet choice anymore, it feels like an insurance policy. Maggie's showing up in this video as proof that you can actually keep your autonomy and your body intact if you feed it right early enough, whether you're 25 or 70 right now.

- Chloe

🔥🔥 Mental Health Carnivore Quest

I Need To Vent: Quit Judging Me

The comments on this one hit different. Someone mentioned gene expression research proving that some people's bodies just fight harder against weight loss, and that landed because it's true. and it matters. Cassie's frustration isn't weakness or lack of discipline, it's biology pushing back, and the family members who get that (like that one comment about respecting the "no thank you") are the ones worth keeping close. What matters most here is that she's still showing up, still eating the way that makes her body feel better, even when the scale isn't cooperating or strangers are loud about what she should do instead. That's not failure. That's exactly what sticking with it looks like for a lot of people. not the Instagram transformation story, but the quiet, steady work of managing blood sugar and not apologizing for it.

- Sarah

▶
🔥 Motivation Emmathecarnivore

This Zero Carb Carnivore Pizza Feels Like Cheating

The parmesan crust pizza thing is everywhere now, and honestly, it works because it's stupid simple. Ground meat, cheese, egg, into the oven. But I notice the comments are splitting two ways: people excited to make pizza again with their families, and people defensive about not needing "extra products" or fancy recipes. Both are right. You don't need a zero-carb pizza to stay carnivore, and you don't need to apologize for making one either. The real win here is that when someone says "pizza night with friends," you don't have to sit there with a plate of meat while everyone else eats. That's the point. Make it, enjoy it, move on. The superiority comments miss that. carnivore isn't better because it's restrictive, it's better because it works for you and fits your life.

- Marcus

▶
🔥 Recipe Kent Carnivore

My Depression Lifted in 21 Days - Without Therapy or Medication 😳

What strikes me most is how many people in the comments are saying the same thing: that 21-day mark where something just switches on. Not gradually better, but a literal flip. One commenter described waking up after a decade of depression and feeling like they'd finally come home in their own body, and that's been stable for seven years. Another stopped antidepressants the same week they started eating meat and never looked back. This matters because depression gets treated like it's purely neurological, something that only responds to the right pharmaceutical cocktail. But what we're actually seeing here is a metabolic story. Chronic inflammation, blood sugar chaos, and nutrient deficiencies from a plant-heavy diet can absolutely tank your mood and energy. It's not magic that it shifts so fast for some people, it's just that your brain gets what it actually needs. I covered some of this in our piece on carnivore and mental health, but the speed people report still surprises me. The real takeaway from these comments isn't "everyone's depressed because of carbs." It's that if you're struggling and nothing else has worked, you actually have nothing to lose by trying this for a month and seeing what happens. Even if your family doesn't get it yet.

- Sarah
