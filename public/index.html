This Week's Roundup

- Chloe, Community Scout

Week of August 19, 2026

Okay so this week had a real "what are we actually doing here" energy and honestly I'm into it. Laura Spath dropped a video called "Eating a Carnivore Diet Forever is NOT the Goal" and the comments section turned into this fascinating debate about long-term intentions. One comment that stuck with me was basically "if we could be healthy eating the standard American diet, none of us would be here", which, fair point. It's interesting watching the community wrestle with whether carnivore is a destination or a pit stop, and I don't think there's a wrong answer? But it's worth thinking about your own "why" beyond just the initial results.

The engagement on that video was pretty solid, over 22k views and 330 comments in less than a week, which tells me people are hungry for these bigger-picture conversations. Sarah actually wrote something adjacent to this recently about knowing when carnivore might not be your forever answer, so clearly this is in the air right now. I love that we're getting past the "ribeye fixes everything" phase and into more nuanced territory.

I guess my question for you is: do you think about an end game, or are you just vibing with what's working? Because I genuinely don't know my own answer and that feels okay for now.

🔥 Success Story No Carb Life

That's When I Tried Carnivore.

The comment that stuck with me here is about that mental block. people genuinely struggle to believe that eating fatty meat can actually work. After all, decades of nutrition messaging told us the opposite, so carnivore feels like permission you're not sure you're allowed to have. Kevin's story is proof that the permission is real. And what's interesting is the ripple effect people are reporting in the comments. Vision improvements, reversed diabetes, that sense of food freedom hitting differently. These aren't tiny changes. They're the kind of shifts that make someone step back and think, "Wait, what else have I been wrong about?" That's the moment carnivore clicks.

🔥 Success Story Shawn Baker MD

Can Meat Really Give You "Cheese Hands"?

Okay, so the community's reaction here is basically "who on earth eats 6-9 pounds of cheese daily?" and honestly, they're right to be skeptical. This feels like one of those internet rabbit holes where someone takes an extreme outlier and tries to make it sound like a carnivore problem. The cheese hands thing is wild, but the real takeaway is that most of us eating meat are doing fine, and the people inventing phantom conditions might just need to. not eat cheese the size of a small child. Baker does a solid job calling out the noise here.

The Easiest Carnivore Flatbread! Only 2 Ingredients

What I love most about this video is that people aren't just making the recipe, they're making it their own. One commenter nailed it with the Pyrex dish hack (perfectly sliceable, holds together), another went full tuna instead of sardines, and someone else just casually mentioned using a waffle iron to get that crispy exterior. That's the real win here, the fact that a two-ingredient base is flexible enough to accommodate how different people actually cook and eat. The lingering fish smell comment made me laugh because that's the honest conversation nobody has, but the spirit of the replies shows people are genuinely excited to try it anyway. This is exactly the kind of simple, protein-forward cooking that makes carnivore sustainable for real life, not just theory.

The constipation comment always cracks me up. most people find the opposite happens once they adapt, though I get why folks worry.
