This Week's Roundup

- Chloe, Community Scout

Week of August 02, 2026

Okay so this week was basically "respect your elders" week in the best possible way. Dr. Baker dropped a video about an 85-year-old carnivore that absolutely took off, over 214k views, and the comments section turned into this beautiful parade of people in their 70s and 80s sharing their own stories. My favorite was an 83-year-old who's been carnivore for six and a half years, lifts three days a week, and still works part-time helping her daughter's family. The vibe wasn't "look at this exception," it was more like. Maybe we've been underestimating what's possible when you just eat meat and stay moving?

What struck me most was how the conversation kept circling back to attitude and consistency over any magic formula. One commenter pointed out that the woman in the video seems to have a genuinely good attitude and spends time outside, not exactly groundbreaking advice, but sometimes the basics hit different when you see them playing out in someone's actual life. It's a nice counterpoint to all the optimization chatter that can dominate this space. Speaking of keeping things simple, if you missed my piece on the home curing trend, it's basically the same energy, people getting back to basics and finding joy in it.

Honestly, this week left me wondering: are we overcomplicating this whole thing? Or is there something specific about longevity on carnivore we should be paying more attention to? I'd love to hear from anyone who's been doing this for five-plus years, what's actually changed for you?

Content of the Week

Handpicked by our editorial team

▶
🔥 Success Story Bart Kay - Health Science

Carnivores - Give Me 12-Minutes, I'll Give You 6 Reasons to AVOID Salt !!!

Here's the thing. Bart's basically saying what our community already figured out but couldn't quite articulate. That one commenter who went from medicated and numb to salting everything and feeling normal? That's the real story. The salt panic is another case of someone reading one study and treating it like gospel, when your body's actually pretty good at telling you what it needs. Most of us don't need to overthink the shaker. Meat already has sodium, and if you're sweating or feeling off, your body will let you know. This is why carnivore works. it strips away the noise and lets you listen to what's actually happening instead of following a 25-step protocol.

- Chloe

▶
🔥 Success Story Dr. Tony Hampton - The Metabolic Health Doc

Why Fat Loss Stops on Carnivore

The dairy thing keeps coming up in these comments, and honestly, it's one of the first things I check with clients hitting a stall. Not because dairy is "bad". it's not. but because it's easy to overeat when everything tastes amazing and doesn't trigger hunger signals the way plant foods do. Some people genuinely do better dropping cheese and cream for a few weeks to reset, like that commenter mentioned. The lifting weights point is solid too. If you're not building muscle while losing fat, your body has less reason to keep dropping the scale. And that quote about gravity? That person got it exactly right. The scale is just one tool, and sometimes the real wins are what you can't measure. how your clothes fit, your energy, your joints moving better. If you've hit that stubborn 10-pound wall, it might not be that carnivore stopped working. It might just be time to get specific about what's actually happening under the surface.

- Sarah

▶
🔥 Science KenDBerryMD

Carnivore on a Tight Budget: 10 Cheap Foods That Still Deliver Nutrition

The community's response says everything. People aren't just grateful for budget tips, they're relieved that someone with credentials is saying out loud what they already know: you don't need to be rich to eat carnivore. The comments keep circling back to one thing, Dr. Berry's lack of pretension. No gatekeeping about grass-finished this or that. Just honest talk about spam, eggs, and ground beef as legitimate nutrition. That's what's resonating here, and honestly, it's the opposite of what wellness culture usually sells us.

- Chloe

▶
🔥🔥 Mental Health Dr. Eric Westman - Adapt Your Life

25 years Of Carnivore Advice In Just 15 Minutes

Dr. Westman nails something the internet loves to gloss over: there's no one carnivore script that works for everyone. Look at these comments. you've got strict carnivore folks thriving, people doing KetoVore with a few veggies, others finding that sweeteners wreck their adherence, and someone literally choosing meat because it keeps them out of the hospital after hernia surgery. That's not confusion. That's real life. The "effortless weight loss" myth is exactly what trips people up, and I've seen it happen in my coaching too. You can eat the right foods perfectly and still plateau if you're not tuning into *your* individual response. The sweetener debate in these comments is telling. some people lose control around erythritol, others use monk fruit and do fine. What matters most is paying attention to what actually happens in your body, not what the protocol says should happen.

- Sarah

▶
🔥 Motivation Carnivore Quest

I Have Finally Lost My Mind.August Will Be Tough

This is less about losing his mind and more about recognizing what actually works. The community is clearly onto something with sardines, and the consistency is striking, sardines plus eggs is showing real results for people who've hit plateaus or need a reset. August as a focused month makes sense, it's tactical, not crazy. What's interesting here is the support angle that keeps showing up in the comments, people doing this together with partners or family, which changes everything about whether you stick with it or bail. Worth watching how this plays out because extreme simplicity like this often reveals what your body actually responds to when you strip everything else away.

- Marcus

▶
🔥 Recipe pigmie

Meat & Fruit - 1 Month Follow Up - What Happened?

The comment about "at least two months" really landed, and honestly, that's where most experiments fail. One month with fruit mixed in isn't really testing carnivore or animal-based eating, it's testing snacking habits. The adaptation period is real, your body needs time to shift fuel sources, and you can't tell what's working when you're still halfway in. That said, the commenter who mentioned every diet has people who thrive on it? They're right too. The magic isn't in the diet itself, it's in whether you can actually stick to it and whether your markers improve. Some people do great with fruit and animal foods together, others need strict carnivore to feel the difference. The real test isn't winning a debate online, it's whether you feel better, sleep better, and your bloodwork backs it up.

- Sarah
