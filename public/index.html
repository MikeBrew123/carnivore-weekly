Week of April 06, 2026

The post that stopped everyone's scroll this week came from Reddit user IM_DaWarez: 95 lbs lost, A1C dropped from 7.4 to 4.1, off six medications, and now done with his doctor entirely. His take — that medicine shifted from treating causes to treating symptoms — hit a nerve. 388 upvotes and a comment section full of people sharing their own med exits. Across the sub, the pattern kept repeating: 20 lbs in two months, a woman whose period returned after just 11 days on carnivore, and one person who finally pulled the trigger on a whole NY strip loin at Costco and hasn't looked back. The most relatable post of the week? Someone complaining that the most annoying part of carnivore is running out of clothes.

On YouTube, the electrolyte supplement conversation got a reality check. Dr. Tony Hampton's deep-dive into LMNT, Re-Lyte, and Keto Chow drew 22,000 views and lit up the comments — turns out a lot of people are quietly annoyed about the maltodextrin in popular electrolyte formulas. The top comment was from someone mixing their own for pennies: 400mg magnesium glycinate, potassium citrate, half a teaspoon of Celtic salt, 32oz of water. Meanwhile the protein debate is heating up after Kent Carnivore tackled the "low protein is healthiest" claim that's been circulating in longevity circles.

The human stuff matters too. Ground Beef Chief introduced his girlfriend to 174 comments worth of people losing their minds with joy. Week 74 of his journey, and the comment "your heart rate was high cos you're in love" got 20 likes. That's carnivore in 2026 — people aren't just losing weight, they're getting their lives back. Which brings me to you: has carnivore changed something unexpected for you, outside of the scale? Drop it in the comments.

🔥🔥 Trending Kent Carnivore

Protein Minimalism: Bold Claim, Weak Evidence

The claim that a "low protein diet was actually the healthiest" is the kind of headline that deserves a hard look before you adjust your plate. Kent Carnivore dissects a viral assertion that's circulating in plant-based circles — and the comment section tells you everything you need to know about how carnivore practitioners receive it: one viewer reported doing five sets of thirty pull-ups approaching age 50 after going high-protein in 1994, the same year his vegetarian sister began a lifelong health decline. The actual research picture is more nuanced than either side usually admits: adequate protein intake is associated with preserved lean mass, better satiety signaling, and lower all-cause mortality in middle-aged and older adults. What gets lost in the debate is protein quality and bioavailability — animal proteins supply all essential amino acids in ratios the body can actually use.

🔥 Rising Amy Berger - Keto Without the Crazy

Your HbA1c Is High on Keto — Here's Why That's OK

This is one of the most important and most misunderstood lab findings in the low-carb world, and Amy Berger breaks it down with the clarity it deserves. HbA1c measures the percentage of hemoglobin that has glucose attached to it over roughly 90 days — but on a very low carbohydrate diet, red blood cells live longer because they're exposed to less oxidative stress from glucose, which means older cells accumulate more glycation even when blood glucose itself is well-controlled. A 5-year strict carnivore practitioner at 71 years old with 11% body fat and perfect CGM curves can still show an A1c of 6.1 that would alarm a conventional GP. The practical takeaway: pair your A1c with fasting glucose and a CGM before making any dietary changes based on that number alone.
