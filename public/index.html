This Week's Roundup

- Chloe, Community Scout

Week of June 28, 2026

Okay so this week was basically the Steak and Butter Gal show and I'm not even mad about it. Her zero-carb "sourdough" video absolutely blew up, 73k views and climbing, and honestly the comment section is sending me. Someone literally said "can you sell this already, I ain't trynna make it I just want to eat it" and that's the most relatable thing I've read all month. The recipe uses egg white powder and heavy cream powder which feels like science but apparently it works? The bread discourse is REAL right now in every carnivore group I'm in.

What I found interesting is that this wasn't just a novelty thing, people are genuinely craving solutions for the texture and ritual of bread without the carbs. It's not about missing bread nutritionally, it's about missing the experience of it, you know? Like having something to toast, something to spread butter on that isn't just. More meat. The 420 comments were surprisingly thoughtful too, lots of troubleshooting and recipe tweaks rather than the usual "that's not real carnivore" gatekeeping. Speaking of navigating carnivore in tricky situations, I wrote about surviving the office break room this week and honestly the "just bring your own food and stop apologizing" energy applies here too.

I guess my question is, where do y'all draw the line on stuff like this? Is egg white powder and cream powder "carnivore enough" or are we getting into keto-adjacent territory? Not judging either way, genuinely curious what's working for people.

🔥 Motivation Kent Carnivore

Sunscreen EXPOSED

The comment section here tells the real story. You've got people who burned like lobsters their whole lives now tanning normally. Redheads who ditched sunglasses. Folks who spent decades peeling after sun exposure and suddenly, after going carnivore, their skin just. handles it. That's not anecdotal noise, that's a pattern worth paying attention to. Here's what strikes me: the sunscreen industry has convinced us our skin is fragile. But the community is pointing out something the data backs up, too. Skin cancer rates keep climbing year after year, even as sunscreen use goes up. If the product worked, shouldn't we see the opposite trend? The "they died earlier" excuse doesn't hold when you're looking at consecutive year-on-year increases in new diagnoses. Something's off with that story. The metabolic angle makes sense. When you're fat-adapted and your cells aren't running on inflammatory fuel, your skin's resilience changes. Better cell repair, less oxidative stress. A few people mentioned making their own zinc oxide and tallow sunscreen instead, which is the practical middle ground if you're genuinely concerned about UV exposure. But the bigger takeaway is this: if your diet's cleaning up your metabolism, your body's defenses actually work again. That's worth more than any commercial product.

- Marcus
