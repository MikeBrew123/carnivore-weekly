This Week's Roundup

- Chloe, Community Scout

Week of July 05, 2026

Okay so, Fourth of July week brought the fireworks AND the drama. Steak and Butter Gal absolutely dominated with nearly 2 million views, and her "laziest way to kill visceral fat" video is everywhere right now. She had Robert Sikes on as a guest which probably explains the massive reach, when two big names team up, the algorithm pays attention. Speaking of pairing up, Marcus just dropped a killer meal prep guide for couples over on KetoDial that's honestly perfect timing if you're trying to get your partner on board this summer.

The visceral fat angle is interesting because it feels like the community is shifting from pure weight loss messaging to more specific health markers. People aren't just asking "will I lose weight" anymore, they want to know about the fat around their organs, their metabolic health, the stuff that actually matters long-term. Bella's been smart to lean into that with her 90-day transformation challenge too. It's less about aesthetics and more about what's actually happening inside.

I'm curious though, are we seeing the start of carnivore going more mainstream scientific, or is this just the algorithm rewarding health buzzwords? Because visceral fat has been trending across ALL diet spaces lately, not just ours. Would love to know if you're noticing the same thing in your feeds.

🔥🔥 Trending Steak and Butter Gal

93% Buy Meat At Costco Wrong (Buy This Instead!)

The marbling conversation in the comments is real. People aren't just looking for the cheapest cut anymore, they're learning to spot the difference between high Choice and low Prime, which honestly changes everything when you're buying in bulk. And yeah, the lamb comment caught me off guard too, but it tracks, people forget Costco's grass-fed lamb is legitimately one of the best protein-per-dollar plays they have. What's interesting here is that this isn't just about price anymore, it's about quality literacy. Three years ago carnivore meant "buy whatever meat is cheapest." Now the community's actually picking through cases, understanding fat content, and getting selective. That shift means people are staying on the diet longer because they're not getting bored with mediocre cuts.

🔥🔥 Trending Shawn Baker MD

Why Everyone’s Leaving Carnivore

The people staying on carnivore aren't leaving because they're missing carbs or because they heard scary news. They're staying because they stopped hurting. That's the pattern you see in the comments, over and over. One person mentions arthritis flare-ups disappearing. Another talks about finally being pain-free after years of chronic disease. A third points out that any time they slip off, they feel terrible enough to come right back. That's not willpower or stubbornness, that's your body telling you the truth about what works for it. The noise around "why people quit" usually ignores the people who actually stayed, and what their experience shows.

I'm Eating This ENTIRE Pizza Everyday and Losing Weight

This is exactly what people need to see. The comments are flooded with folks saying things like "pizza is my hardest food to give up" and "I've reversed heart disease and lost 60 pounds," which tells you something important: comfort food replacements aren't just nice to have, they're actually critical for staying on track long-term. What makes this different from the usual almond flour nonsense is that it's simple, it works, and it tastes legitimately good without requiring a pantry full of specialty ingredients. The cottage cheese base is clever because it's just food, not some processed substitute. If you've been thinking carnivore means never eating something satisfying again, this proves you wrong.

🔥🔥 Trending Mikhaila Peterson

Carnivore/Lion Diet Update: Not Carnivore Anymore?

This is the conversation we need to be having more often. Mikhaila's been the face of "never eating plants again," so watching her expand her diet after eight years takes guts. and it's honest. The lion diet got her to remission, which is incredible, but she's showing us that strict elimination isn't always the final destination. It's a tool. For a lot of people reading this, that's permission they didn't know they needed. You can use carnivore to heal, hit reset, figure out what your body actually tolerates. Then you get to make choices instead of being trapped by fear. That's the real win.
