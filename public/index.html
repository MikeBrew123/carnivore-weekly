This Week's Roundup

- Chloe, Community Scout

Okay so this week the algorithm really wanted us to panic about. Butter? Steak and Butter Gal dropped a video about how butter is "quietly changing" and we'll be "FORCED to eat it" which, look, I get the concern about ingredient creep, but the comments were wild, half the community immediately started sourcing raw butter from farms three states away while the other half pointed out they've been eating Kerrygold for years and their bloodwork is fine. Classic carnivore discourse. Meanwhile, Dr. Ken Berry's still out here with his greatest hits getting solid views, and there was a surprising amount of chatter about whether ground beef and eggs is actually the perfect baseline meal (spoiler: Marcus already covered this and honestly he's not wrong).

The vibe this week felt less "transformation story" and more "how do I actually sustain this long-term without going broke or crazy." Lots of practical content doing well, meal prep stuff, budget breakdowns, what to do when weight loss stalls. People are past the honeymoon phase and into the "okay but what about the next five years" questions, which honestly feels like growth for the community.

I'm curious though, are we getting too deep into the weeds with food purity stuff? Like yes, read your labels, but at what point does "butter vigilance" become its own kind of stress that probably isn't helping anyone's cortisol levels? Where's your line between informed and anxious?

🔥🔥 Trending Mikhaila Peterson

Carnivore/Lion Diet Update: Not Carnivore Anymore?

Mikhaila's update hits on something I see constantly in my coaching: the carnivore diet isn't always the final destination, it's often the starting point. After eight years of strict lion diet, her ability to reintroduce plants without triggering arthritis or depression is actually a win, not a failure. The real work was getting her body healed enough to tolerate variety again. This is why I always tell people that your day-one carnivore approach might not be your day-365 approach, and that's okay. What matters most is listening to your own body rather than treating any diet like dogma.
