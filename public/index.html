This Week's Roundup

- Chloe, Community Scout

Week of July 01, 2026

Okay so this week was basically the algorithm serving us a reality check. The most-watched carnivore-related video? A guy explaining why carnivore didn't work for him and he moved on to something else. FaceIQ's "The Diet That Actually Made Me Healthy (Not Carnivore)" pulled 42k views and honestly, the comments section is a whole journey, people debating raw mangoes, calling out plastic plates, the usual chaos. I'm not mad about it though. These "I quit carnivore" videos always spark the most interesting conversations about what actually works long-term versus what's just a phase.

What I found kinda telling is that it was a slow week for new carnivore content overall. Only 42 videos hit the relevance threshold, and most of the usual heavy hitters were quiet. Sometimes that happens around holidays, everyone's too busy grilling to film themselves grilling, I guess. Speaking of being out in the world, if you're hitting up Fourth of July cookouts this week and dreading the "wait, you're ONLY eating the burgers?" conversations, I wrote about surviving exactly that vibe last week. Same energy, different venue.

Honestly though, I keep thinking about why the critical videos get so much more engagement than the success stories. Is it because people love drama, or because there's a genuine curiosity about where the edges of this way of eating are? Maybe both. What's your take, do these "carnivore didn't work" videos make you defensive or genuinely curious?

If We’re Meant To Eat Fruits & Vegetables, Explain This!

The comments here tell the real story. People aren't just losing weight or fixing inflammation, they're getting their lives back, and they're shocked by how simple it was. One person reintroduced plants after years of carnivore and watched arthritis and psoriasis come roaring back within weeks, which is exactly the kind of personal experiment that changes minds faster than any argument ever could. What strikes me most is the tone, though, not anger or preaching, just quiet conviction, people saying "I feel better, so this is what works for me." That's the opposite of dogma, and it's why carnivore keeps spreading even without mainstream validation.

▶
🔥 Motivation Dr. Tony Hampton - The Metabolic Health Doc

A Dietitian Challenged My Carnivore Diet

The comments here tell the real story dietitians won't. People aren't switching to carnivore because it's trendy or because they've joined a cult. They're doing it because standard nutritional advice literally sent them to the ICU, packed on a hundred pounds, or left them foggy and sick for years. One commenter nailed it: "I don't feel as though I'm missing out on anything without carbs, and nobody understands that." That's not fanaticism. That's just someone who actually feels better. The hard truth is that the dietitian community has been trained on outdated carb-heavy guidelines while their patients' blood sugar and waistlines keep getting worse. This conversation matters because it's what needs to happen more: thoughtful professionals sitting down and actually asking whether the data backs up what we've been recommending. Check out our piece on blood sugar results to see what the actual outcomes look like.

I'm noticing the comment section is split between "this looks amazing" and "check your ingredients list." And honestly, that tension is worth paying attention to.
