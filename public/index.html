This Week's Roundup

- Chloe, Community Scout

Week of September 23, 2026

Okay so, pretty quiet week on the YouTube front, we basically got one video that made any noise, and it was Aseel Soueid trying Chris Pratt's carnivore approach for a day. Fifty-six thousand views, which honestly isn't bad for what's essentially "celebrity diet tourism." The video itself was fine, entertaining enough, but the comment section was just a wasteland of supplement bots and crypto-esque book promotions. Like, not a single genuine conversation happening down there, which felt like a bummer.

I think what's actually interesting is what wasn't there this week. No big transformation stories, no controversial takes getting everyone riled up, no "I quit carnivore" drama. Just. Quiet. Sometimes that happens, and I don't think it's a bad thing, the community doesn't need constant content churn to keep existing. But it did make me notice how much we rely on YouTube personalities to set the weekly conversation topics, you know? Speaking of which, if the whole "starting over again" cycle is hitting close to home, I actually wrote something about how to stop restarting that might help more than a celebrity diet video.

Honestly though, I'm curious, when things are quiet like this, does that feel peaceful to you, or does it make you wonder if the momentum is fading? I go back and forth.

New to carnivore? Our health coach Sarah put together a free email series, Your First Weeks on Carnivore, short notes covering what to eat, what to expect, and how to make it stick. Get it here →

🔥 Success Story Low Carb Love

THE SOUP THAT HELPS YOU LOSE FAT! 👀

Here's the vibe: bone broth and cabbage soup are having a real moment in the community, and there's something genuinely useful happening here beyond the nostalgia factor. What's interesting is that people aren't just excited about the recipe. they're sharing multi-generational stories (your 91-year-old mom swore by it, someone's aunt called it "peel a pound" soup). That tells me this isn't trending because of a TikTok algorithm, it's trending because it actually works for real people. The "negative calorie" myth gets debunked by nutrition experts, but what's *actually* happening is the protein and satiety from bone broth, plus the fiber and volume from cabbage, makes it genuinely hard to overeat. which means fat loss follows naturally. If you're hitting a plateau or looking for something filling that doesn't mess with your macros, this is worth a try.

- Chloe

▶
🔥 Success Story Low Carb Revelation

Keto Zero Carb Snacks / Keto Day of Eating

One thing that jumped out from the comments: people are genuinely invested in seeing you with your mom, not just the snacks. That says something real about what builds community. On the snack side though, I noticed someone mention their cholesterol spiked after going heavy on butter-based treats, and honestly, that's the conversation we need to have more openly. It's not that brown butter bites are "bad" for carnivore, it's that some people respond differently to fat ratios than others, and that's worth paying attention to instead of just assuming all high-fat foods work the same way. Your day-of-eating format is perfect for that because it shows real food in real quantities, not theoretical macros.

- Sarah

🔥 Science Dr Karan

Inside the Cult of Meat-Only Influencers

Dr. Karan's hitting on something the community keeps wrestling with quietly: the difference between carnivore as a personal tool and carnivore as an identity that crowds out everything else. Look at the comments, though. people aren't just disagreeing on macros, they're talking about feeling terrible on restrictive approaches, which is real and doesn't get enough airtime in diet spaces. The sustainability point lands hard too, especially when you're talking about long-term adherence versus what sounds good for 90 days. If you're curious about whether carnivore actually works for weight loss and health outcomes (spoiler: the data's more nuanced than the influencers admit), we've got actual research breakdowns that skip the dogma.

- Chloe

▶
🔥🔥 Mental Health Meet Carnivore

Why I Don't Explain the Carnivore Diet Anymore

The fiber joke in the comments nails it, but what really stands out is how many people are saying the same thing: they've stopped explaining because it's exhausting and pointless. This video captures something real about the carnivore community right now. You can show someone research, before-and-afters, even their own doctor's notes, and people still want to argue instead of listen. The creator's approach, "it works for me," is actually the smartest response because it sets a boundary without being defensive. You're not making a claim they can pick apart. You're just stating a fact about your life. That's why the "I didn't sign up for a debate" line resonates so hard in the comments, and honestly, it applies to way more than just diet conversations.

- Sarah

▶
🔥 Motivation Carnivore Quest

I Ate Only Sardines For A Month - Here's What Happened

One month, one food, real results. What strikes me most isn't just the 12 pounds, it's what the community noticed first: the face. Skin clarity, energy, the whole package shifted. A lot of people think carnivore is about the scale, but sardines stripped that down to the core truth, and it shows. Organ meats, bone broth, nose-to-tail eating all matter, but there's something honest about eliminating everything except one nutrient-dense whole food and watching what actually happens to your body. That's the kind of clarity most of us are chasing when we start this in the first place.

- Marcus

▶
🔥 Science Aseel Soueid

I Tried Chris Pratt's Carnivore Diet

What strikes me most about this video is the comment from someone who's been carnivore for 18 months and lost 90 pounds. That's not a celebrity transformation story. that's someone who picked up the diet, stuck with it, and actually saw their blood sugar and insulin normalize without medication. That's the real outcome people care about, and it's quieter than the "I tried Chris Pratt's diet" angle but way more meaningful. The simplicity factor is what keeps people going, especially when they're tired of counting macros or obsessing over food combinations. If you're curious about how carnivore actually works for weight loss and metabolic health, check out our research on carnivore weight loss. it covers exactly what you're seeing in these real-world results.
