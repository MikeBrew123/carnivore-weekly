This Week's Roundup

- Chloe, Community Scout

Week of September 20, 2026

Okay so this week was basically the JD Vance discourse hour and I'm not even mad about it. HomeSteadHow dropped a video called "Why JD Vance Won't Say the Word Carnivore" and it racked up 60k views with people absolutely going OFF in the comments. The whole debate is whether he's being strategically vague about his diet to avoid the vegan backlash or if he's just not fully committed, either way, one commenter's doctor apparently started carnivore after seeing their 254-pound weight loss, so we're converting the medical establishment one shocked physician at a time I guess.

The comments were honestly the best part, people speculating about political optics, sharing their own "my doctor couldn't believe it" stories, and the usual debates about whether saying "high protein with fermented vegetables" is carnivore-adjacent or just cowardice. It's kind of fascinating how this way of eating still feels like a thing you whisper about in certain circles, you know? Like we're all part of some underground steak club.

I keep thinking about how much energy goes into defending what we eat versus just. Eating it. If you're in that awkward phase where your body's still adjusting and you're wondering if you're doing it wrong, Marcus just wrote about how much to eat when you're not super active, solid read if you're overthinking portions. Anyway, curious what y'all think: do public figures talking around the diet help normalize it, or does the vagueness just make us look like we have something to hide?

New to carnivore? Our health coach Sarah put together a free email series, Your First Weeks on Carnivore, short notes covering what to eat, what to expect, and how to make it stick. Get it here →

🔥 Success Story Coach Stephen

Meat Consumption and the Planet: What They Don't Want You To Know

Coach Stephen's hitting on something the community keeps bringing up: the environmental argument against meat feels designed to make us doubt our own nutrition. What's resonating in these comments is the data flip, where people are realizing crop farming actually decimates way more wildlife and topsoil than grazing does, and that most cattle aren't grain-finished for their whole lives anyway. The regenerative agriculture angle (shout-out to those Carbon Cowboys references) shows us this isn't binary, either, and folks eating grass-finished beef aren't the environmental villains the narrative wants them to be. It's a reminder that the science on this stuff is murkier than the headlines suggest, and our readers deserve better than guilt-tripping when they're already making a choice that works for their bodies.

- Chloe

▶
🔥 Success Story Edward A. Goeke

Wait, Is JD Vance Carnivore?

What strikes me most about this isn't whether JD actually goes full carnivore. it's that RFK Jr. is out here casually mentioning diet change to a major political figure, and people are actually listening. The community's right: when high-profile people report real health wins, it shifts what's possible in people's minds. You can tell someone a thousand times that meat fixes your energy and digestion, but seeing it happen to someone they recognize? That lands differently. The political baggage around diet is real (that comment about tribalism nails it), but here's what matters: anyone who gets desperate enough to try something different and sees their health improve stops caring about the narrative. That's the actual power move.

- Sarah

🔥 Success Story Carnivore Revolution

3 Carnivore Rules That Finally Made It Work

The comment section is basically saying what we all need to hear: stop copying influencers and start listening to your own body. Someone went from keto bread obsession to zero-minute meal prep. Another person's been carnivore since 2017 but only figured out butter was causing bloat this year. That's real. It takes time to find your own rules, and honestly, that's where the magic happens. The people succeeding aren't following someone else's formula, they're experimenting until something clicks for them. Simplicity wins every single time.

- Chloe

🔥🔥 Mental Health Emmathecarnivore

This EASY Carnivore Mac & Cheese Is INSANELY Good

Emma's nailed something really practical here that our community keeps asking about: how do you make comfort food feel normal again on carnivore, not like you're "making do"? The comments are spot on about the double boiler technique (that gentle heat matters), but what I love is that she's showing people the actual shortcuts. Pork rind crust instead of breadcrumbs. Meat as the base instead of pasta. Simple swaps that take five minutes but feel indulgent. That's the difference between white-knuckling through carnivore and actually enjoying it long-term, especially for people dealing with cravings or family meals where everyone else is eating something totally different.

- Sarah

▶
🔥 Motivation Dr. Tony Hampton - The Metabolic Health Doc

The TRUTH About Redmond Real Salt

Salt marketing in the carnivore space has gotten almost religious, and this video cuts through it. The reality? Most of us don't need to spend $9 a pound on Redmond's when a good kosher salt or mineral-rich alternative does the same job for a fraction of the price. What's interesting from the comments is that people are actually thinking critically now. one person detailed switching from premium salts to Sicilian rock salt at a dollar a pound and hitting their mineral needs just fine. That's the move. Pick a salt without microplastics, hit your sodium and electrolyte targets, and stop treating it like a supplement. Protocol beats the brand name every time.

- Marcus

▶
🔥 Recipe Jenny Mitich

15 Carnivore Foods I Keep for Days I Refuse to Cook

Jenny's hitting on something our community brings up constantly: the mental load of meal decisions is often harder than actually eating. One of the top comments nails it. "zero mental effort spent deciding what to eat". and that's the real win here. Most nutrition advice ignores how exhausting it is to constantly plan, shop, and prepare, but carnivore solves that just by nature. The prep hacks in this video (frozen burger patties, sliced brisket between parchment, sardines ready to go) aren't fancy, but they're exactly what busy people need. Simple beats perfect every single time.
