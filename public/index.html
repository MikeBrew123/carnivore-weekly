Carnivore Weekly Roundup: What We're Watching This Week

Real talk, the sleep content is taking over my feed this week.

Steak and Butter Gal dropped a video about cortisol and sleep that absolutely exploded, over 1.3 million views in a week. "Just 1 TSP Kills Cortisol & Makes You Sleep 8 Hours Straight" is the kind of title that makes you roll your eyes and then click anyway, and apparently everyone else did too. But honestly? The fact that sleep keeps trending in carnivore spaces tells me something, we're past the "what do I eat" phase and into the "why do I still feel off" phase for a lot of people. That's actually growth.

Meanwhile, the usual suspects are doing their thing. Dr. Berry and Dr. Chaffee are putting out solid educational content, Kelly Hogan's keeping it real with long-term lifestyle stuff, and Nutrition with Judy is filling the "what's actually happening in my body" gap. But the overall vibe this week felt less about dramatic transformations and more about troubleshooting, which tracks with what I'm seeing in the community too. People aren't asking "should I try this?" anymore, they're asking "why isn't this working perfectly?" (Speaking of troubleshooting, Sarah just tackled the stalled weight loss question if that's on your mind.)

Here's what I'm sitting with: Are we entering a phase where carnivore content needs to get more nuanced to stay relevant, or will the clickbait cortisol hacks keep winning? I genuinely don't know, but I'm watching.

My Lab Work Results From Eating 117-Days of Pure Fat on a Carnivore Diet

Okay, so pure fat for 117 days is genuinely wild, but what's actually interesting here isn't the extreme. It's that someone tested it and showed their labs. The carnivore community obsesses over what the numbers *should* look like, and then we get actual data. Kelly's leaning into the fat-adaptation angle hard, which makes sense if you're trying to break through a plateau. The real question people will ask is whether this is sustainable or just a reset button, and honestly, that depends on your body. Some people need these kinds of experiments to move forward. Others get there with a solid weight stall protocol and patience. Either way, the lab work transparency is what matters here.

7 Weight Loss Lies + Q&A with Dr Berry & Nurse Neisha

The comment section here tells the real story. You've got a 68-year-old guy who lost 170 pounds total, a menopausal woman maintaining muscle on OMAD, and someone from Appalachia who had to heal her gut before carnivore could work. One commenter nailed it, mentioning Susan Powter from the 80s, and honestly that messaging never really went away, it just got recycled. Dr. Berry and Neisha tackled the biggest weight loss myths head-on, and the proof is literally in these comments, people reversing what they thought they knew about eating real food.

🔥 Science Anthony Chaffee MD

The #1 Method To Lose Belly Fat (NO CARDIO)

Anthony's hitting on something the community keeps asking about: you don't need to destroy yourself with cardio to shift body composition. The real driver here is protein and resistance training, which basically forces your body to hold onto muscle while fat becomes the fuel source. That's why so many people see better results eating more on carnivore than they did grinding away on a treadmill eating less. The takeaway isn't that cardio is useless, it's that if you're already eating for satiety and building strength, your metabolism does the heavy lifting for you.

- Chloe

▶
🔥 Science Paul Saladino MD

You'll Never Eat Sardines Again

Look, the comment section here is doing the heavy lifting Paul didn't. People are pointing out that sardines are bottom feeders (so mercury accumulation isn't the issue it is with tuna), that organic arsenic in fish is basically inert in your body, and that BPA in cans is becoming obsolete in most quality brands. The real talk from folks eating them 2-3 times weekly with great lab results says more than fear-mongering headlines. If you're buying Wild Planet or King Oscar in water instead of seed oil, you're getting one of the most affordable, nutrient-dense sources of omega-3s and bioavailable calcium on the planet. Context matters way more than clickbait.

- Sarah

▶
🔥 Science Jenny Mitich

Dr. Ken Berry Spits the Hard Truth About Carnivore, GLP-1s, Coffee & Dairy

Dr. Ken nails something most carnivore advocates won't say out loud: the diet works better when you're not fighting against medication or expecting it to fix everything at once. GLP-1s, dairy tolerance, coffee sensitivity, processed meat concerns. these aren't weaknesses in the framework, they're just variables you have to account for. What stands out here is how he refuses the binary thinking. It's not "carnivore is perfect" or "carnivore is dangerous," it's "here's what actually happens when you eat this way, and here's how to troubleshoot your individual situation." That pragmatism is what separates the people who succeed long-term from the ones who quit because they expected overnight miracles.

- Marcus

$17 Carnivore Bar vs $1.60 Homemade Carnivore Bar

Most people don't realize how much you're actually paying for convenience when you buy pre-made carnivore bars. Emma's breakdown here is eye-opening, and honestly, it's a reminder of what I always tell coaching clients: if you've got 20 minutes and basic ingredients, you can solve the "I need something portable" problem for a fraction of the price. The taste test being pretty much a tie is the real win here. You don't need to spend $17 to feel like you're eating something special on carnivore, and that matters when budget is already a barrier for a lot of people trying this way of eating. If DIY isn't your thing, fine. but at least know what you're actually paying for.
