Are Eggs and Dairy Allowed on the Carnivore Diet?

2026-05-28 by Chloe Navarro

This post may contain affiliate links. For educational purposes only, not medical advice. Details

Yes, eggs and dairy are allowed on the carnivore diet. They come from animals, so most people eating carnivore include eggs, butter, cheese, and cream every day. The only common version that cuts them out is the strict "Lion Diet" (beef, salt, and water), and even most of those folks add eggs and butter back later.

Okay, so this is one of the most-asked questions I see, every single week. "Are eggs and butter counted as part of carnivore?" "Is plain yogurt allowed?" "Does tallow count as meat or does dairy break the diet?" Here's the thing: the internet loves to argue about it, but the people who've stuck with this for years have mostly settled it in their kitchens. Here's the plain answer, food by food.

Which Eggs and Dairy Count as Carnivore

FoodCarnivore?What people actually do
Eggs (chicken, duck, quail)YesA daily staple for most people. Whole eggs, yolk included.
Butter and gheeYesThe default cooking fat in most carnivore kitchens.
Tallow, lard, bacon greaseYesRendered animal fat. Not dairy at all, and fine even on stricter versions.
Hard and aged cheese (cheddar, parmesan, gouda)Yes, for most peopleVery little milk sugar. Easy to overeat, so many people treat it as a topping.
Heavy cream and sour creamYes, for most peopleUsed in small amounts, in coffee or on ground beef.
Cream cheeseUsuallyCheck the label. Plain only, no flavors or added sugar.
Plain full-fat yogurtGrey areaHas more milk sugar. Stricter folks skip it; others keep a little.
MilkGrey areaThe most milk sugar of anything here. Many people drop it, or keep it for coffee only.
Flavored yogurt, flavored creamers, "cheese product"NoAdded sugar, starches, and oils. Not carnivore.

The simple rule the community uses: the fattier and plainer the dairy, the more "carnivore" it is. Butter and aged cheese are near the top. Milk and yogurt are lower, because they carry more of the natural milk sugar (lactose).

Why Some People Say Dairy "Isn't Carnivore"

You'll see comments like "dairy is not carnivore, full stop" on Reddit, and that comes from the elimination side of the community. If you're using carnivore to figure out what bothers your body, cutting dairy for a stretch makes sense. If dairy gives you bloating, skin flare-ups, or stalls your weight loss, that's a real reason to drop it for a while. Sarah has a whole piece on whether cheese is stalling your weight loss after 50, and another on being still bloated two months in.

But "dairy can bother some people" is different from "dairy isn't allowed." For most people, eggs and dairy aren't a cheat. They're food from animals, and they make this way of eating much easier to live with. If you want the strict version, here's when the Lion Diet actually makes sense.

Why Long-Term Carnivores Lean on Eggs and Dairy

I've been watching this community a long time, and the people still doing this at year two aren't eating ribeye every night. They're eating eggs. Lots of eggs. Plus butter, cheese, and a splash of cream. Three reasons keep coming up.

  • Cost. Steak is expensive. Eggs and cheese cost a fraction of what a good steak does per meal. Marcus ran the numbers on hitting your protein for about $5 a day, and eggs carry a lot of that.
  • Variety. Beef, chicken, pork, repeat. Boredom is a big reason people quit, which Sarah covers in why most people quit around week three. Eggs scrambled, fried, boiled, or baked with cheese break that up.
  • Speed. Four eggs scramble in three minutes. Sour cream on leftover ground beef is dinner. On a tired Wednesday, that matters more than any argument online.

Real talk: the diet that works is the one you can do when you don't feel like cooking. For a lot of us, that's eggs.

A Simple Week That Uses Eggs and Dairy

  • Breakfast most days: eggs cooked in butter. Pick the two or three ways you like best and rotate.
  • Lunch: leftover meat from last night, with a slice of cheese or a spoon of sour cream.
  • Dinner: ground beef or chicken thighs most nights. Steak once or twice a week if the budget allows.
  • Shopping list staples: eggs, butter, one block of aged cheese, heavy cream or sour cream, ground beef. That's the backbone.

Keep weekdays boring and save the nicer cuts for the weekend. That's how most people make this last. If you want the full picture of what else fits, the carnivore food list has it all in one place.

What If Eggs Bother You?

This one comes up more than you'd think: "Why can't I stomach eggs anymore?" Some people find eggs sit heavy, or they get tired of the taste. Try a different cooking method first, since some people do better with fried or soft eggs than hard-boiled, or the other way around. You can also lean on other meats for a while. If you get real stomach pain, rashes, or swelling after eating eggs or dairy, that's a doctor conversation, not a forum conversation.

A Quick Health Note

If you've been told to limit dairy, eggs, or saturated fat, or you have a known allergy, lactose intolerance, or a heart, kidney, or digestive condition, talk to your doctor before making eggs and dairy the center of your plate. If you're on meds or diagnosed with anything, you need actual medical supervision. Don't make changes based on internet articles, including mine.

I'm not a doctor. I'm just someone who's deep in the community and reads everything. Take all health stuff with a grain of salt (pun intended). I can tell you what people are trying and what's working for them, but you've got to make your own calls with your own doctor.
