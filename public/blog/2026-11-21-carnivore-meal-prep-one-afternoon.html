Carnivore Meal Prep: One Afternoon, Five Days of Food

2026-11-21 by Marcus Cole

This post may contain affiliate links. For educational purposes only, not medical advice. Details

Here's the short answer. Carnivore meal prep for the week takes one afternoon and three foods: one chuck roast, one tray of ground beef patties, and a pot of hard-boiled eggs. Cook all three on Sunday, portion them into five labeled containers, and freeze Friday's. Weeknight dinner becomes a two-minute reheat.

This post is the how. The full day-by-day menu lives in the 30 day carnivore meal plan you can print.

First, a Word on the Numbers

In your first 8 weeks, don't count calories. Eat to satiety.

The 1,500 and 2,000 calorie portions below are a sizing guide. They're for readers past that stage, or anyone who wants a reference. If you want a starting point for your own numbers, the free macro calculator gives you a protein target based on your goal weight.

Two rules shape every container. Protein lands at 30-40 g per meal. And when you want to eat less, you trim the added fat first (butter, tallow), never the meat.

The Shopping List

This covers one person for five days, three meals a day.

  • Chuck roast: 3 lb for the 1,500 week, 3.5 lb for the 2,000 week
  • 80/20 ground beef: 4 lb (makes 10 patties)
  • Eggs: 1 dozen for the 1,500 week, 2.5 dozen for the 2,000 week
  • Butter and salt

Rough cost: ground beef at about $5/lb is $20, chuck at $6-7/lb is $18-21, eggs and butter about $5. That's $43-46, or roughly $9 a day. Solo readers usually land anywhere from $5 to $10 a day depending on cuts and region. My $50/week ground beef protocol sits lower. This one buys you a roast and some variety.

The Sunday Cook, Step by Step

Block out four hours. You're hands-on for about one.

  • 0:00. Heat the oven to 400°F. Weigh the 4 lb of ground beef into 10 balls of about 6.4 oz each. Flatten onto a lined sheet pan and salt.
  • 0:10. Patties in. Bake 15-18 minutes, until the center reads 160°F on a meat thermometer. That's the USDA number for ground beef. An air fryer works too.
  • 0:15. Eggs on. Cover the eggs with cold water, bring to a boil, then turn off the heat and lid the pot for 11 minutes. Move them to ice water.
  • 0:30. Roast in. Pull the patties and drop the oven to 300°F. Salt the chuck well, put it in a lidded Dutch oven with half a cup of water or broth, and cook 3 to 3.5 hours until a fork twists it apart. Prefer the slow cooker? Start it in the morning instead. The times and settings are in my chuck roast slow cooker guide.
  • 4:00. Portion. Let the roast rest 15 minutes, then weigh it. Everything goes into the fridge within 2 hours of coming off the heat.

A note on salt while you're seasoning. Most people on carnivore land around 3-5 g of sodium a day, about 1.5-2 teaspoons of salt, spread across their meals. This is not for everyone. Talk to your doctor first if you have high blood pressure, kidney or heart problems, diabetes, are pregnant, or take medicine.

Raw Weight vs Cooked Weight

Meat shrinks. A chuck roast loses about 30-40% of its raw weight as it braises.

  • 3 lb raw chuck (48 oz) gives you about 29-34 oz cooked.
  • 3.5 lb raw chuck (56 oz) gives you about 34-39 oz cooked.
  • One 6.4 oz raw patty of 80/20 carries about 31 g of protein and about 455 calories raw. Some fat renders into the pan, so call it roughly 400 eaten.
  • 5 oz cooked chuck carries about 38 g of protein and roughly 440 calories. Fattier roasts run higher.
  • One large egg is about 6 g of protein and 75 calories.

Portion the roast by cooked weight. That's what you eat.

The Two Container Sizes

About 1,500 calories a day:

  • Meal 1: 1 patty + 1 egg. About 37 g protein, 475 calories.
  • Meal 2: 5 oz cooked roast. About 38 g protein, 440 calories.
  • Meal 3: 1 patty + 1 egg. About 37 g protein, 475 calories.
  • Plus 1 tablespoon of butter across the day. About 100 calories.

Total: about 112 g protein and 1,490 calories. Five days uses 10 patties, 25 oz of cooked roast and 10 eggs. The 3 lb roast covers it with a few ounces spare.

About 2,000 calories a day:

  • Meal 1: 1 patty + 2 eggs. About 43 g protein, 550 calories.
  • Meal 2: 6 oz cooked roast + 1 tablespoon butter. About 45 g protein, 630 calories.
  • Meal 3: 1 patty + 2 eggs. About 43 g protein, 550 calories.
  • Backup: 2 eggs. About 12 g protein, 150 calories.
  • Plus 1 tablespoon of butter in the pan. About 100 calories.

Total: about 143 g protein and 1,980 calories. Five days uses 10 patties, 30 oz of cooked roast and 30 eggs. Boil two dozen and fry the other six fresh.

Look at what changes between the two. Same patties, nearly the same roast. The gap is mostly butter and eggs. When you want to eat less, the butter goes first. The meat stays.

Storage: The 4-Day Rule

The USDA says cooked meat keeps 3-4 days in the fridge. Cook on Sunday and Thursday is day 4. Friday is day 5, which is past the line.

  • Monday to Thursday: fridge.
  • Friday's container: straight into the freezer on Sunday night.
  • Thursday night: move Friday's container to the fridge to thaw.
  • Hard-boiled eggs: about a week in the fridge, so they make it to Friday fine.
  • Reheat meat to 165°F. Add a spoon of the roast juices so it doesn't dry out.

Why This Works

At 6 pm on a Wednesday, the fridge is either ready or it isn't. A labeled container beats willpower every time.

Run it for one week. On Saturday, note what you didn't finish and adjust next Sunday's list.

Printable Sunday Checklist

  • Buy: chuck roast (3 or 3.5 lb), 4 lb 80/20 ground beef, eggs, butter, salt
  • Oven to 400°F, shape 10 patties of about 6.4 oz raw
  • Bake patties 15-18 min to 160°F inside
  • Boil eggs, lid on 11 min, ice water
  • Oven to 300°F, roast in a lidded pot 3 to 3.5 hours
  • Rest roast 15 min, weigh it cooked
  • Portion 5 or 6 oz cooked roast per day, 2 patties per day
  • Label lids Monday to Friday
  • Freeze Friday's container tonight
  • Thursday night: move Friday to the fridge
