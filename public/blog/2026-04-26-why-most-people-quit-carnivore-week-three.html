Carnivore Week 3 and Ready to Quit? What's Normal

2026-04-26 by Sarah Whitfield

This post may contain affiliate links. For educational purposes only, not medical advice. Details

If you're in week three of carnivore, feeling worse instead of better and ready to quit, you're right on schedule. Week three is when low salt, a body still switching fuel sources and plain old fatigue with the whole thing tend to peak at the same time. For most people it eases within a week or two, so salt your food, eat fattier cuts, stop weighing every day and give it until week six before you decide.

I've watched this pattern for years: friends who said they'd give it 90 days and tapped out at day 19. The energy hasn't kicked in, the scale is being weird, someone at dinner is asking about scurvy, and you feel like garbage. So you quit, often just before things start to turn.

Why week three is the hardest

Salt and water. When carbs drop, insulin drops, and your kidneys let go of more sodium and water. That's the main reason for what people call keto flu: headaches, feeling lightheaded when you stand up, leg cramps at 2am. It's not your body rejecting meat. You're losing salt faster than you're putting it back.

You're between fuel systems. Your body is still getting better at running on fat, and it isn't great at it yet. People describe this as foggy, weak at the gym and a bit weepy. It's common and it passes.

The novelty is gone. By week three you've explained yourself a dozen times, said no to the office pizza, and maybe your spouse is tired of cooking two dinners. The good stuff (steady energy, clearer head, clothes fitting better) hasn't shown up loud enough yet to drown out the noise. So the quiet voice that says "maybe this is dumb" gets louder.

What's normal in week three

  • Tiredness, especially mid-afternoon
  • Brain fog and low motivation
  • Headaches or lightheadedness when you stand
  • Leg cramps, often at night
  • Bathroom habits changing from week to week
  • Sleep that's deep one night and broken the next (here's why week 3 can wreck your sleep)
  • A scale that won't cooperate
  • Being completely sick of ribeye

Not normal, and worth a call to your doctor: chest pain, fainting, a racing or pounding heart that doesn't settle, or feeling worse every single week. If your heart is skipping or fluttering, read my post on heart palpitations on carnivore and get it checked.

A simple map of the first six weeks

Weeks 1 to 2: adjusting. Excitement and a rough patch, sometimes on the same day. Water weight drops for many people, and keto flu often hits around day four or five.

Weeks 2 to 6: the messy middle. The longest stretch and the one most people don't get through. Symptoms come and go. Digestion, sleep and mood can all bounce around. The day-to-day feels chaotic, but the trend usually points somewhere good.

Week 6 and beyond: settled. For most people this is when energy steadies, hunger shows up on a schedule and goes away after meals, and workouts come back. Everyone's timeline is different, but this is when most people stop white-knuckling it.

For the full week-by-week picture, see the carnivore adaptation timeline.

What to actually do this week

Salt your food properly. Most people starting carnivore under-salt. Salt your meat to taste and don't be shy about it. If you're cramping or lightheaded, low salt is the first thing to suspect. I keep LMNT packets in my bag for days I'll be sweating, because it's easier to stay ahead than to catch up. If you take blood pressure medication or have heart or kidney concerns, ask your doctor about salt before you add more.

Eat fattier cuts. If you're living on lean ground beef and chicken breast, you'll feel terrible. Your body needs fat for fuel right now. Ribeye, chuck roast, 80/20 ground beef, pork shoulder, lamb, and eggs cooked in butter.

Eat enough. Under-eating is the quiet reason a lot of people feel awful in week three. Eat until you're full at each meal.

Sleep like it's your job. Go to bed earlier than you think you need to. The fog clears faster when you're rested.

Stop weighing every day. Water shifts make week-three numbers close to useless. Weigh once a week at most, or go by how your clothes fit.

Find one person who gets it. Just one. A friend, a forum, a coach. Someone who won't ask about your kidneys every time you mention dinner. Feeling alone is what ends most attempts in week three, not the food.

Why writing it down helps you push through

Feelings lie in week three. You feel like nothing's working. But if you've jotted down your meals, your salt, your sleep, and a 0 to 10 score for symptoms, energy and mood each day, you have evidence that disagrees with the feeling.

You look back at day 8 and your headache was a 7. Today it's a 3. That's the difference between people who quit and people who keep going. Not willpower. Visibility.

Tracking also helps with "what do I even eat" fatigue. By week three, a lot of people have had ribeye eleven times and are staring into the fridge wondering if eggs are allowed. (They are.) A one-page list of yes, maybe and no foods ends the overthinking.

And if you've already quit once and you're starting over, that's fine too. My post on how to stop restarting at day one is for you.

You're not failing in week three. You're in the hardest stretch of a big change, and it usually looks worse right before it gets better. Keep going.
