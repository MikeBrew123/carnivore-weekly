Warm Drinks on Carnivore When You Don't Drink Coffee

2026-11-28 by Chloe Navarro

This post may contain affiliate links. For educational purposes only, not medical advice. Details

Okay, so it's November, it's dark by 4:30, and you want something warm in your hands that isn't coffee. Here's the short answer to what people drink on carnivore besides water: salted bone broth, plain hot water with a pinch of salt, broth with a spoon of butter or a splash of heavy cream stirred in, and, if you're not strict, plain unsweetened herbal tea. That's the whole list most people actually use, and it covers more cold evenings than you'd think.

Water and sparkling water stay the base. Coffee is fine for plenty of people too, so this isn't an anti-coffee post. It's for everyone who skips it, whether that's a sensitive stomach, a sleep thing, or you just never liked it. A thread on this exact question pulled close to a hundred replies, and the same handful of mugs kept coming up.

Bone broth: the one almost everyone lands on

Real talk: broth is the answer for most people. It's savory, it's warm, it feels like a meal without being one, and it's 100% on plan. Some people sip it mid-afternoon when the "I need a little something" feeling shows up around 3pm. Others keep it for the evening, which is when the cold really gets you.

If you like a richer mug, stir in a spoon of butter or a splash of heavy cream. That adds fat, which counts toward your daily total. If you want a starting point for your numbers, the free macro calculator will give you protein and fat targets, so a creamy mug becomes part of the plan instead of a guess.

Here's where the salt part matters. Store-bought broth can be salty. One cup often carries 800-1,200 mg of sodium. Most people on carnivore land around 3-5 g of sodium a day, spread through the day, because lower insulin makes the kidneys let go of more sodium and water than you're used to. This is not for everyone. Talk to your doctor first if you have high blood pressure, kidney or heart problems, diabetes, are pregnant, or take medicine.

For the full why behind salt on this diet, our post on why you're probably low on electrolytes lays it out.

What to look for on a broth label

Here's the thing about carton broth: not all of it is just bones and water. Flip the box over before you buy.

  • Look for: bones, water, salt, maybe vinegar. Short is good.
  • Skip: anything with sugar, cane juice, dextrose, or "natural flavors" doing a lot of work.
  • Your call: onion, garlic, celery, or herbs. Strict folks skip them, plenty of people don't mind.
  • Gel test: homemade broth that wobbles in the fridge usually means a good, collagen-rich batch.

Homemade is cheaper than you'd think. Some people save every bone from a week of roasts and drop them in a slow cooker on Sunday with water and a splash of vinegar. Ask your butcher for soup bones or knuckle bones, too. At my butcher counter in Whistler they're often a couple of bucks a bag, especially once the tourist rush dies down.

Hot water with salt: the "don't knock it" option

I know. It sounds like the saddest drink on earth. But a mug of hot water with a pinch of good salt is weirdly comforting, and a lot of people swear by it on nights when broth feels like too much.

A small salty drink before bed also comes up a lot for night cramps and that annoying 3am wake-up. Some people find it settles them right down.

My partner Dev works ski patrol, and he comes home from a shift frozen to the core. His mug is a thermos of salted broth, and on the days it runs out, plain hot salted water from the kettle.

Herbal tea: the grey area

Okay, so this is where the community splits. Herbal tea is made from plants, so if you're doing strict carnivore, it's out. Full stop. If you're more relaxed, plain unsweetened herbal tea is something a lot of people keep, the same way they keep salt and black pepper.

The ones that come up most are peppermint, chamomile, ginger, and rooibos. People pick them for flavor and the ritual, not because they do anything special. If you're in the "carnivore-ish" camp, plain herbal tea is usually the first thing people allow back.

The catch is the box. Some blends hide sweeteners, dried fruit, or "dessert" flavoring meant to taste like a treat. If the label reads like a bakery menu, put it back. You want leaves and maybe flowers, nothing else.

Why we don't chase the sweet version

Here's the part people don't love hearing. When a warm drink becomes a reason to bring sweet stuff back in, the craving loop keeps running. Not with a "sugar-free" syrup, not with a sweetened creamer, not with a packet of anything. Swaps keep your brain asking for the thing you're trying to leave behind.

What actually quiets a craving is boring: enough protein at meals, enough fat, enough salt, and time. A lot of people say the evening "I want something" feeling fades within a few weeks once meals are big enough. A mug of salty broth helps a lot more than people expect.

Keeping the warm-mug habit

The good news is you don't have to give up the ritual. The mug, the blanket, the ten quiet minutes before bed: all of that stays. Only what's in the cup changes.

  • Morning: hot water with salt, or broth if you're hungry.
  • Afternoon slump: a cup of broth, maybe with butter.
  • Evening: a small mug of salted broth or plain herbal tea if you allow it.
  • Out and about: a thermos of broth in the car. Gas station coffee is not your only option.

Some people also like to keep broth in ice cube trays in the freezer. Pop two cubes in a mug, add hot water, done. My Sphynx cat Juno, who wears a sweater from October to April, sits next to the kettle every time it clicks on.
