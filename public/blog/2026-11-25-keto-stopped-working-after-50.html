Keto Stopped Working After 50? Why Round Two Is Harder

2026-11-25 by Sarah Whitfield

This post may contain affiliate links. For educational purposes only, not medical advice. Details

A reader wrote to me last month with one line: "Keto worked so well for me at 42. Now I'm 56, I'm doing the exact same thing, and nothing is happening." If that's you, here's the short answer. Keto isn't broken. Your body is different from the one that did round one, and the same plan now asks for different numbers.

Round two is usually slower because your maintenance calories are lower, you've likely lost some muscle, menopause has changed how you store fat and sleep, and a few old habits have crept back in. The fix starts with protein, a fresh calorie target at your current weight, and six to eight weeks of patience before you judge it.

Why the first time felt so easy

Think back to your first week of keto. The scale probably dropped fast. A lot of that was water and stored carbohydrate, and most people see 3-7 lbs leave in that first week. Smaller and older bodies tend to see less.

That drop sets an expectation round two can't match. If you're comparing this week to that week, you're comparing fat loss to water loss.

Four reasons round two is harder

1. Your maintenance number dropped. A smaller body burns less fuel. If you lost weight the first time and kept some of it off, the calories that once created a deficit may now be closer to maintenance.

2. You've likely lost some muscle. Muscle slowly declines with age, and it goes faster when protein is low or we move less. Less muscle means a lower daily burn and less of the tissue that soaks up blood sugar. If round one was low in protein, you may be starting round two with less than you think.

3. Menopause moved the goalposts. As estrogen falls, many women notice fat settling around the middle instead of the hips. Sleep often gets lighter, with 3am wake-ups and night sweats. Poor sleep pushes hunger up and makes cravings louder, and that alone can stall a scale.

4. Old habits came back quietly. The first time, you were strict and curious. This time you "know how to do keto." For a lot of readers, that's when the extra butter in the coffee, the handfuls of nuts, the cheese while cooking, and the packaged "keto" snacks drift back in. Together they can quietly erase a deficit.

What to change first

Here's the order I'd follow, one change at a time, so you can tell what worked.

Step one: make sure it's really a stall. Give it 3-4 weeks with no change before calling it one. Use a tape measure at your waist and hips, not just the scale. A lot of women over 50 lose inches while the scale sits still, especially when they're eating more protein.

Step two: check sleep, stress and salt. These are the quiet ones. Most people on keto and carnivore land around 3-5 g of sodium a day, which is about 1.5-2 teaspoons of salt, spread through the day. Lower insulin makes your kidneys let go of more sodium and water, so most people need more salt than they're used to. This is not for everyone. Talk to your doctor first if you have high blood pressure, kidney or heart problems, diabetes, are pregnant, or take medicine.

Step three: put protein first. This is the biggest change for most round-two readers. Most people aim for 0.8-1.0 g of protein per pound of goal body weight. If your goal is 150 lbs, that's roughly 120-150 g a day.

Spread it out. After 50, 30-40 g per meal tends to work better than one giant dinner. That looks like four or five eggs with leftover beef in the morning, then a good-sized steak or chuck roast plate in the evening. I wrote more about getting there in how much protein you actually need.

Step four: run a 7-day food audit. Write down everything for one week, including cooking fat, cream, cheese and the bites while you cook. Protein is the anchor and fat is the lever, so when you trim, trim the added fat first, never the protein.

Step five: recalculate at your current weight. The numbers that worked at 42 were built for a different body. Run them again with today's weight and today's activity. If you were a keto reader and you're curious about going meat-only, here's how the two compare: carnivore vs keto.

If all of that is steady and you're still stuck, the next levers are a 2-week break from dairy or a deliberate week at maintenance. Those come last on purpose.

Patience, with a real number

Here's the part nobody wants to hear. Over 50, losing 0.5-1 lb a week is a good pace. That's 3-8 lbs over eight weeks, which can feel like nothing after a 10-pound first month in your 40s.

But it's real fat. Give the changes above six to eight weeks before you decide anything. Most people feel properly settled in by weeks 4-6, and a fair judgment comes at 60-90 days.

When I lost weight on meat-based eating, it came off steadily rather than dramatically. What I noticed first wasn't the scale. It was my jeans, and not thinking about food all afternoon.

If you take medicine

Eating this way can change blood sugar and blood pressure readings, sometimes within days. Any change to your medicine is your prescriber's decision. Your job is to keep track of your readings and bring them to your appointment.

For the hormone side of things, KetoDial has a helpful piece on what changes on keto during perimenopause.

See a doctor soon if...

  • You're losing weight without trying, or much faster than the pace above.
  • You feel dizzy, faint, or have a racing or pounding heart.
  • You're exhausted all the time, cold all the time, or losing hair in clumps.
  • You have new bleeding after menopause.

The bottom line

Keto not working anymore usually means the plan needs updating, not abandoning. Confirm the stall, fix sleep and salt, lead with protein, audit the extras, and recalculate for the body you have now. Then give it time.

I'm not a doctor. I've researched this deeply and spent years listening to readers work through it, but I'm not your doctor. Everything I write is educational, based on research and what I've seen work. Your situation might be different.
