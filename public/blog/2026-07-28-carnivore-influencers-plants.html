Why Carnivore Influencers Are Quietly Adding Plants

2026-07-28 by Chloe Navarro

This post may contain affiliate links. For educational purposes only — not medical advice. Details

Okay, so your feed did the thing again

You open Instagram and there's a carnivore creator you've followed for two years holding up a bowl of berries. Or talking about honey. Or casually mentioning they've been eating white rice after workouts now.

And your stomach drops a little. Real talk: if strict meat-only was the answer, why is this person you trusted quietly walking it back?

I've watched this play out in the community for a while now, and I want to talk about it honestly. Not defensively. Not with the "they were never REAL carnivore" thing that shows up in every comment section. Just what's actually happening and what it means for you, which is probably less than the internet wants you to think.

Who's actually doing this

The most famous example is Paul Saladino. This is a guy who literally branded himself "Carnivore MD" and spent years telling people plants were basically trying to poison them. He went all-in on strict carnivore around 2018.

Then he changed course. He's publicly said he ran into health issues on long-term strict carnivore, started adding fruit and honey, and has since said fruit makes up the majority of his diet now. That's not me putting words in his mouth. He's said it out loud, repeatedly, on his own channels.

Chris Williamson is another one people bring up. He's talked openly about stopping carnivore because of what it did to his cholesterol numbers. Again, his words, his reasoning, publicly shared.

Beyond those two, there's a wider drift. A handful of big names in the keto and carnivore space have quietly added starches, fruit, or dairy-heavy stuff back in and said they feel better for it. I'm keeping those general on purpose, because half the "so-and-so quit carnivore" headlines are exaggerated and I'm not going to be the person who misquotes someone for clicks.

What the community is actually saying

Here's the thing. The reaction splits into about three camps, and you've seen all of them.

  • The betrayed camp: "I built my whole life around this and now you're eating mangoes?" Genuine hurt, sometimes anger.
  • The told-you-so camp: mostly people who never did carnivore, showing up to spike the football.
  • The shrug camp: "Cool, their body, their call, I still feel great, moving on."

I get why the first group feels how they feel. When someone's content helped you fix your energy or your gut or your relationship with food, watching them change feels personal. Like the ground moved.

But I met a creator at a health conference last year who put it in a way that stuck with me. She said the mistake isn't changing your diet. The mistake is pretending your current diet is the final answer for every human forever. Bodies change. N of 1 stays N of 1.

Why someone adds food back (and why it's not a scandal)

People reintroduce plants or carbs for boring, human reasons. Their cholesterol went somewhere they didn't like. Their sleep got worse. Their training suffered. They got bored and wanted a strawberry. All valid.

Strict carnivore is a powerful tool, especially as an elimination protocol. You strip everything back, calm things down, then some people slowly test foods and find their actual line. For a lot of folks, that line isn't zero plants. It's low. Or it's meat-based with a few things added back.

That's not failure. That's the process working the way it's supposed to. Sarah broke down the science of why bloodwork and cholesterol shift so much on this diet, and if the numbers stuff is stressing you out, go read her take, because that's her lane and she's better at it than me.

The part nobody wants to hear

One person walking it back does not mean the diet failed you.

I know that sounds obvious written down, but the way people react, you'd think their own results evaporated the second a creator ate an apple. Your energy is still your energy. Your gut is still calmer. Your joint pain didn't come back because a YouTuber posted a fruit bowl.

The community has a bad habit of treating this like team sports. Like every person who adds food back is a defector and every person who stays strict is a loyalist. It's food. You're allowed to just eat in a way that works for your body and let other people do the same.

And honestly? Some people genuinely thrive on strict carnivore for years. I know several. Great bloodwork, great mood, zero desire to add anything. That's real too. Both of these things are true at the same time, and the internet hates that because nuance doesn't trend.

So what do you actually do

Nothing, unless your own body is telling you to. Not because a creator changed. That's the whole point.

If you're thriving strict, keep going. If you've been white-knuckling it, sleeping badly, and secretly miss fruit, maybe that's worth a careful experiment. Pay attention to how you feel, not how your favorite influencer feels.

And whatever you do, don't skip your minerals when you're adjusting anything. Half the "carnivore made me feel terrible" stories in the community are just people who forgot they need way more sodium and electrolytes than they used to. That's the one thing basically everyone agrees on, whether they eat plants or not.

The creators changing their diets aren't a warning sign. They're just people, in public, doing the same figuring-it-out you're doing in private. Let them. Then get back to your own plate.

I'm not a doctor. I'm just someone who's deep in this community and reads everything. I can tell you what people are trying and what's trending, but your health calls are yours to make. If you're on medication or managing a diagnosed condition, loop in an actual healthcare provider before you change how you eat.
