They Lost 15 Pounds in a Month. You Lost 6. Here's Why

2026-11-01 by Chloe Navarro

This post may contain affiliate links. For educational purposes only, not medical advice. Details

Okay, so you know the kind of post that shows up in your feed. A guy in his mid-thirties, 5'9", started at 260 pounds, and he's down 15 in his first month on carnivore. He mentions he did this diet almost ten years ago, lost 30 pounds in five months, fell off, and he's posting now to encourage anyone who's struggled. Genuinely nice post.

Then you scroll to the replies, and there you are. A woman in her 50s or 60s, down 4 to 6 pounds after a month, doing everything right, asking what's wrong with her. Nothing is wrong with her. She's measuring herself against a body that has almost nothing in common with hers.

What "average first-month weight loss" actually means

Here's the honest answer. There isn't one average, because month one is really two different things glued together. Week one is mostly water and glycogen, typically 3 to 7 pounds, and less for smaller or older bodies. After that, fat loss settles in at 0.5 to 1.5 pounds a week, and for most people over 50, 0.5 to 1 pound a week is a good, sustainable pace.

Add that up for a woman over 50 and a realistic first month lands somewhere around 4 to 8 pounds. Six is smack in the middle. Not behind. Not broken. Right where the math says you'd be. This is not for everyone. Talk to your doctor first if you have high blood pressure, kidney or heart problems, diabetes, are pregnant, or take medicine.

If you want a starting point for your own numbers instead of borrowing someone else's, the free calculator at /calculator.html works from your goal weight and your own stats, which is this whole article in tool form.

What the 15-pound post leaves out

Nobody's lying in these posts. They just skip the parts that explain the number.

  • Starting weight. A 260-pound body carries far more stored glycogen and water than a 165-pound body, so the week-one drop is bigger before anyone has lost an ounce of fat. Sarah broke this down in 8 Pounds Gone in a Week? That's Mostly Water, so I won't repeat it. Short version: his first week could easily have been 8 to 10 pounds of water on its own.
  • Sex. Men carry more muscle, which burns more at rest, and they tend to drop water faster. I wrote a whole piece on this when readers kept asking why their husband loses weight faster. Same kitchen, same food, different engine.
  • Age. At 36, his resting metabolism, his muscle mass and his hormones are all working with him. Past menopause, the body is thriftier and holds fat around the middle more stubbornly. That's biology, not a character flaw.
  • History. He's done this before. He already knows what to buy, how to cook it and what days 3 to 10 feel like. That's a head start, not a miracle.
  • What week one hid. If 8 to 10 of his 15 pounds were water, his actual fat loss was maybe 5 to 7 pounds. Suddenly his month and yours are a lot closer than the headline suggests.

What your first month realistically looks like

Say you're 58, 5'4", starting at 175. Week one, you might drop 3 or 4 pounds of water, maybe less if you were already eating fairly low carb. Sarah covered the small week-one drop in Only Down 3 Pounds in Week One? Why That's Normal. Weeks two through four, you lose a half pound to a pound each week while your appetite calms down.

Month-one total: somewhere between 4 and 7 pounds. Which is exactly the number women keep apologizing for in the comments.

And here's the thing nobody says in those threads. His pace slows down, too. By month three, the water is long gone and he's losing at the same steady rate everyone else is. After month one, everyone's chart flattens into the same slow, boring, effective line.

Why 6 pounds is a genuine win

Real talk: 6 pounds in a month, at 58, without hunger, without counting anything, is the pace that actually stays off. The people posting giant first months often have a second post six months later about falling off. Our friend at 260 said as much about his own first go-round.

The half-pound-to-a-pound-a-week pace is the one your body doesn't fight. It's the pace where you keep your muscle and somewhere around week six you notice you're not thinking about food at 3pm anymore. Around here we judge the diet at 60 to 90 days, not at 30, because the first month is the noisiest month you'll ever have on this way of eating.

What to measure besides the scale

In month one the scale is the worst instrument you own. Pick two or three of these and write them down on day one.

  • Waist at the belly button. Soft tape, relaxed, same time of morning. Women over 50 often lose inches here before the scale moves.
  • Rings and watch band. If your rings spin by week two, that's water and puffiness leaving. It counts.
  • Sleep. Expect a rough patch in weeks two and three, then notice if you're waking less.
  • Afternoon hunger. The 3pm raid on the pantry disappearing is, honestly, the biggest change most readers report in month one. The scale can't see it.
  • One pair of pants. Not the comfy ones. Try them on every two weeks.

How to stop comparing yourself to a stranger's screenshot

I'm not going to tell you to stop reading those posts. I read them. But try this: when a big number shows up, before you feel anything about it, ask three questions. How much did they start at? Are they a man under 45? Is this their first time? If the answers are "a lot, yes, no," you've just learned that post is about a different sport.

Then look at your own numbers from day one. Compare yourself to you, four weeks ago. That woman was hungry every afternoon and her rings were tight. You're 6 pounds lighter and you're not. That's the post worth writing.

And if you're at week four with 4 to 6 pounds gone and you're doing everything right, I'll say it plainly: nothing is wrong with you. You're on schedule. Keep going, and check back in at day 90.

I'm not a doctor. I'm just someone who's deep in the community and reads everything. Take all health stuff with a grain of salt (pun intended). I can tell you what people are trying and what's trending, but you gotta make your own calls. I'm here to give you the real tea, not medical advice.
