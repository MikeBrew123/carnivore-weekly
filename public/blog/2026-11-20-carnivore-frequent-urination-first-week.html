Peeing All Night on Carnivore? Why Week One Does It

2026-11-20 by Sarah Whitfield

This post may contain affiliate links. For educational purposes only, not medical advice. Details

You're four days into carnivore and you've been up to the bathroom three times since midnight. During the day it's not much better. You drink a glass of water and twenty minutes later you're hunting for a restroom again.

Here's the short answer. Frequent urination in the first week or two of carnivore is usually your body letting go of stored water. When you cut carbs, you burn through the sugar stored in your muscles and liver, and that sugar was holding a lot of water. Lower insulin also tells your kidneys to release more sodium, and water follows sodium out. For most people it eases off within two to four weeks. Below is why it happens, what helps, and the few signs that mean you should call your doctor instead of waiting it out.

Where all that water is coming from

Your body stores carbohydrate as glycogen, and each gram of glycogen holds roughly 3 grams of water. Once you stop eating carbs, your body uses that glycogen up over the first few days, and the water it was holding leaves through your kidneys.

The second piece is insulin. One of its quieter jobs is telling your kidneys to hang on to sodium. When insulin drops, your kidneys let go of more sodium, and water goes with it. That's why the scale often shows a quick 3 to 7 pound drop in week one, a bit less for smaller or older bodies. I covered that in why the first week's weight loss is mostly water. The bathroom trips are the other half of the same story.

How long does it usually last?

The roughest stretch for most people is somewhere around days 3 to 10. That's when glycogen runs low and the sodium shift is strongest. Most people notice things settling by weeks 2 to 4.

Your situation might be different. Some people barely notice it, and others feel it longer. What matters most is the direction. If it's a little better each week, that's the pattern you want.

What helps: salt, timing, and coffee

Here's what I've seen work. None of it is complicated.

  • Spread your salt through the day. Because your kidneys are letting go of more sodium, most people need more salt than they're used to early on. Most people on carnivore land around 3 to 5 grams of sodium a day, about 1.5 to 2 teaspoons of salt, spread across meals. This is not for everyone. Talk to your doctor first if you have high blood pressure, kidney or heart problems, diabetes, are pregnant, or take medicine.
  • Front-load your water. Drink most of your fluids in the morning and early afternoon, then taper off in the two or three hours before bed. Same amount, better timing.
  • A small salty drink in the evening is fine. A mug of bone broth or a pinch of salt in a small glass of water can help your body hold fluid overnight instead of sending it straight through. A cup of broth can carry 800 to 1,200 mg of sodium, so it counts toward your day.
  • Cut coffee after noon. Coffee is fine on carnivore, but caffeine nudges your kidneys and can irritate the bladder. An afternoon cup can mean an extra trip at 2 a.m.

If you're waking at night but don't really need to go, that's likely a different issue. My post on waking up at 3am on carnivore covers that side of it. And if the first week also brought headaches or that heavy, flu-ish feeling, KetoDial's guide to the electrolyte side of keto flu explains the same water shift.

Why this can feel bigger after 50

A lot of readers in their 50s and 60s tell me the bathroom trips hit harder than they expected. There are real reasons for that.

Bladders change with age. They can hold a little less, and the signal to go comes sooner and feels more urgent. After menopause, lower estrogen affects the tissue of the bladder and urethra, which can mean more urgency and more night trips. So the water shift lands on a bladder that's already working differently. That doesn't mean anything is wrong. It means the first weeks can feel louder.

Water pills, or diuretics, are the other big one. They already make you lose sodium and water, so starting carnivore on top of them stacks two effects in the same direction. Please don't adjust the pill yourself. Any change to your medicine is your prescriber's decision. More on that in salt on carnivore when you take blood pressure pills.

When it's not the diet: see a doctor if...

Peeing more in week one is common. But some symptoms point to something else, like an infection or high blood sugar. Don't chalk these up to adaptation. See your doctor promptly, or get urgent care, if you notice:

  • Burning or pain when you pee, or a constant urge with only a little coming out.
  • Blood in your urine, or urine that looks pink, red, or cola-colored.
  • Fever, chills, or pain in your back or side, which can mean an infection has reached the kidneys.
  • Thirst that won't quit along with peeing a lot. Feeling parched no matter how much you drink can be a sign of high blood sugar.
  • Confusion, or feeling dizzy or faint when you stand up. That can mean you're low on fluid or salt, and it raises the risk of a fall.

In older adults, a urinary infection doesn't always show up as burning. Sometimes the first sign is new confusion or just feeling "off."

The bottom line

Peeing all night in your first weeks of carnivore is usually glycogen water and sodium leaving as insulin drops. It tends to peak around days 3 to 10 and settle by weeks 2 to 4. Spread your salt, front-load your water, keep evening drinks small and salty, and skip coffee after noon. Burning, blood, fever, constant thirst or dizziness isn't adaptation. That's a call to your doctor.

I'm not a doctor. I've researched this deeply and heard from a lot of readers going through it, but I'm not your doctor. Everything I write is educational, based on research and what I've seen work. Your situation might be different.
