Carnivore Meatloaf Without Breadcrumbs That Holds

2026-10-25 by Marcus Cole

This post may contain affiliate links. For educational purposes only, not medical advice. Details

You don't need breadcrumbs to make a meatloaf that holds. Eggs do most of the binding. Crushed pork rinds or grated parmesan soak up the juices that breadcrumbs used to handle. And the step that matters most isn't an ingredient at all. It's letting the loaf rest 10 minutes before you slice it.

Here's the protocol: 2 pounds of 80/20 ground beef, 3 eggs, 1 ounce of crushed pork rinds (or half a cup of parmesan), salt. Bake it, rest it, cut 10 slices. That's five easy lunches at 2 slices each, and each lunch lands right around 39 g of protein.

Ground beef and eggs are our default beginner food. This loaf is the same two foods in a format you can slice, pack, and forget about until noon.

How Much Protein Is in a Slice?

The math doesn't lie, so let's run it. 80/20 ground beef has roughly 77 g of protein and 1,150 calories per raw pound.

  • 2 lb 80/20 ground beef: about 154 g protein, 2,300 kcal
  • 3 large eggs: about 18 g protein, 210 kcal
  • 1 oz crushed pork rinds: about 17 g protein, 155 kcal
  • 1 tbsp unflavoured gelatin (optional): about 6 g protein, 25 kcal

Total: about 195 g of protein. Cut into 10 even slices, that's roughly 19-20 g of protein per slice. Calories land near 250 per slice, a bit less after fat renders out. Swap in half a cup of grated parmesan for the rinds and the numbers barely move.

We aim for 30-40 g of protein per meal, especially for readers over 50. Two slices hits about 39 g. If you're a smaller eater, 1.5 slices still gets you near 30 g. If you want a starting point for your numbers, the free macro calculator tells you your daily protein, and from there you'll know how much your lunch slice needs to cover.

This is not for everyone. Talk to your doctor first if you have high blood pressure, kidney or heart problems, diabetes, are pregnant, or take medicine.

The Ingredients

  • 2 lb ground beef, 80/20 (or 75/25). Don't go leaner.
  • 3 large eggs, or 2 eggs plus 1 extra yolk for a softer loaf
  • 1 oz pork rinds, crushed fine (about 1 cup crushed), OR half a cup finely grated parmesan
  • 2 tsp salt
  • Optional: 1 tbsp unflavoured beef gelatin plus 3 tbsp cold water or bone broth
  • Optional: swap half a pound of the beef for ground pork for a more tender loaf

Keto recipes often call for oat fibre, almond flour, or flax. Those are keto binders, not carnivore ones. Strict carnivore skips them, and honestly, you don't need them.

Why Meatloaf Crumbles (and How to Stop It)

Crumbly loaves usually fail for one of four reasons.

  • You sliced it hot. Give it 10 minutes and the juices settle back in.
  • You overmixed it. Working the meat too hard makes it dense and dry. Mix with your hands only until it comes together.
  • The meat was too lean. 90/10 dries out and falls apart. Fat holds moisture. On carnivore, fattier meat, a bit of pork, or an extra yolk does the job that onions and mushrooms do in a keto loaf.
  • You lifted it out too soon. Leave it in the pan until it has rested. Then pour off the fat and lift.

If 1 pound of beef with 2 eggs still crumbles on you, it's usually a resting problem, not an egg problem.

The Gelatin Trick

This one's optional but it works. Sprinkle 1 tbsp of unflavoured gelatin over 3 tbsp of cold water or broth. Let it sit 5 minutes until it turns spongy. Stir it into the eggs before they go in the meat.

Gelatin sets as the loaf cools. That means cleaner slices straight from the fridge, which is exactly what you want for packed lunches. It's an animal product, so it fits carnivore.

The Steps

  • Heat the oven to 350°F. Use a 9x5 loaf pan, or a rimmed sheet pan if you'd like fat to drain away.
  • Whisk the eggs with the salt (and bloomed gelatin, if using).
  • Add the crushed pork rinds or parmesan to the eggs. Let it sit 2-3 minutes so the crumbs soak up the liquid.
  • Add the ground beef. Mix by hand just until combined. Stop there.
  • Press it into the pan, or shape a loaf about 9 inches long on the sheet pan.
  • Bake 60-75 minutes, until the center reads 160°F on a meat thermometer.
  • Rest 10 minutes in the pan. Don't skip this.
  • Pour off the fat, lift it out, and cut 10 even slices. A slice about three-quarters of an inch thick is close.

Turning It Into a Week of Lunches

Next, you do this. Let the loaf cool completely. Pack 2 slices per container. Five containers, done.

  • Fridge: keep 3 days' worth, eaten within 3-4 days of cooking.
  • Freezer: wrap the last 2 lunches and freeze them. Move one to the fridge the night before.
  • Reheat: a covered skillet on medium-low with a splash of water keeps it moist. Many people just eat it cold.

Cooking for two? Double it in two pans.

Cost check: 2 lb of ground beef, 3 eggs, and a small bag of pork rinds run roughly $13-15 in most areas. That's under $3 per lunch, which leaves plenty of room inside a $5-10 daily food budget. For more on stretching ground beef, see our ground beef budget strategy.

Optional: Hide Some Liver in It

Meatloaf is the easiest place to sneak in liver. Blend 4 oz of raw beef liver smooth and mix it in with the eggs. That's under 1 oz per lunch, and most people can't taste it.

Count it toward your weekly limit. We cap liver at 4-8 oz per week, total. If this loaf carries 4 oz, you've got room for at most 4 more ounces from anything else that week. Don't double the liver just because you can't taste it.

If Your Weight Loss Stalls

Protein is the anchor. Fat is the lever. If the scale and tape haven't moved in 3-4 weeks, don't cut the meatloaf. Cut the added fat around it first. Pour off the drippings and check how much butter and cheese is creeping in. Then use our guide to how much protein you actually need to recheck your target.

This is why it works. Lunch is decided before Monday starts.
