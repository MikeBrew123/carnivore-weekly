Frequently Asked Questions

What is the carnivore diet?

The carnivore diet is a meat-based eating approach popularized by Dr. Shawn Baker, author of "The Carnivore Diet," and Dr. Paul Saladino, who wrote "The Carnivore Code." You eat animal products only: beef, pork, chicken, fish, eggs, and some dairy. No vegetables, fruits, or grains. Many carnivore followers eat nose-to-tail, including organ meats like liver for maximum nutrients.

How much protein do I need on carnivore?

Dr. Donald Layman's research supports roughly 2g of protein per kilogram of body weight (about 0.9g per pound) as a strong baseline for most adults. Fat loss? You'll need that protein to preserve muscle while cutting calories. Maintenance? The same baseline keeps you covered. Most people undereat protein by 20-30g daily. Apps like Cronometer can help you track if you're hitting your target. Our calculator figures out your personalized number in 90 seconds.

How do I calculate carnivore macros?

The standard approach uses the Mifflin-St Jeor equation to estimate your base metabolism, then adjusts for activity. Protein is set at roughly 2g per kilogram of your body weight. Fat fills the remaining calories. Your goal (fat loss, maintenance, or muscle gain) determines the calorie target, which shifts how much fat you eat. Our free calculator handles this math automatically. If you want to track manually, Cronometer makes it simple.

Do I need to track calories on carnivore?

Not always. The r/carnivore community on Reddit shows many people thrive eating to satiety without tracking. But tracking helps if you've stalled, want muscle gain, or feel exhausted. Apps like Cronometer or MyFitnessPal make it painless. I'd say: try intuitive eating for 30 days first. If results stall, knowing your numbers removes the guesswork.

What's the difference between carnivore and keto?

Keto allows 20-50g carbs from vegetables, nuts, and low-carb foods. Carnivore eliminates all plants. Dr. Ken Berry, a popular keto advocate, often discusses both approaches. Dr. Shawn Baker focuses strictly on carnivore. Both achieve ketosis, but carnivore is simpler: just meat, eggs, and animal products. Carnivore also tends to prioritize higher protein than traditional keto.

Can I use this calculator for keto or low-carb diets?

Absolutely. This works for keto, carnivore, pescatarian, or what Dr. Paul Saladino calls "animal-based" eating. The protein math is the same across all diets: roughly 2g per kilogram of body weight. What changes is the food list and carb allowance. Carnivore keeps carbs at zero. Keto allows 20-50g from low-carb vegetables. The calculator adjusts automatically based on which diet you select.

How many calories should I eat on carnivore?

It depends on your goal, but most people start near maintenance and adjust from there. Fat loss calls for a moderate deficit, muscle gain a moderate surplus, and maintenance holds steady. Because carnivore meals run high in protein and fat, appetite tends to self-regulate more than on a mixed diet, which is why a lot of people find calories easier to manage here. Our calculator sets your personalized calorie target based on your weight, activity level, and goal in under a minute.

What fat to protein ratio should I use on carnivore?

There's no single ratio that fits everyone. Most carnivore eaters land somewhere between 1:1 and 2:1 fat to protein by weight, and where you fall depends on your goal. Fat loss usually means leaning toward more protein relative to fat, while maintenance and muscle gain can carry more fat. A flat ratio you saw in a forum post rarely matches your body.
