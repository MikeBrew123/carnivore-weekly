What the Community Is Asking This Week
“I lost a lot of weight on carnivore, my blood sugar was doing great, and now a year and a half in my fasting glucose is creeping back up. I didn't change anything. What's happening?”
This one genuinely needs a conversation with your doctor, not a forum. That said, many long-term carnivore members find that a rise in fasting glucose after significant weight loss can reflect a few different things, including a normal physiological adaptation some call 'glucose sparing,' where your body preserves glucose for the tissues that need it. It's also worth knowing that stopping a medication like metformin changes the picture significantly. Bring the full timeline to your doctor, including when you stopped the medication, and ask them to look at fasting insulin alongside glucose.
“I'm worried that eating high-calorie foods like bacon, eggs, and ribeye will make it impossible to lose weight. How does anyone actually succeed on this eating enough fat?”
It's one of the most common sticking points people bring up, and it makes total sense if you've been counting calories for years. Many people find that the hunger-regulating effect of protein and fat means they naturally eat less overall, without tracking every bite. The comparison to low-fat diets breaks down pretty fast once you're a few weeks in and realizing you're actually satisfied between meals.
“My skin has been a disaster for years. Could changing what I eat actually make a difference?”
Community members report this more than almost anything else, and it's one of the most motivating things to see. The timeline varies a lot, but many people share that skin changes, including acne clearing up, tend to show up somewhere in the first three to six months. Nobody can promise a specific outcome, but it's worth knowing you're not the only one who's experienced this, not by a long shot.
Try This Week

Track your protein grams for just three days, not calories, just protein. Marcus makes the case that most stalls come down to a fat-to-protein ratio that quietly drifted in the wrong direction. Pull out whatever you're eating on a typical day, look up the protein number, and see if it's actually where you think it is. Three days of data tells you more than a month of guessing.
