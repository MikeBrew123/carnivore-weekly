What the Community Is Asking This Week
“I quit low-carb a few months ago and felt terrible the whole time. I went back to it recently and felt better almost immediately. Is that real or am I just imagining it?”
You're probably not imagining it. Many people find that mood, energy, and mental clarity shift noticeably when they return to low-carb eating, sometimes within hours of getting back into ketosis. The brain runs differently on fat than on glucose, and for some people that difference is really felt. It's worth paying attention to and sticking with it long enough to see if the pattern holds.
“I'm in my 50s with blood sugar issues and I'm wondering if I need to cut calories too, or if just changing what I eat is enough.”
This is one of the most common questions we get from women in perimenopause, and the honest answer is that it depends on your body and your goals. Many people find that eating more protein and fat naturally reduces how much they eat overall, without counting a thing. If you're curious about the calorie side of things, Marcus's piece this week on TDEE after 45 is a great place to start.
“Someone in my family was told they'd have type 2 diabetes forever, but I keep hearing that people are reversing it with diet. Is that actually possible?”
It's a conversation worth having with a doctor, but yes, many people have seen dramatic improvements in blood sugar through low-carb and ketogenic eating. Community members share stories of this regularly, and some research supports it as well. The key word is 'reversal' versus 'cure,' and working with a doctor who will monitor your numbers along the way is really important.
Try This Week

Check your blood pressure at home, twice. Take a reading in the morning before coffee and again in the evening after dinner, then write both numbers down.
