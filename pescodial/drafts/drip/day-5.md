Subject: Mercury, made simple
Preheader: Which fish to eat often, which to limit, and the pregnancy guideline.

Hi,

Mercury worries stop a lot of people from eating fish at all. The good news is that the FDA and EPA have done the sorting for us. Their advice puts common fish into three groups.

**Best choices: 2 to 3 servings a week.** These are lower in mercury. The list includes:

- Salmon, sardines, anchovies, herring, trout
- Atlantic mackerel and Pacific chub mackerel
- Cod, haddock, pollock, tilapia, flounder, sole, catfish, whiting
- Shrimp, scallops, clams, oysters, crab, lobster, squid
- Canned light tuna, including skipjack

**Good choices: 1 serving a week.** These carry a bit more:

- Albacore (white) tuna, canned or fresh, and yellowfin tuna
- Halibut, mahi mahi, snapper, grouper, Chilean sea bass
- Spanish mackerel

**Choices to avoid:** king mackerel, marlin, orange roughy, shark, swordfish, bigeye tuna and tilefish from the Gulf of Mexico.

An adult serving in the FDA/EPA advice is 4 ounces, measured before cooking. The full chart has more species and is worth printing: https://www.fda.gov/fishadvice

**Notice the mackerel trap.** Atlantic mackerel is a best choice, Spanish mackerel is a good choice, and king mackerel is on the avoid list. Same word, very different fish. Check the label.

**If you're pregnant, could become pregnant, or are breastfeeding:** the FDA and EPA advise 8 to 12 ounces of fish a week, which is 2 to 3 servings, from the best choices list. If you pick from the good choices list, have just one serving that week and no other fish. Children need smaller servings, and the FDA chart lists them by age. If you're feeding grandkids, that chart is handy too.

**Fish you or a friend caught?** Check your local or state fish advisory first. If there isn't one, the FDA advises eating just one serving that week and no other fish.

**By style:**

- **Pescatarian Mediterranean, Keto or Low Carb:** build most of your fish meals from the best choices list, and keep good choices to the occasional meal.
- **Pescatarian Carnivore:** you'll likely eat fish more often than the advice was written for, so build most meals from the best choices list, rotate species, and skip the avoid list entirely. Ask your doctor whether it makes sense to check anything in your bloodwork.

If you take a blood thinner, blood pressure medicine or diabetes medicine, talk to your doctor before changing your diet. Your calculator numbers can help that conversation: https://www.pescodial.com/calculator/
