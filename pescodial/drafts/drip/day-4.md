Subject: Shrimp, eggs and cholesterol
Preheader: What the evidence actually says, in plain words.

Hi,

If you've ever put shrimp back in the freezer case because of your cholesterol, this one's for you.

**The short version:** for most people, the cholesterol in food has a smaller effect on blood cholesterol than we used to think. What you eat alongside it, and your overall pattern, tends to matter more.

**What changed?** For years, US guidelines set a daily limit for dietary cholesterol. The 2015-2020 edition dropped that specific number. The 2020-2025 edition still said to keep cholesterol intake as low as possible while eating a healthy pattern, so this isn't a free pass. It's a shift in focus from single foods to the whole plate.

**Eggs.** In 2019 the American Heart Association reviewed the evidence on dietary cholesterol. Their advice: healthy people can include up to one whole egg a day as part of a heart-healthy pattern, and older adults with normal cholesterol can include up to two a day. They also noted that people with high cholesterol, especially those with diabetes or at risk of heart failure, should be more careful. That's a conversation for your doctor.

**Shrimp.** Shrimp carries more cholesterol than most seafood, but it's very low in saturated fat, and it's on the FDA/EPA "best choices" list for low mercury. How it's cooked makes a big difference. Grilled or sautéed in olive oil is a very different meal from battered and deep-fried.

**People respond differently.** Some people's blood cholesterol rises more than others when they eat more cholesterol. The only way to know which camp you're in is bloodwork. If you're changing how you eat, ask your doctor whether a lipid panel before and a few months after makes sense for you.

**By style:**

- **Pescatarian Mediterranean:** eggs a few times a week, shrimp now and then, fish most other days. Easy.
- **Pescatarian Keto, Low Carb or Carnivore:** eggs often become a daily staple, and sometimes several a day. That's exactly when a before-and-after lipid panel with your doctor is worth asking about.

**Please talk to your doctor first** if you're on a cholesterol medicine, a blood thinner, blood pressure medicine or diabetes medicine. Don't stop or change any medication because of something you read here.
