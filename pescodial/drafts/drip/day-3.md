Subject: A simple weekly fish plan
Preheader: How many fish meals to aim for, and which fish to start with.

Hi,

Let's make this easy. Here's a weekly fish plan you can start with on Monday.

**How often?** The American Heart Association suggests eating fish, especially oily fish, at least twice a week. Their serving is about 3.5 ounces cooked, or about three-quarters of a cup of flaked fish. The 2020-2025 US Dietary Guidelines put it as at least 8 ounces of seafood a week for most adults. Think of these as a floor, not a ceiling.

**A starting week for Pescatarian Mediterranean:**

- **2 oily fish dinners.** Salmon, sardines, trout, herring or Atlantic mackerel.
- **1 or 2 mild white fish dinners.** Cod, haddock, pollock or tilapia. Good if you're not sure you like "fishy" fish yet.
- **1 shellfish meal.** Shrimp, scallops, mussels or clams.
- **The other nights:** eggs, beans, lentils or chickpeas, with plenty of vegetables and olive oil.

That's three or four fish meals, which lands comfortably above the twice-a-week starting point. Your calculator protein number tells you how much to put on the plate. If you're under it most days, add a little more fish or an egg at breakfast.

**If you chose another style:**

- **Pescatarian Keto or Low Carb:** same fish, swap the bean nights for more eggs and seafood, and lean on the oily fish for fat.
- **Pescatarian Carnivore:** fish and seafood show up at most meals, so variety matters more. Rotate species through the week rather than eating the same one every day. Day 5's email on mercury will help you choose.

**Three tips that make it stick:**

1. **Buy frozen.** Frozen fillets are often cheaper, they don't spoil by Thursday, and they cook from frozen in the oven.
2. **Pick one "home base" fish.** Something you like and can cook without thinking. Build from there.
3. **Cook once, eat twice.** Bake an extra salmon fillet and flake it into a salad or onto eggs the next day.

Not sure how much to put on your plate? Your numbers are here: https://www.pescodial.com/calculator/

If you take a blood thinner, blood pressure medicine or diabetes medicine, check with your doctor before making big changes to how you eat.
