Subject: Olive oil and the Mediterranean plate
Preheader: How to turn your fat number into something you can pour.

Hi,

One week in. Today is about the ingredient that does the quiet work in Pescatarian Mediterranean: olive oil.

**Why olive oil?** The best-known trial here is PREDIMED, run in Spain with older adults at high risk of heart disease. People eating a Mediterranean diet with extra olive oil, or with extra nuts, had fewer major heart events, like heart attack and stroke, than people told to follow a lower-fat diet. One trial doesn't settle everything, and the people in it were at high risk to begin with, but it's a big reason olive oil sits at the centre of this style.

**Turning your fat number into spoons.** Your calculator gives you a daily fat target in grams. A tablespoon of olive oil has roughly 14 grams of fat. So if you know your number, you can work out roughly how much of it olive oil covers, and let fish, nuts and seeds fill in the rest. No need to be exact. Most people find a measuring spoon by the stove for one week teaches them what "enough" looks like.

**Building the plate.** Here's how I'd sketch a Pescatarian Mediterranean dinner:

- About half the plate: vegetables, cooked or raw
- A palm-sized piece of fish or seafood, sized to your protein number
- A smaller scoop of beans, lentils or a whole grain like farro or brown rice
- Olive oil to cook with and a drizzle to finish, plus lemon and herbs

Baked salmon on a tray with peppers, onions and chickpeas, all tossed in olive oil, is one pan and about half an hour. That's the whole idea.

**Buying it.** Extra virgin is the least processed grade. Buy a size you'll finish in a couple of months, store it away from the stove's heat, and don't save it for "special". It's meant to be used.

**By style:**

- **Pescatarian Keto:** olive oil often becomes your main fat source. Skip the grain and beans, double the vegetables.
- **Pescatarian Low Carb:** keep the oil, shrink the grain portion.
- **Pescatarian Carnivore:** most of your fat comes from oily fish and eggs instead. Olive oil is optional.

Please talk to your doctor before changing your diet, especially if you take a blood thinner, blood pressure medicine or diabetes medicine.
