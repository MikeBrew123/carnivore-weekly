## 2. Fish card notes

<!-- One line per fish the plans use. Written to fit every style, so no beans, grains or greens in the serving ideas. **Salmon:** A rich, fatty fish that's easy to bake or pan-sear. Fresh, frozen and canned all count.

**Sardines:** Canned sardines are cheap, keep for months and need no cooking. Eat them bones and all.

**Shrimp:** Cooks in a few minutes. Buy frozen and thaw only what you need.

**Cod:** A mild, flaky white fish that takes well to butter or olive oil and lemon.

**Trout:** Mild and quick to cook. Bake it whole or pan-fry the fillets skin side down.

**Canned light tuna:** A low-mercury pantry staple. Check the label says "light" or "skipjack," not albacore.

**Atlantic mackerel:** A rich, fatty fish with a strong flavor. Make sure it says Atlantic, not king.

**Scallops:** Pat them dry, then sear in a hot pan for a minute or two per side.

**Halibut:** A firm, mild white fish on the FDA's Good Choices list, so once a week is plenty.

# Eating out and travel

Almost every restaurant serves fish, seafood or eggs. A few simple requests turn them into a meal that fits your plan.

## At a restaurant

- Look for fish that's grilled, baked, broiled, steamed or pan-seared.
- Ask for a second portion of fish, shrimp or eggs in place of the fries, rice or salad.
- Ask for butter on the side instead of a sauce.
- Check the low-mercury guide before you order swordfish, shark or a tuna steak.
- Box half your meal when the portion is large.

## Easy menu picks

- **Seafood restaurant:** grilled or broiled fish, steamed clams or oysters, shrimp with melted butter.
- **Italian:** grilled fish or shrimp cooked in butter, with no pasta or bread.
- **Greek or Middle Eastern:** grilled whole fish or shrimp, with a side of feta in place of the salad.
- **Japanese:** grilled salmon or cooked shrimp dishes, sashimi only if you eat raw fish. Ask which tuna is used, since bigeye is on the avoid list.
- **Mexican:** grilled fish or shrimp on a plate with no tortillas, rice or beans. Ask for cheese on top.
- **Steakhouse:** the fish of the day or shrimp, cooked in butter, with a second portion in place of the sides.
- **Diner or breakfast spot:** eggs any style, an omelet with cheese, a side of smoked salmon if they have it.

## When you travel

- Pack pouches of salmon or tuna, which need no fridge until opened.
- Hard-boiled eggs and cheese travel well in a small cooler bag.
- At hotel breakfasts, start with the eggs.
- Grocery stores sell cooked shrimp, smoked salmon and hard-boiled eggs.
- Carry a refillable water bottle.
- If a meal goes off plan, get back to it at the next one.

## What to say when you order

- "Could I have the fish grilled in butter, with extra shrimp instead of the sides?"
- "Is there anything on this besides the fish? I'd like it plain."
- "Could I get the sauce on the side, please?"
